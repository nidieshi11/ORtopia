Learning-based inventory ordering: Algorithm 1

Objective and physical constraints

The objective is to minimize, over feasible non-anticipative ordering policies π, the cumulative expected holding and lost-sales cost

Cπ(T,L) = ∑ t=1T𝔼[C tπ] = ∑ t=1T𝔼 [h ⋅ (I t + s(x1,t,Zt) − Dt)+ + b ⋅ (D t − It − s(x1,t,Zt))+] . 		(1)

The physical state and initial conditions are subject to

It+1 = (It + s(x1,t,Zt) − Dt)+, t = 1,…,T, xi,t+1 = xi+1,t, 1 ≤ i ≤ L − 1,t = 1,…,T, xL,t+1 = qt, t = 1,…,T, I1 = 0,xi,1 = 0, 1 ≤ i ≤ L.

A policy is specified by q1π,…,qTπ. It is feasible if and only if it is non-anticipative: for each t, qtπ can depend only on the system states

(Iτπ,x 1,τπ,…,x L,τπ),τ ≤ t,

and the realized supplies (s1,…,st). Here the realized period-t supply is s(x1,t,Zt). The period cost is

Ctπ = h ⋅ (I t + s(x1,t,Zt) − Dt)+ + b ⋅ (D t − It − s(x1,t,Zt))+.

Inputs, roles, and timing (Section 3, pp. 7–8). There is a single product and a horizon of T periods, indexed by t = 1,…,T. The lead time L is a positive integer. The demand variables are drawn independently from the same unknown distribution F, with

Dt ∈ [0,D¯].

The supply function has the printed signature

s(q,z) : ℝ2→ℝ.

The variables Z1,…,ZT are i.i.d. nonnegative random variables with unknown common distribution G; their stated interval is [α̲,α¯]. The lower endpoint α̲ is distinct from the unmarked parameter α in the third supply formulation below. The holding cost h and lost-sales penalty b are fixed inputs known at the beginning. The order quantities are decisions, not realizations of supply.

At the beginning of period t, the company observes It and the pipeline (x1,t,…,xL,t), where xi,t is the order placed in period t − L + i − 1, for i = 1,…,L. Next the order placed L periods earlier arrives, increasing available inventory to

It + s(x1,t,Zt).

The company then places qt, which arrives at the beginning of period t + L. Demand is realized afterward. Demand not satisfied by inventory is lost and unobservable. The observable sales quantity is only

min ⁡ {It + s(x1,t,Zt),Dt},

not Dt. When It+1 = 0, the lost-sales volume is unknown. Likewise, only s(x1,t,Zt), not necessarily Zt, is observed.

Long-run criterion and selected regret benchmark.

C∞π = limsup ⁡ T→∞1 T ⋅ Cπ(T,L). 		(2)
The constant-order policy πq orders the same quantity q every period, independently of the state. The quantity q∗ minimizes the long-run cost among constant-order policies. The selected regret criterion is

Regret ⁡ Tπ = Cπ(T,L) − T ⋅ C ∞πq∗ . 		(3)

Exogenously specified supply family and observation rule

Section 3.2 specifies that the supply function is exogenously given as one of the following four functions; these are alternatives for the fixed input s, not simultaneous equalities:

Formulation 1: s(q,Z) = q ⋅ Z, Formulation 2: s(q,Z) = min ⁡ {q,Z}, Formulation 3: s(q,Z) = qZ q + αZρ, ρ ≤ 1,α > 0, Formulation 4: s(q,Z) = qk q + Z, k > 0.

In Formulation 4, k is the supplier’s total output and Z captures other firms’ order quantities. This parameter k is not the grid index used below. The deterministic case s(q,Z) = q is identified as a special case, not imposed here.

Observation 1 (pp. 9–10). If the supply function takes one of Formulations 1–4, then, for any q and Z, observing s(q,Z) determines s(q′,Z) for every q′≤ q. The paper states that in Formulations 1, 3, and 4, Z can be inferred backward from q and s(q,Z). For Formulation 2 its complete case distinction is

q = s(q,Z) ⟹q ≤ Z⟹s(q′,Z) = q′, for every q′≤ q, q > s(q,Z) ⟹q > Z = s(q,Z)⟹s(q′,Z) = min ⁡ {q′,s(q,Z)}, for every q′≤ q.

The observation is used only to reconstruct lower-order supplies from observed supplies. No inverse formula or exceptional-value convention is added. In particular, the paper’s unrestricted claim about inferring Z in Formulation 1 includes q = 0, although s(0,Z) = 0 does not identify Z; the observation about lower-order supplies and the printed algorithm are retained without an added invertibility premise.

Constant-order benchmark and pseudo-cost

The benchmark construction in Section 4 uses

It+1πq = (Itπq + s(q,Zt) − Dt)+. 		(4)

The paper calls I∞πq the limiting inventory level and states convergence in probability of {Itπq}t=1∞ under

𝔼Z∼G[s(q,Z)] < 𝔼D∼F[D]. 		(5)

Assumption 1. The company knows an upper bound q¯ of q∗, satisfying the strict input restriction

q∗≤q¯,𝔼[s(q¯,Z)] < 𝔼[D].

The benchmark identities printed on p. 11 are

C∞πq = h ⋅ 𝔼 [(I∞πq + s(q,Z) − D)+ ] + b ⋅ 𝔼 [(D − s(q,Z) − I ∞πq )+ ], I∞πq = d(I∞πq + s(q,Z) − D)+, I∞πq + s(q,Z) − D = [I∞πq + s(q,Z) − D]+ − [D − s(q,Z) − I ∞πq ]+, 𝔼[I∞πq ] + 𝔼[s(q,Z)] − 𝔼[D] = 𝔼[I∞πq + s(q,Z) − D]+ − 𝔼[D − s(q,Z) − I ∞πq ]+ = 𝔼[I∞πq ] − 𝔼[D − s(q,Z) − I∞πq ]+.

Here = d means identical in distribution. In the last two lines, the placement of + outside the expectation brackets is retained from the printed page; the preceding derivation describes taking expectations of positive parts.

C∞πq = h ⋅ 𝔼[I∞πq ] + b ⋅ 𝔼[D] − b ⋅ 𝔼[s(q,Z)]. 		(6)

Since 𝔼[D] is independent of q, the paper replaces benchmark-cost minimization by minimization of

Ĉ∞πq = h ⋅ 𝔼[I∞πq ] − b ⋅ 𝔼[s(q,Z)] 		(7)

over the printed set

Q = {q : q ≤q¯}.

The printed set does not include a lower-bound row; it is not replaced here by [0,q¯]. The algorithm’s grid, in contrast, is explicitly constructed on [0,q¯]. Equation (4) is the paper’s constant-input benchmark recursion; the physical system still has the zero initial pipeline stated above. Applying (4) from the first physical period would disregard that pipeline delay, so it is not an additional recursion enforced on the adaptive system.

Printed scope of the algorithm. The formal algorithm contains no integer-rounding rule for K = T, epoch endpoints, or summation endpoints, and no explicit final-epoch truncation instruction at T. These are left as printed, rather than supplied as implementation conventions. The introductory epoch description on pp. 11–12 omits the factor κ2; the formal Algorithm 1 above is the selected operative version.

Simulation definitions and the printed sign conflict

Section 4.1 defines, for each a ∈An, the demand-driven simulated process

Iτn+La = I τn+L,It+1a = (I ta + s(a,Z t) − Dt)+,t = τ n + L,…,τn+1 − 2. 		(9)

Its implementable, censored-data replacement is the two-case update in Algorithm 1 above. Lemma 1 repeats that update for t = τn + L,…,τn+1 − 2; Algorithm 1 itself extends the update range through τn+1 − 1.

The following equation is printed in Section 4.1 for the actual inventory:

It+1 = (It − s(an∗,Zt) − Dt)+. 		(10, printed)

Source contradiction: the minus sign before supply in (10) conflicts with the physical dynamics on p. 7, with (4), and with Algorithm 1’s reconstruction formula. Equation (10) is recorded here as a printed discrepancy, not enforced alongside the physical dynamics. The operative physical and simulation rows above retain their printed plus-supply signs.

The accompanying argument on pp. 14–15 states that, if It+1 > 0, demand can be recovered and (9) evaluated; if It+1 ≤ 0, it uses

s(a,Zτ) ≤ s(an∗,Zτ)for all τ ≤ t,Ita ≤ I t ≤ Dt − s(an∗,Zt) ≤ Dt − s(a,Zt),

which implies It+1a = 0. This argument likewise uses the plus-supply physical recursion rather than the sign printed in (10).

Confidence rule and algorithm constants

The grid benchmark used to describe elimination is

a∗ = arg ⁡ min ⁡ a∈AĈ∞πa ,Ĉ∞πa∗ = min ⁡ a∈AĈ∞πa .

For each completed epoch, the confidence interval selected in Section 5, step 3, is

[C~na − (h + b) ⋅γn 2 ,C~na + (h + b) ⋅γn 2 ].

With N denoting the total number of epochs, its simultaneous confidence event is

E = { |C~na −Ĉ ∞πa | ≤ (h + b) ⋅γn 2 ,∀ ⁡a ∈An,∀ ⁡1 ≤ n ≤ N − 1}. 		(11)

Lemma 6 gives

ℙ(E) ≥ 1 −7(K + 1)N T2 .

These are confidence statements, not additional deterministic feasibility constraints on the realized sample path. Section 5.3 also prints intervals [C~na − γn,C~na + γn]; those alternate widths are not substituted for (11) or the threshold in (8).

Selected constant requirement (Section 4.2). The fixed constant is required to satisfy

κ2 ≥ δ(F,G,q¯),

where δ(F,G,q¯) depends solely on F,G,q¯, not on L,T, and is unknown initially. This abstract function is not assigned an invented formula. The optional κ2 = log ⁡ T version and the optional initial pure-learning phase are not imposed in this fixed-constant version.

The appendix obtains κ1 from its quoted Lundberg inequality: for the limiting inventory of

It+1 = (It + Q − D)+,

where Q,D are positive random variables in that lemma,

ℙ(I∞≥ u) ≤ exp ⁡ (−ρu)(u > 0),λ(z) = 𝔼[exp ⁡ (z(Q − D))],λ(ρ) = 1.

Here Q is that lemma’s random input, not the feasible set Q; ρ and the function λ(z) are local to the tail lemma, not the supply exponent or the confidence constant.

The confidence constant λ comes from the stationary-Markov-chain concentration result quoted as Lemma 8. For a stationary Markov chain (Xi)i≥1, initialized in its stationary distribution, it asserts existence of a constant λ > 0 with

ℙ ( |∑ i=1mX i − 𝔼 [∑ i=1mX i]| ≥m𝜖) ≤ 2exp ⁡ (−𝜖2(1 − λ) 4 ),𝜖 > 0.

This is the unambiguous event form used in the proof of Lemma 6 on p. 36. No additional upper bound on λ is supplied here: the paper’s Lemma 8 explicitly states only λ > 0, despite the later use of 1 − λ in the constant requirement.

Scope of the appendix dependencies. The constant bounds above are retained as printed; they are not recomputed from corrected concentration arguments. In particular, p. 36 introduces

m = τn+1 − τn − 1 − κ2 max ⁡ {log ⁡ T,2L}

but then prints

m = κ2 (max ⁡ {γn−2 log ⁡ T,3L}− max ⁡ {log ⁡ T,2L}).

The latter changes γn+1 in Algorithm 1’s epoch length to γn and drops the subtraction of one. It is a proof-level inconsistency, not an alternative operative epoch rule. The algorithm and empirical-cost denominator remain those printed in Algorithm 1. No bounds on supply by D¯, rounding rules, independence between demand and supply beyond the stated distributional assumptions, or missing parameter-domain restrictions are added to repair these arguments.
