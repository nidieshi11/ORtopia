\section*{P163: fixed depot--port joint truck routing and charging}
The model jointly schedules truck routing and charging. Source: paper.pdf, pp.6--11, Section2, assumptions1--8,
equations(1)--(30). Each truck has a given depot $d_v$ and port $h_v$.
Initial charge may be below full (p.7 assumption2). Every depot/port has finite
charging capacity (p.7 assumptions3--4). All original demand units must be delivered.
Handling duration is negligible as stated on p.7 and footnote5 on p.9.
Generated release/loading/unloading metadata are retained in the instance history,
as diagnostics.

\subsection*{Input and exact clock convention}
Let $G=(N,R)$ be the directed road network, $V$ trucks, $J$ full-truckload product
units. Unit $j$ belongs to product $g(j)$, with fixed origin/destination
$(o_j,e_j)$, due period $D_g$, tardiness price $\pi_g\ge0$.
Truck $v$ has availability $A_v$, battery $B_v$, initial energy $E_v^0$ and
horizon $H_v$. A road $r$ has positive integer duration $T_r$ and loaded/empty
energy $e_r^1,e_r^0$. These are the input special case of time-dependent
$T_{r,p}$ in the paper. At node $n$, charging supplies $q_n>0$ per occupied
period and at most $C_n$ trucks can charge. Its per-period price is $c_{n,p}$.
Source periods are indexed from 0. Thus a road starting at period $b$
occupies $[b,b+T_r)$ and its source arrival index is $a=b+T_r-1$.
Thus a subsequent departure at $a+1$ is immediate at the next boundary.
The objective retains the literal source difference ``latest arrival index
minus first departure index''.

\subsection*{Decision domain and routing}
Choose for each $v$ an even number $K_v\in\{0,2,\ldots,2\lfloor H_v/2\rfloor\}$
of contiguous one-way trips. The bound follows from positive integer duration
and the finite horizon.
For $k=0,\ldots,K_v-1$ choose a simple directed path
$P_{vk}=(n_0,\ldots,n_{L_{vk}})$ satisfying
\[
 (n_0,n_{L_{vk}})=
 \begin{cases}(d_v,h_v)&k\ {\rm even},\\(h_v,d_v)&k\ {\rm odd},\end{cases}
 \qquad (n_i,n_{i+1})\in R,\quad n_i\ne n_j\ (i\ne j).
\]
All such paths are admissible.
Choose $y_{vkj}\in\{0,1\}$ and $\ell_{vk}\in\{0,1\}$:
\[
 \sum_j y_{vkj}=\ell_{vk}\le1,\qquad
 y_{vkj}=0\quad\text{if }(o_j,e_j)\ne(n_0,n_{L_{vk}}),\qquad
 \sum_{v,k}y_{vkj}=1\quad(j\in J).
\]
A loaded/empty state is constant within each trip. Every used truck starts at
its own depot, alternates endpoints, and finishes at the same depot.
Unused trucks have no events and zero cost.

\subsection*{Timing, charging and exact energy continuity}
For every road on each chosen path choose integer departure $b_{vki}$ and arrival
$a_{vk,i+1}=b_{vki}+T_{(n_i,n_{i+1})}-1$.
The first departure is at least $A_v$. Every later road departure is at least
the preceding arrival plus1, including between trips. Final arrival is at most
$H_v-1$. Waiting is permitted only at the current node.
Choose at most one contiguous integer charging interval
$[s_{vkn},f_{vkn})$ at each visited node, possibly empty.
Charging must follow an actual arrival and end before the next departure:
\[
 a_{vkn}+1\le s_{vkn}\le f_{vkn}\le b^{next}_{vkn},\qquad
 c_{vknp}=\mathbf1\{s_{vkn}\le p<f_{vkn}\}.
\]
At a trip endpoint the interval belongs to the arriving trip and may precede
the departure of the next trip. Initial energy is supplied for the first trip. Charging is absent at nodes with $C_n=0$.
For each period and node,
\[
 \sum_{v,k}c_{vknp}\le C_n .
\]
Energy before the first road is $E_v^0$. Along a road $r$,
\[
 E^{arr}=E^{dep}-\ell_{vk}e_r^1-(1-\ell_{vk})e_r^0,\qquad
 E^{dep,next}=E^{arr}+q_n(f_{vkn}-s_{vkn}),\qquad
 0\le E^{arr},E^{dep}\le B_v .
\]
Waiting preserves energy. Energy carries exactly between trips. These equations give source SOC by division by $B_v$.
Because road discharge and charging are monotone during their intervals,
checking their endpoints also bounds SOC at every interior time.

\subsection*{Delivery, tardiness, objective}
For product $g$, let $u_g$ be the latest unloading start, the arrival period
of its latest delivered unit:
\[
 u_g=\max\{a_{vk,L_{vk}}:y_{vkj}=1,\ g(j)=g\},\qquad
 \tau_g=\max(0,u_g-D_g).
\]
All product quantities are positive and hence this maximum is defined.
With $a_v^{last}$ the final depot arrival period and $b_v^{first}$ the first
departure period, minimize the source equation(29):
\[
 \min\quad
 C^{lbr}\sum_{v:K_v>0}(a_v^{last}-b_v^{first})
 +\sum_{v,k,n,p}c_{n,p}c_{vknp}
 +\sum_g\pi_g\tau_g .
\]

Electricity costs cover actual charging periods, and every demand is served.
The source's malformed extracted big-M duplicates and swapped inbound/outbound
names are replaced by the explicit equivalent path/event semantics above.

\subsection*{Algorithm and evidence boundary}
The accompanying code searches product assignments, orders and vehicle priority,
then constructs feasible simple routes and finite-capacity charging intervals.
Its route ranking and greedy interval selection are heuristic algorithm choices,
used to construct candidates within the model. Each exported candidate is independently
replayed against every group above. The result reports a feasible heuristic objective and records null for the full-model bound and gap. Time budget is3600 seconds.
Input repair retains node/vehicle/product counts, quantities, due dates, penalties,
battery capacities, initial energies and horizon. Illegal same-type OD endpoints
and absent fixed vehicle endpoint mappings are repaired and logged. Missing depot
chargers are finite. A common conservative road duration is restored because the
source's road duration does not depend on the loaded state.
