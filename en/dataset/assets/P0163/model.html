P163: fixed depot–port joint truck routing and charging

The model jointly schedules truck routing and charging. Source: paper.pdf, pp.6–11, Section2, assumptions1–8, equations(1)–(30). Each truck has a given depot dv and port hv. Initial charge may be below full (p.7 assumption2). Every depot/port has finite charging capacity (p.7 assumptions3–4). All original demand units must be delivered. Handling duration is negligible as stated on p.7 and footnote5 on p.9. Generated release/loading/unloading metadata are retained in the instance history, as diagnostics.

Input and exact clock convention

Let G = (N,R) be the directed road network, V trucks, J full-truckload product units. Unit j belongs to product g(j), with fixed origin/destination (oj,ej), due period Dg, tardiness price πg ≥ 0. Truck v has availability Av, battery Bv, initial energy Ev0 and horizon Hv. A road r has positive integer duration Tr and loaded/empty energy er1,er0. These are the input special case of time-dependent Tr,p in the paper. At node n, charging supplies qn > 0 per occupied period and at most Cn trucks can charge. Its per-period price is cn,p. Source periods are indexed from 0. Thus a road starting at period b occupies [b,b + Tr) and its source arrival index is a = b + Tr − 1. Thus a subsequent departure at a + 1 is immediate at the next boundary. The objective retains the literal source difference “latest arrival index minus first departure index”.

All such paths are admissible. Choose yvkj ∈{0,1} and ℓvk ∈{0,1}:

A loaded/empty state is constant within each trip. Every used truck starts at its own depot, alternates endpoints, and finishes at the same depot. Unused trucks have no events and zero cost.

Timing, charging and exact energy continuity

For every road on each chosen path choose integer departure bvki and arrival avk,i+1 = bvki + T(ni,ni+1) − 1. The first departure is at least Av. Every later road departure is at least the preceding arrival plus1, including between trips. Final arrival is at most Hv − 1. Waiting is permitted only at the current node. Choose at most one contiguous integer charging interval [svkn,fvkn) at each visited node, possibly empty. Charging must follow an actual arrival and end before the next departure:

avkn + 1 ≤ svkn ≤ fvkn ≤ bvknnext,c vknp = 1{svkn ≤ p < fvkn}.

At a trip endpoint the interval belongs to the arriving trip and may precede the departure of the next trip. Initial energy is supplied for the first trip. Charging is absent at nodes with Cn = 0. For each period and node,

∑ v,kcvknp ≤ Cn.

Energy before the first road is Ev0. Along a road r,

Earr = Edep−ℓ vker1−(1−ℓ vk)er0,Edep,next = Earr+q n(fvkn−svkn),0 ≤ Earr,Edep ≤ B v.

Waiting preserves energy. Energy carries exactly between trips. These equations give source SOC by division by Bv. Because road discharge and charging are monotone during their intervals, checking their endpoints also bounds SOC at every interior time.

Algorithm and evidence boundary

The accompanying code searches product assignments, orders and vehicle priority, then constructs feasible simple routes and finite-capacity charging intervals. Its route ranking and greedy interval selection are heuristic algorithm choices, used to construct candidates within the model. Each exported candidate is independently replayed against every group above. The result reports a feasible heuristic objective and records null for the full-model bound and gap. Time budget is3600 seconds. Input repair retains node/vehicle/product counts, quantities, due dates, penalties, battery capacities, initial energies and horizon. Illegal same-type OD endpoints and absent fixed vehicle endpoint mappings are repaired and logged. Missing depot chargers are finite. A common conservative road duration is restored because the source’s road duration does not depend on the loaded state.
