\section*{Integrated sample-average truck--drone routing and waiting-time model}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min\quad &c\sum_{k\in K}\sum_{(i,j)\in A}r_{ij}(x_{ij}^k+y_{ij}^k)
+\hat c\left(\sum_{k\in K}\sum_{(i,j)\in A}\hat y_{ij}^k\frac{\hat r_{ij}}{\hat v}
+\sum_{k\in K}\sum_{i\in N_d}\hat s_i z_i^k\right)
+c^0\sum_{k\in K}u^k\\
&+\frac{1}{|\Xi|}\sum_{\xi\in\Xi}\left(\sum_{i\in N_c}(c^e\delta_i^{-,\xi}+c^l\delta_i^{+,\xi})+\sum_{k\in K}c^k\delta^{k,\xi}\right).
\end{aligned}\tag{21}
\end{equation}
\noindent Subject to (2)--(8), (10)--(12), and (14)--(20), $\forall\xi\in\Xi$, expanded below, and the inherited variable domains. The first-stage variables are common to all scenarios. As stated immediately before (21), (9a)--(9b) are redundant and are not imposed here.

\subsection*{Network, fixed inputs, and sampling}
The directed graph and node sets in Section 3.2 and Table 2 are
\[
\begin{gathered}
G(N,A),\qquad N=\{0,1,\ldots,n+1\},\qquad N_c=\{1,\ldots,n\},\\
N_d\subseteq N_c,\qquad
N_s=N\setminus\{n+1\}=\{0\}\cup N_c=\{0,1,\ldots,n\},\\
N_e=N\setminus\{0\}=N_c\cup\{n+1\}=\{1,\ldots,n+1\},\\
A=\{(i,j)\mid i\in N_s,\ j\in N_e,\ i\ne j\}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Nodes $0$ and $n+1$ are the starting and ending copies of the single depot. $N_d$ is the set of drone-eligible customers, and $N_c\setminus N_d$ is the set of truck-only customers. Table 2 defines $K$ as the set of available homogeneous truck--drone pairs, indexed by $k\in K$; the introductory prose also uses $K$ for the fleet size. In the formulas it is the index set.

The fixed distance $r_{ij}$ is the Manhattan truck distance on arc $(i,j)$, and $\hat r_{ij}$ is the Euclidean drone distance, both in km. Truck speed is stochastic:
\[
v_{ij}\sim F_{ij},\qquad (i,j)\in A,
\]
with all $v_{ij}$ independent. Each distribution $F_{ij}$ is known or can be estimated at planning time and may be continuous or discrete. The drone speed $\hat v$ is constant. The tables give both truck and drone speeds in km/h.

Demand $d_i$ is known for customer $i\in N_c$ (kg). The truck and drone capacities are $W$ and $\hat W$ (kg). Drone battery capacity $B$ is measured in minutes. Truck service time $s_i$ is defined for $i\in N_c$, and drone service time $\hat s_i$ for $i\in N_d$, both in minutes. The required launch and retrieval times are $s^l$ and $s^r$ (minutes). $M$ is a sufficiently large number, left abstract by the model.

Each node has a soft time window $[e_i,l_i]$ (minutes); the depot window is $[0,l_{max}]$. Here $e_i$ is a fixed window endpoint, distinct from the decision variable $e_i^k$ for cumulative drone energy. The fixed deployment cost is $c^0$ per pair, truck operation costs $c$ per unit distance (\$/km), and drone operation costs $\hat c$ per unit time (\$/min). The earliness and lateness penalties are $c^e$ and $c^l$ (\$/min). The late-return penalty for pair $k$ is $c^k$ (\$/min), with the mandatory assumption in Table 3
\[
c^k>c^l>c^e,\qquad k\in K.
\]
No numerical values from the computational experiments are fixed for (21).

Let
\[
\boldsymbol\theta=\{x_{ij}^k,y_{ij}^k,\hat y_{ij}^k,\ \forall k\in K,\ (i,j)\in A\},
\]
and let $\mathbf v$ be the vector of stochastic truck speeds on all arcs. A sample of $|\Xi|$ realisations $\mathbf v^\xi$ is generated, with $v_{ij}^\xi$ the fixed sampled truck speed on arc $(i,j)$ in scenario $\xi$. Section 3.3 uses
\[
\frac{1}{|\Xi|}\sum_{\xi\in\Xi}Q(\boldsymbol\theta,\mathbf v^\xi)
\quad\text{to approximate}\quad E[Q(\boldsymbol\theta,\mathbf v)].
\]
The linked recourse-value definition is
\begin{equation}
Q(\boldsymbol\theta,\mathbf v^\xi)
=\min\left\{\sum_{i\in N_c}(c^e\delta_i^{-,\xi}+c^l\delta_i^{+,\xi})+\sum_{k\in K}c^k\delta^{k,\xi}\right\},\tag{13}
\end{equation}
where, for this definition only, the routing vector and speed realisation are fixed and the minimisation is subject to the already displayed (14)--(20) and Table 3 domains. In (21) these recourse rows are integrated scenario by scenario; (13) is a definition of the sampled value, not an additional optimisation objective.

\subsection*{Variable roles and inherited domains}
The first-stage routing and auxiliary variables are those of Table 2:
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_{ij}^k,y_{ij}^k,\hat y_{ij}^k&\in\{0,1\},&& (i,j)\in A,\ k\in K,\\
z_i^k&\in\{0,1\},&& i\in N_c,\ k\in K,\\
u^k&\in\{0,1\},&& k\in K,\\
e_i^k&\in[0,B],\\
w_i^k&\in[0,\hat W].
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $x_{ij}^k=1$ denotes truck $k$ traversing $(i,j)$ with its drone on board; $y_{ij}^k=1$ denotes the truck traversing independently; and $\hat y_{ij}^k=1$ denotes the drone traversing independently. Each is zero otherwise. The binary $z_i^k$ indicates that customer $i$ is served by drone $k$, and $u^k$ indicates deployment of pair $k$. The variable $e_i^k$ is the cumulative energy consumed by drone $k$ upon leaving node $i$ in a flight; $w_i^k$ is its payload after visiting node $i$. Table 2 gives these interval domains without separate node-index ranges; the rows above retain that presentation. In particular, the upper bound on $w_i^k$ supplies the drone capacity restriction.

For completeness, Table 2 also declares the auxiliary relative visit position
\[
\lambda_i^k\in\mathbb Z_+,\qquad 1\leq\lambda_i^k\leq n,
\qquad i\in N_c,\ k\in K.
\]
It refers to the relative position of customer $i$ in the visit sequence of truck/drone $k$. It has no operative occurrence in (21) after the explicit exclusion of (9a)--(9b); no ordering rows are reinstated.

For every scenario $\xi\in\Xi$, the Table 3 domains are
\[
\begin{gathered}
\omega_i^{k,\xi}\geq0,\qquad
\delta_i^{-,\xi}\geq0,\qquad\delta_i^{+,\xi}\geq0,\qquad\delta^{k,\xi}\geq0,\\
\tau_i^{k,\xi}\geq0,\qquad\hat\tau_i^{k,\xi}\geq0,\qquad
\varphi_i^\xi\geq0,\qquad\bar\tau_i^{k,\xi}\geq0,\\
\alpha_i^\xi\in\{0,1\},\qquad\beta_i^\xi\in\{0,1\},\qquad
\gamma_i^{k,\xi}\in\{0,1\}.
\end{gathered}
\]
These are the table's node- and pair-indexed domains, applied to the variables in the displayed rows; Table 3 does not separately enumerate node-index sets for these declarations. The recourse decision $\omega_i^{k,\xi}$ is the truck waiting time at node $i$. The auxiliary variables $\delta_i^{-,\xi}$ and $\delta_i^{+,\xi}$ measure service-start earliness and lateness, and $\delta^{k,\xi}$ measures the lateness of pair $k$ returning to the depot. Truck and drone arrival times are $\tau_i^{k,\xi}$ and $\hat\tau_i^{k,\xi}$; $\varphi_i^\xi$ is the service-start time. The ready-for-retrieval time is $\bar\tau_i^{k,\xi}$. The indicators $\alpha_i^\xi$ and $\beta_i^\xi$ are one if service occurs before the lower or after the upper window endpoint, respectively, and zero otherwise. Table 3 describes $\gamma_i^{k,\xi}$ as one if the truck arrives at retrieval node $i$ later than its drone, and zero otherwise.

\subsection*{Common operational assumptions and printed qualifications}
The problem description on physical pages 4--5 requires each customer to be served exactly once by either vehicle, with truck-only customers served by trucks. Each deployed homogeneous pair starts at and returns to the single depot. A drone may serve multiple customers in a flight, subject to payload and battery capacity, and may be launched and retrieved repeatedly along its truck's route, at different locations. At each customer location, a drone can be launched and retrieved at most once. When the paired vehicles reconvene, the one arriving first waits for the other. Customer nodes have drone aprons, so drone waiting energy is negligible. A retrieved drone receives a fully charged replacement battery. Vehicles start service immediately upon arrival at customers; trucks may wait after service before departing, including at the depot. Soft-window violations incur earliness or lateness penalties, and a return after $l_{max}$ incurs a late-return penalty rather than a hard return-time restriction.
