Fixed problem data and index scope

The source is Khorasanian, Patrick, and Sauré, Dynamic Home Care Routing and Scheduling with Uncertain Number of Visits per Referral, preprint dated March 12, 2024, Sections 2–4 and Electronic Companion EC.1. Equation numbers below are the source numbers.

There are K service types and L regions. Regions are equal-sized squares, with their centers used as patient locations. Location 0 is the depot. The described area is a circular grid with Manhattan distances; the authors also allow asymmetric areas. Each day’s route starts and ends at the depot. Known travel times are dll′, for l,l′∈{1,…,L}∪{0}. For service type k, hk is the constant number of days between consecutive visits, Tk is the maximum wait for an accepted referral’s first visit, ek is the deterministic service time per visit, and Ĵk is the random required number of visits, at most Jk. The distribution of Ĵk is input data. The service time includes a small fixed allowance for travel between actual patient locations and the region-center approximation.

The regular daily shift length is χ and the maximum allowable overtime is χ′. The geographical accessibility premise is

d0,l + max ⁡ k{ek} + dl,0 ≤ χ,∀ ⁡l.

Decisions are made once daily before work starts. A referral arriving during a day does not require a visit that same day. The rolling planning horizon is

T = max ⁡ {max ⁡ k{hk},max ⁡ k{Tk}}.

Day t becomes day t − 1 at the next epoch. Index ranges are

t = 1,…,T,k = 1,…,K,l = 1,…,L,j = 0,…,Jk − 1.

Unqualified sums and quantifiers over these indices have these ranges; in particular the range of j depends on k. In the routing constraints, l,l′ denote regions; depot travel is written explicitly using index 0.

New referrals have known, steady-state daily distributions for each service type and region. Y kl denotes the corresponding referral count, and 𝔼[Y kl] its expected value. These distributions remain abstract in the general model. The Poisson arrivals, numerical discount factor, service-type ranges, and cost-weight values of Section 7 are experimental settings, not additional restrictions of this full general formulation. The symbol γ is the daily discount factor of the discounted infinite-horizon model; Sections 2–4 do not print a numerical interval for it.

Only new referrals can be rejected. Once accepted, a patient must be served until discharge. Visits have no within-day appointment-time restriction. Only the immediate next visit is scheduled: whether another visit is needed becomes known at the preceding visit. A diverted visit is served by an on-call nurse, assumed available near every location; diversion does not discharge the patient.

States and structural zeros

The state is s = (x→,y→), where

x→ = {xtklj;t = 1,…,T,k = 1,…,K,l = 1,…,L,j = 0,…,Jk − 1},y→ = {ykl;∀ ⁡kl}.

Here xtklj counts patients of type k in region l with j visits completed before day t and visit j + 1 assigned to day t. Patients with Jk completed visits have left the system. The count ykl is the number of new referrals awaiting a decision. The finite upper bounds xtkljmax ⁡ and yklmax ⁡ are fixed input values, left abstract by the authors.

S = {(x→,y→)∣xtklj ≤ xtkljmax ⁡ ,∀ ⁡tklj,y kl ≤ yklmax ⁡ ,∀ ⁡kl, xtklj ∈ ℤ≥0,∀ ⁡tklj,ykl ∈ ℤ≥0,∀ ⁡kl}. 		(1)

The accompanying state-defining prose additionally specifies

xtklj = 0, ∀ ⁡tklj,t ≥ hk + 1,j ≥ 1, xTk,kl,0 = 0, ∀ ⁡kl.

First-visit state entries xtkl,0 can be nonzero only for t ≤ Tk − 1. The source writes the set of always-zero indices as

Θ = {∀ ⁡tklj∣(t ≥ hk + 1,j ≥ 1) ∪ (Tk ≤ t ≤ T,j = 0)}.

Thus xtklj = 0 for indices in Θ, as specified by that prose. These are state restrictions, not restrictions forcing the ALP coefficients τtklj to zero.

Actions and the inherited feasible action set

For a given state, the action is

a = (r→,n→,z→,o→,u),r→ = {rkl;∀ ⁡kl},n→ = {ntkl;∀ ⁡tkl},z→ = {zkl;∀ ⁡kl},o→ = {oll′;∀ ⁡ll′}.

The quantities rkl, ntkl, and zkl are, respectively, numbers of rejected new referrals, accepted new referrals assigned to day t, and diverted visits. First visits of accepted referrals are assigned within their respective wait-time targets. The binary variable oll′ takes value one if the nurse serves location l before l′. The quantity u is overtime. Diversion, routing, and overtime concern only day 1, and diversion concerns one visit of each patient at a time.

The auxiliary variable wl indicates a location to be visited, fl represents accumulated travel time from the depot to location l, q is the day’s total travel time, and g is the day’s tour length. The fixed coefficient M is described only as a big number.

(12)

The source defines As as the set of all feasible actions for state s satisfying (1)–(12), with the state and action meanings just stated. At the action-generation stage s is fixed; the action and auxiliary routing quantities are selected.

Printed-scope note. The action prose identifies referral and visit quantities as counts and the time quantities by their physical roles. Unlike the state domains in (1) and the binary domains (5), (8), the printed action block does not separately list integer/nonnegative domain rows for r,n,z or nonnegative time-domain rows. No extra domain rows or big-M bound are supplied here. Also, (7)–(8) print no relation linking oll′ to ol′l: the stated precedence interpretation is not enforced by an additional ordering equation in that block. The printed routing rows are retained without repair.

Stochastic transitions and their expectations

After j completed visits, the probability that a type-k patient needs at least one more visit is pk,j+1. Its printed definition is

pkj = ℙ(Ĵk ≥ j∣Ĵk ≥ j − 1),∀ ⁡j ≥ 1.

The symbol B(x,p) denotes a binomial random variable with x trials and success probability p. For the next state s′ = (x→′,y→′), the random vector corresponding to x→′ is X→′, defined by

Xtklj′(s,a) = { B(x1,kl,0 + n1,kl,pk,2),j = 1,t = hk, B(x1,kl,j−1,pk,j+1), j ≥ 2,t = hk, xt+1,klj, j≠0,t ≤ hk − 1, xt+1,kl,0 + nt+1,kl, j = 0,t < Tk, ∀ ⁡s ∈ S,∀ ⁡a ∈ As,∀ ⁡tklj. 		(13)

The accompanying explanation gives

xt+1,klj = 0,∀ ⁡t = hk,kl,j ≥ 1,

as the reason no such carried-over term appears in the first two branches. The binomial counts include patients whose current visit is diverted, since those visits are still served.

Let ξ→ = {ξklj;∀ ⁡klj} denote the numbers of patients visited on the previous day who need at least one more visit. The authors assume independence among the elements of ξ→, among the arrival counts across types and regions, and between ξ→ and y→′. Their transition-probability expression is

ℙ(s′∣s,a) = ℙ(ξ→)ℙ(y→′) = ∏ kljℙ(ξklj) ×∏ klℙ(ykl′).

The next day’s referral distribution is the fixed daily referral distribution. The expected transition used in (20c) and the greedy objective is

𝔼[Xtklj′(s,a)] = { (x1,kl,0 + n1,kl)pk,2,j = 1,t = hk, x1,kl,j−1pk,j+1, j ≥ 2,t = hk, xt+1,klj, j≠0,t ≤ hk − 1, xt+1,kl,0 + nt+1,kl, j = 0,t < Tk, ∀ ⁡s ∈ S,∀ ⁡a ∈ As,∀ ⁡tklj. 		(19)

No truncation or overflow rule reconciling these transitions with the abstract finite state bounds is specified in the defining passages; none is added here. The conditional probability definition is retained without an added convention for conditioning events of probability zero.

Immediate cost and fixed cost coefficients

c(s,a) = ∑ klRkrkl + ∑ klZkzkl + Uu + Qq(s,a),∀ ⁡s ∈ S,a ∈ As. 		(14)

Here Rk is the cost of rejecting a whole referral of type k, Zk is the cost of diverting one visit of that type, U is the unit overtime cost, and Q is the unit travel-time cost. The fixed coefficient definitions are

Rk = ζr𝔼[Ĵ k]ek,Zk = ζze k,U = ζu,Q = ζq.

The weights ζr,ζz,ζu,ζq are chosen according to stakeholder preferences. Rejection cost represents the expected lost profit of not serving all visits of a referral. Diversion cost accounts for on-call service and the violation of continuity of care, and does not depend on location. The notation q(s,a) refers to the travel-time quantity in (9)–(10), not a separate input coefficient.

State-relevance coefficients

The source introduces strictly positive state-relevance weights for its exact-LP argument:

α(s) > 0,∀ ⁡s ∈ S.

The retained coefficient definitions on page 14 are

𝔼α[Xtklj] = ∑ s∈Sα(s)xtklj,𝔼α[Y kl] = ∑ s∈Sα(s)ykl.

These are distinct from the arrival expectation 𝔼[Y kl] and the conditional transition expectation 𝔼[Xtklj′(s,a)].

The coefficient choice specified on page 16 and repeated in EC.1 is retained:

𝔼α[Xtklj] = { 𝜀,tklj∉Θ, ∀ ⁡tklj,𝔼α[Y kl] = 𝔼[Y kl],∀ ⁡kl.

Here 𝜀 is an unknown constant to be tuned, not a D-ALP decision variable. EC.1 tunes it through simulation-based policy comparisons and explicitly includes 𝜀 = 0. It does not replace the full D-ALP rows or prescribe a universal numerical value for 𝜀; its example value is not imposed here.

Printed relevance-weight qualifications. The source prints coefficient one on η in (18a), and hence right-hand side one in (20b), but does not explicitly print a normalization of α in the defining passage. Those coefficients are retained without adding such a normalization. Strict positivity of α in the exact-LP discussion cannot produce 𝔼α[Xtklj] = 0 for a coordinate positive in any state. Thus the explicitly allowed tuning endpoint 𝜀 = 0 need not have the strictly positive-weight interpretation of that preceding discussion. The printed moment coefficients, including that endpoint, are retained rather than repaired by a new restriction on 𝜀.
