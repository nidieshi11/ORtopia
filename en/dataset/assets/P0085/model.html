Core model: minimum-cost JOCR-R

Source: Sections 3 and 4.1, PDF pp.5–7 (printed pp.624–626), equations (1)–(12). The restricted-focus and unrestricted-focus policies are both introduced as main models in Section 4. The retained policy is JOCR-R, matching the discrete-location input and stated code interface.

Scope and data. L = {0,1,…,N} contains the depot 0 and customer locations. Focal points must be in L. A truck carries at most G identical drones; each drone makes at most one out-and-back delivery per cluster and returns to the waiting truck. The truck can carry all orders; it visits all selected focal points in one tour. K ≥ 2 is the maximum number of selected focal points, including the depot. For each location i, (Ai,Bi) is its coordinate and Fi ≥ 0 its payload-dependent one-way admissible assignment distance, in the convention of (8). Truck-only locations have Fi = 0. Distinct location coordinates ensure this forces them to be focal points. Define

DijE = (A i − Aj)2 + (Bi − Bj)2,DijR = |Ai − Aj| + |Bi − Bj|.

CD1,CD2,CT ≥ 0 are fixed drone cost, drone distance cost, and truck distance cost. A round-trip flight contributes 2DijE.

Variables. xij ∈{0,1} assigns location i to focal point j; xjj = 1 selects j. yij ∈{0,1} for i≠j selects truck arc i → j; yii = 0. g ∈{0,…,G} is the drone count. Nondepot order variables ui ∈{1,…,K − 1} represent an ordering; unselected locations can take any value in that range. Their integrality and bounded representatives do not remove any valid tour with at most K focal points.
