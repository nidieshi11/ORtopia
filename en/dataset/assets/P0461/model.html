Ghost-kitchen integrated scheduling and delivery policy optimization

min ⁡ π∈Π𝔼 [ ∑ k=0KDΔ(S k,π(Sk))|S0] . 		(5)

The policy is subject to feasible integrated preparation and delivery decisions and the sequential state evolution described below:

π(Sk) = xk ∈X(Sk), k = 0,…,K, Sk+1 = SM(S k,xk,Wk+1), k = 0,…,K − 1, S0 = (0,∅,∅,∅,∅,∅,∅).

Here Π is the set of policies, a policy being a mapping assigning each state Sk a decision π(Sk) = xk. The objective is the expected sum of delay, expressed in (5) as the expected sum of marginal costs. Equation (5) prints DΔ without the subscript k; the immediate-cost definition (3) below prints DkΔ.

Inputs and decision epochs

There is an order-capture phase [0,Tc] and an operation phase [0,T], with

T > Tc,

where T is sufficiently large to permit fulfillment of all placed orders. Orders are unknown until placed, and their realizations follow known probability distributions in time and space. These distributions remain abstract in the model.

The fixed resource and order notation is as follows.

  • I: the set of all possible orders.
  • F: the set of food types. Each kitchen is associated with one fixed ghost restaurant, called a food type, and prepares only orders of that food type. Each order contains only one food type and is prepared entirely in its corresponding kitchen.
  • C: the set of cooks; Cf is the subset working on food type f ∈ F. Kitchens have dedicated cooks.
  • V : the joint fleet of delivery vehicles.
  • δf, f ∈ F: the freshness time, described as the maximum time allowed between completion of preparation and delivery at the customer’s location.
  • τ ∈ ℝ: the promised delivery time after order placement. Exceeding this promise incurs delay cost; it is not a hard delivery deadline.
  • κ ∈ ℕ: vehicle capacity, measured as the maximum number of orders carried simultaneously.

  • Location 0 denotes the ghost kitchen. Travel times satisfy

    tijt ∈ ℝ ≥0,i,j ∈ I ∪{0}.

A decision point occurs whenever a new order is placed and at the end of the capture phase, at time Tc, when no more orders will arrive. The number of decision points K is a random variable. The objective uses the index range k = 0,…,K. The final state is SK at Tc; the process terminates after its decision is made.

State and schedule definitions

A state Sk ∈S is

Sk =(tk,Ik⏟ Orders,Ψk,tks⏟ Cooks,Θk,tkd,t kr⏟ Vehicles). 		(1)

The current time is tk. Except for the final state, the state contains a new order ik. The open-order set Ik consists of orders already placed but not yet departed from the ghost kitchen for delivery, including the new order ik. For each i ∈ Ik, its revealed order data are

(fi,tio,t ip,l i),fi ∈ F,tio ∈ [0,Tc],t ip ∈ ℝ ≥0,

where fi is the food type, tio the placement time, tip the known meal-preparation time, and li the customer location. These order attributes are not scheduling decisions.

Currently planned cook schedules. These schedules were decided at decision point k − 1 and pruned during the transition. They include the orders in Ik other than the new order ik. For each cook,

ψkc = (i1c,i 2c,…) ⊂ I k ∖{ik}, c ∈ C, Ψk = {ψkc∣c ∈ C}, tkis ∈ [t io,T], i ∈ I k ∖{ik}, tks = {t kis∣i ∈ I k ∖{ik}}.

Here ψkc is the planned preparation sequence of cook c, and tkis is the planned starting time of preparation of order i. The superscript s is a label.

Currently planned vehicle schedules. These schedules also result from the previous decision and pruning, and contain all orders in Ik except ik. Their definitions are

Θkv = (𝜃kv1,𝜃kv2,…), v ∈ V, 𝜃 = (i1𝜃,i 2𝜃,…) ⊂ I k ∖{ik}, 𝜃 ∈ Θkv, Θk = {Θkv∣v ∈ V }, tk𝜃d ∈ [t k,T], 𝜃 ∈ Θk, tkd = {t k𝜃d∣𝜃 ∈ Θ k}, tkvr ∈ [t k,T], v ∈ V.

The sequence Θkv lists vehicle v’s planned trips, each trip listing its orders in delivery order. The time tk𝜃d is the planned departure time of trip 𝜃; d is a label. The time tkvr is vehicle v’s next return time: it equals tk if the vehicle is idling, and otherwise is the return time from its last trip departing before tk. That already-departed trip itself is not part of Sk. The set of vehicle return times is denoted by tkr; r is a label.

Printed collection notation. The source defines Θk as the collection of vehicle trip sequences, but writes 𝜃 ∈ Θk in the departure-time definitions and in the open-order transition below. Those expressions are retained. In (2), the source explicitly uses ⋃ ⁡ Θ for the union of all sets of trips contained in Θ.

Integrated decisions and feasibility

A decision updates the current plan:

xk = (Ψkx,t ks,x,Θ kx,t kd,x) ∈X(S k),(Ψk,tks,Θ k,tkd)⟼(Ψ kx,t ks,x,Θ kx,t kd,x).

The decision variables are the updated cook preparation sequences and starting times, and the updated vehicle trip sequences and departure times. The update must integrate ik, except at the final state SK at Tc. It may also reassign and reschedule other orders to cooks or vehicles.

Section 3.3.3 gives the following main-paper descriptions of feasibility. They are retained as source descriptions; the complete formal A.1 representation follows below. Where the two source representations conflict, the main-paper wording is not stacked as an extra constraint on the selected appendix rows.

  1. Each order is scheduled for preparation and delivery by exactly one cook and one vehicle. Preparation takes place in the kitchen associated with that order’s food type.
  2. The freshness constraint is satisfied: for each order i, the time between the end of preparation and its scheduled delivery is less than δfi.
  3. Already started preparations remain unchanged.
  4. Each order’s preparation finishes before its delivery trip departs.
  5. Only one order is prepared by a cook at a time.
  6. Each trip of a vehicle departs only after the return from its previous trip.
  7. Vehicles satisfy the capacity constraint of at most κ orders carried simultaneously.
  8. Trips are elementary tours starting and ending at the ghost kitchen.

Freshness wording. Section 3.3.3, physical PDF page 7, states “less than δfi,” which is the strict wording retained here. Section 3.1, physical PDF page 4, instead says that ready-to-door time “does not exceed” an order-specific duration, and Section 3.3 describes δf as a maximum. These differ at equality; no boundary repair is imposed.

Postdecision state

The postdecision state is the state immediately after choosing the updated plan and before the next order arrives:

Skx = (t k,Ik,Ψkx,t ks,x,Θ kx,t kd,x,t kr) ∈Sx.

Delay and marginal cost

For a plan (Ψ,ts,Θ,td), the printed total planned delay is

D(Θ,td) = ∑ 𝜃∈⋃ Θ ∑ j=1|𝜃|max ⁡ (0,t 𝜃d + (∑ l=1j−1t il𝜃,il+1𝜃t) − t ij𝜃o − τ ). 		(2)

Here ⋃ ⁡ Θ is the union of all sets of trips contained in Θ, so that the outer sum ranges over all trips of all vehicles; |𝜃| is the number of orders in trip 𝜃. The source describes the summand as the maximum of zero and the difference between click-to-door time and the promised delivery time.

The immediate marginal cost is

DkΔ(S k,xk) = D(Θkx,t kd,x) − D(Θ k,tkd). 		(3)

It may be negative when the updated plan has less delay than the previous plan. The paper states that the sum of marginal costs over all realized states coincides with the sum of realized delays over all orders.

Printed travel-term omission in (2). The original page image confirms that the travel sum in (2) starts at l = 1. With i1𝜃 defined as the first order and t𝜃d defined as the ghost-kitchen departure time, (2) contains no ghost-kitchen-to-first-order travel term. The displayed formula is retained without inserting such a term.

Stochastic information and transition

The stochastic information Wk+1 ∈Ω consists of the newly realized order ik+1 and its attributes

fik+1,tik+1o,t ik+1p,l ik+1.

In the special case

Wk+1 = ∅,

no further orders are realized, and the process moves to the final state at Tc. This case is distinct from the ordinary new-order transition.

The transition mapping is

SM : S ×X ×Ω→S,S k+1 = SM(S k,xk,Wk+1).

For a newly realized order the printed updates are

tk+1 = tik+1o, Ik+1 = ⋃ 𝜃∈Θkx{i ∈ 𝜃∣tk𝜃d,x > t k+1}∪{ik+1}.

For every c ∈ C, the cook’s schedule is truncated to include only orders in Ik+1. For every v ∈ V , the new planned trip sequence is obtained by truncating trips 𝜃 ∈ Θkvx with

tk𝜃d,x < t k+1.

The vehicle return times are updated according to

tk+1,vr = max ⁡ (t k+1,max ⁡ 𝜃∈Θkvx∣tk𝜃d,x<tk+1 tk𝜃d,x + t i|𝜃|𝜃,0t + ∑ j=1|𝜃|−1t ij𝜃,ij+1𝜃t) ,v ∈ V. 		(4)

Printed transition qualifications. The open-order update retains only trips with departure strictly after tk+1, whereas the trip-sequence pruning removes trips with departure strictly before tk+1. Thus the printed rules do not treat departure exactly at tk+1 consistently. Both strict comparisons are retained. The original page image also confirms that (4) includes customer-to-customer travel and the final return to location 0, but no travel from location 0 to the first order. Its inner maximization uses only trips in Θkvx departing before tk+1; no empty-index-set convention or explicit carry-forward of an already-underway trip’s return time is printed there. No additional term, boundary rule, or convention has been supplied.
