The admissibility conditions and system relations defining this problem are the following (Section 2, physical PDF pages 4–5).

Admissible policies. The decision variable is the ordering policy π ∈Π. The family Π consists of all policies for which the order placed at time t is a function, possibly a random function, only of

T,t,It,xt,L,h,c,D.

Here It and xt are the inventory level and pipeline vector at the start of period t. The order is placed before seeing the demand of period t and cannot depend on realizations of future demand. Ordering decisions are made only in periods 1,…,T. The order placed in period t is appended to the pipeline as xL,t+1; the prescribed zero orders after the ordering horizon therefore give

xL,t+1 = 0,t = T + 1,…,T + L.

State, pipeline, inventory, lost demand, and cost. For a generic admissible policy, the source writes the system relations without policy superscripts. The pipeline vector is

xt = (x1,t,x2,t,…,xL,t).

It contains orders placed before period t but not received prior to that period. For each operating period t = 1,…,T + L, the pipeline shift, inventory recursion, lost-demand definition, and period-cost definition are

xi,t+1 = xi+1,t, i = 1,2,…,L − 1, It+1 = (It + x1,t − Dt)+, Nt ≜ (It + x1,t − Dt)−, Ct ≜ hIt+1 + cNt = h(It + x1,t − Dt)+ + c(I t + x1,t − Dt)−.

The front entry x1,t is received first, then the order is placed and the pipeline is shifted and appended, and only then is Dt realized. The source states that Dt is independent of It + x1,t. At the end of the period, It+1 is excess inventory and Nt is lost demand; at most one of these two quantities is positive. Unfulfilled demand is lost rather than backlogged. Holding and lost-sales charges are incurred at the end of the period, before period t + 1 begins.

The printed initial conditions are

I0 = 0,x0 = 0,

where 0 is the all-zero vector of the dimension determined by context, here L for the pipeline.

Fixed inputs and their restrictions. The fixed inputs are the per-unit holding cost h, the per-unit lost-demand penalty c, the planning horizon T, the constant lead time L, and the demand distribution D. Time is slotted; T and L count periods, with

c,h > 0,T > L > 0.

The demands Dt are independent and identically distributed according to the non-negative distribution D. With D denoting a random variable governed by D, as defined at the beginning of Section 3.1,

D ∼D,D ≥ 0almost surely,𝔼[D3] < ∞.

The distribution D is not deterministic. Inventory and pipeline entries represent amounts of goods, and orders represent amounts of additional inventory; Section 2 supplies no separate algebraic action-space domain or integrality restriction for these amounts.

Policy-indexed random variables and objective scope. For a policy π, the source denotes its associated random variables by

{Ntπ,C tπ,I tπ,x tπ;t = 1,…,T + L},

all constructed on the same probability space. The generic system relations above apply to these policy-indexed quantities. The expectation in (1) is over both random demand and any random decisions of π. Equivalently, the preceding prose states the expected cost as

𝔼 [∑ t=L+1T+LC t] .

Costs in the first L periods are not penalized. Costs through period T + L are penalized, including the periods [T,T + L] highlighted in the source. Orders in periods T + 1,…,T + L have no effect on this objective, which is the stated reason for setting them to zero.

Printed boundary-index issue. The source explicitly prints I0 = 0 and x0 = 0, but specifies ordering periods beginning at 1 and lists the policy-indexed random variables beginning at 1. Section 2 does not explicitly specify a time-zero order or an initialization at t = 1. The printed zero-indexed conditions are retained here without adding a time-zero decision, replacing them with conditions at 1, or otherwise repairing this boundary-index gap. The reference to Zipkin [44] supporting the horizon convention is not supplied as an original document and remains unverified; no additional constraints are imported from it.
