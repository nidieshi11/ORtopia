\subsection*{Definitions and model scope}
A delivery operator coordinates the given number of truck-and-drone teams. The customers $N_c$ include a given subset of drone-eligible customers $N_d$. A separate starting depot and ending depot are used; departure locations $N_o$ comprise the start and customers, while destination locations $N_e$ comprise customers and the end. The given directed links $A$ carry yes-or-no records for truck travel with its drone $x0k_{ij}$, truck travel without its drone $x1k_{ij}$ and drone flight $x2k_{ij}$ for each team. At a location, truck departures and arrivals count the first two travel types; drone departures and arrivals count truck travel with its drone and drone flights. Every team has exactly one departure from the start and one arrival at the end for both truck and drone. On every link except the direct start-to-end link, the sum of all three travel records across all teams is at most one. The available links are exactly every connection from a departure location to a distinct destination location. Self-links are absent; every stated sum excludes them, and every rule with an explicit connection applies only where that connection exists. Truck road distances use horizontal-plus-vertical travel, while drone distances use straight-line travel.

Each customer's truck arrivals and departures for a team both equal its yes-or-no truck service record $z1k_i$. Drone arrivals and departures balance at every customer. Across all teams, each customer receives exactly one service, from a truck or through its yes-or-no drone service record $z2k_i$; customers outside the eligible subset must receive exactly one truck service. At each eligible customer, incoming and outgoing drone flights together must total at least twice its drone service record. At every customer and team, each of the incoming-flight and outgoing-flight counts cannot exceed the sum of its truck and drone service records. For each departure location and customer destination, the destination's drone service record must be at least the flight record between them plus the origin's truck-departure count minus one. Across all teams, each customer has at most one incoming drone flight and at most one outgoing drone flight. Choosing the direct start-to-end journey with the drone forbids that team from truck service at every customer and drone service at every eligible customer.

The truck's nonnegative arrival and departure times $s1k_i,s'1k_{i}$ and drone's nonnegative arrival and departure times $s2k_i,s'2k_{i}$ have no additional common horizon bound beyond the stated timing requirements. For every link and team, truck arrival at its destination lies within a band centred on origin truck departure plus road distance $r_{ij}$ divided by truck speed $v_T$, with half-width the given allowance $M$ times one minus the with-drone travel record minus the without-drone travel record. Drone arrival lies within a band centred on origin drone departure plus flight time $t_k^{ij}$, with half-width the allowance times one minus its flight record. Flight time equals that link's flight energy $e_k^{ij}$ divided by drone power $P$, and is zero for an unselected flight. At each customer, truck departure must be at least truck arrival plus truck service duration $u_i$ times its truck service record, plus the battery replacement time $BR$ times incoming flights from eligible customers. At an eligible customer, drone departure lies within a band centred on drone arrival plus drone service duration $u'_i$, with half-width the allowance times one minus its drone service record.

For the following synchronization rules, an eligible incoming or outgoing flight count includes only flights from or to drone-eligible customers. At every customer, drone departure must be at least drone arrival plus replacement time, minus the allowance times three minus the without-drone truck-arrival count minus eligible incoming flights minus eligible outgoing flights. Drone arrival must be at least drone departure minus the allowance times three minus the with-drone truck-arrival count minus eligible outgoing flights minus eligible incoming flights. At every departure location, truck departure must be at least drone departure minus the allowance times two minus truck departures minus eligible outgoing flights. At every customer, drone departure must be at least truck arrival plus replacement time times eligible incoming flights, minus the allowance times two minus truck service record minus eligible outgoing flights. Truck departure must also be at least drone arrival plus replacement time, minus the allowance times two minus eligible incoming flights minus truck service record. In each allowance term the allowance multiplies the entire stated difference, and these finite bands are retained when a trip or service is not selected.

Flight energy is nonnegative and cannot exceed battery energy capacity $E$ times the flight record. Its physical estimate is the energy factor $\alpha$ times the sum of empty drone mass $w_0$ and drone payload $wk_i$ at departure, multiplied by flight distance $r'_{ij}$. Energy cannot exceed that estimate and cannot be smaller than the estimate minus the energy allowance $M_E$ times one minus the flight record. The energy allowance is the largest estimate over all supplied links when payload equals the drone payload capacity $W$. Thus selected flights use their physical estimate and unselected flights use zero energy. At each customer, nonnegative hover duration $tk_{h,i}$ is at most battery capacity divided by power, multiplied by a yes-or-no arrival comparison record $t_k^i$. Truck arrival is at least drone arrival minus the allowance times one minus that comparison record, and at most drone arrival plus the allowance times the record. Hover duration lies within a band centred on truck arrival minus drone arrival, with half-width the allowance times one minus that comparison record. The ending depot has zero hovering.

Nonnegative accumulated drone energy $e_k^i$ never exceeds battery capacity. At every departure location it is also at most battery capacity times two minus the truck-departure count minus eligible outgoing flights, with capacity multiplying the entire difference. For every departure location and eligible customer destination, destination accumulated energy must be at least origin accumulated energy plus flight energy plus power times destination drone service duration, minus the allowance times two minus the flight record minus destination drone service record. For each flight pairing from an eligible customer to any destination, origin accumulated energy plus flight energy plus power times destination hover duration times destination truck service record, minus the allowance times two minus the flight record minus the destination truck-arrival count, cannot exceed battery capacity. The allowance multiplies the whole difference in both cases. The destination range includes the ending depot. There both hover duration and the customer-service flag are defined as zero, so the entire hover-energy term is zero.

Drone payload and its delivery-only payload $wdk_i$ lie between zero and drone payload capacity at every location. At every departure location, drone payload lies within a band centred on delivery-only payload with half-width payload capacity times two minus truck departures minus eligible outgoing flights. For each eligible origin and every destination, delivery-only payload at the origin cannot exceed payload capacity times two minus the flight record minus the destination truck-arrival count. For every departure location and eligible destination, destination delivery-only payload lies within a band centred on origin delivery-only payload minus destination delivery demand $d_j$; destination total drone payload lies within a band centred on origin total payload minus destination delivery demand plus destination pickup demand $p_j$. Both bands have half-width the allowance times two minus the flight record minus the destination drone service record. In every stated band, its multiplier applies to the entire difference.

The nonnegative truck payload $vk_i$ cannot exceed truck capacity $Q$ anywhere. At the start, it must be at least the total delivery demand of all customers served by this truck plus all eligible customers served by its drone, minus initial drone payload times the number of flights from the start to eligible customers. Initial truck payload cannot exceed truck capacity times the number of truck departures from the start to customers; the direct ending-depot link is excluded from this count. For every departure location and customer destination, destination truck payload lies within a band centred on origin truck payload plus destination pickup demand minus destination delivery demand. Its half-width is the allowance times one plus eligible outgoing flights at the destination plus eligible incoming flights there, minus the with-drone and without-drone truck records on the current link. A second band has its centre reduced by destination drone payload and half-width the allowance times two plus eligible incoming flights at the destination minus eligible outgoing flights there minus the with-drone truck record.

Two further truck-load bands apply for every departure location, every customer destination and every additional eligible customer. The first is centred on origin truck payload plus destination pickup demand minus delivery demand plus the additional customer's drone payload. Its half-width is the allowance times two plus eligible outgoing flights at the destination minus the flight record from the additional customer to that destination minus the current link's without-drone truck record. The second centre additionally subtracts destination drone payload. Its half-width is the allowance times three minus the flight record from the additional customer to the destination minus eligible outgoing flights at the destination minus the current link's with-drone and without-drone truck records. All these bands apply together, and each half-width multiplies the complete stated difference; no band is silently dropped when a particular movement is not selected. In these two checks the additional eligible customer and the departure location must each differ from the destination, so both explicit connections exist.

The operator seeks the lowest total cost. Road distance on all selected truck links except the direct start-to-end link is charged at the truck distance rate $c_T$, counting both with-drone and without-drone travel. Add the flight energy rate $c_F$ times the sum, across teams, of all flight energies, power times drone service durations at eligible customers selected for drone service, and power times hovering at all customers. Finally add the team-use charge $c_0$ for every team that does not choose the with-drone direct start-to-end link. Load and energy records retain their stated finite bounds; time records remain nonnegative and follow the stated timing requirements. No further rule forces all records at unserved locations to zero. Each team's customer order mark $l_k^i$ lies between one and the customer count. Between distinct customers, the destination mark must be at least the origin mark plus one, reduced by the customer count plus one multiplied by one minus the with-drone and without-drone truck travel records on that connection. This prevents a truck cycle separated from the depot. Unvisited customers may keep marks within the same range. Identical teams are numbered so that used teams precede unused teams: the direct with-drone start-to-end record cannot decrease as the team number increases. This numbering changes neither an operating arrangement nor its cost.
