Full-Horizon Unit Commitment and Dispatch


1 Sets and data

Let T = {1,…,H}, where H ≥ 1 is the number of periods. Let G, I, B, L, W, P, and D be finite sets of thermal generators, interpolation vertices, buses, transmission lines, wind units, storage units, and loads, respectively. For each bus b ∈ B, the given incidence sets Gb ⊆ G, Wb ⊆ W, Pb ⊆ P, and Db ⊆ D identify equipment and loads located at that bus. Each line l ∈ L has start bus sbl ∈ B and end bus ebl ∈ B; let Lbin = {l ∈ L : ebl = b} and Lbout = {l ∈ L : sbl = b}. A reference bus ref ∈ B is given.

All parameters below are fixed scalar data at each indicated index:

  • Δt > 0: period duration; cgi and pgi, (g,i) ∈ G × I: generation-cost and generation-output values at interpolation vertices.
  • Cpls and Cpgs: the load-shedding and grid-shedding penalty coefficients. The subscript p labels these penalty coefficients.
  • UTg,DTg ∈ ℤ≥1: minimum up- and down-times; Pgmin ⁡ ,Pgmax ⁡ : generation limits; and Rgup,Rgdown,RgSU,RgSD: ramp-up, ramp-down, startup-ramp, and shutdown-ramp limits, for g ∈ G.
  • Iwt: available wind power, for (w,t) ∈ W × T; ddt: demand, for (d,t) ∈ D × T.
  • ηpchg and ηpdisc > 0: charging and discharging efficiencies; Cappchg,Cappdisc: charging and discharging power capacities; and Spmin ⁡ ,Spmax ⁡ : storage-state limits, for p ∈ P.
  • Bl: line susceptance; Fl: line-flow capacity, for l ∈ L.
  • xg0,pg0,sp0 and all startup and shutdown history values ugτ,vgτ with τ < 1 appearing in the constraints: known initial-condition data.

All quantities referenced at times below 1 are fixed initial conditions.

2 Decision variables

For every modeled period t ∈ T, the variables are:

  • xgt ∈{0,1}: commitment status, and ugt,vgt ∈ ℝ≥0: startup and shutdown variables, for g ∈ G.
  • wgit ∈ ℝ≥0: interpolation weights, for (g,i) ∈ G × I; pgt ∈ ℝ: thermal generation, for g ∈ G.
  • gwt,cwt ∈ ℝ≥0: wind generation and curtailment, for w ∈ W.
  • pcpt,pdpt,spt ∈ ℝ: charging power, discharging power, and stored energy, for p ∈ P.
  • flt ∈ ℝ: line flow, for l ∈ L; 𝜃bt ∈ ℝ: voltage angle, for b ∈ B.
  • lsbt,gsbt ∈ ℝ≥0, for b ∈ B: nonnegative load shedding and excess generation, as physically defined in source Table 3.

Continuous variables follow the listed domains. Wind generation, curtailment and shedding are nonnegative physical quantities. Source equation (5m), PDF page 7, uses gsbt in the nodal balance; the extracted independent psbt was a transcription error and is removed.

3 Optimization model

The finite-horizon objective combines the commitment equations and full-horizon operating costs below. Minimize

min ⁡ ∑ t∈T [∑ g∈G ∑ i∈Icgiwgit + ∑ b∈B (Cplsls bt + C pgsgs bt)] Δt 		(1)

subject to the following constraints.

Commitment

xgt − x gt−1 = u gt − v gt g ∈ G,t ∈ T, (2) ∑ k=0UTg−1u gt−k ≤ x gt g ∈ G,t ∈ T, (3) ∑ k=0DTg−1v gt−k ≤ 1 − x gt g ∈ G,t ∈ T. (4)

Thermal generation

pgt = ∑ i∈Iwgitp gi g ∈ G,t ∈ T, (5) ∑ i∈Iwgit = x gt g ∈ G,t ∈ T, (6) xgtP gmin ⁡ ≤ p gt ≤ x gtP gmax ⁡ g ∈ G,t ∈ T, (7) pgt − p gt−1 ≤ x gt−1R gup + (1 − x gt−1)R gSU g ∈ G,t ∈ T, (8) pgt−1 − p gt ≤ x gtR gdown + (1 − x gt)R gSD g ∈ G,t ∈ T. (9)
