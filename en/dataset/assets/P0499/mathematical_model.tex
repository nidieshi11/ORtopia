V(r_0)=\max_{p_t\in[0,U]}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^t\Pi(r_t,p_t).

The decision is the sequence of prices $p_t$ charged by the firm in periods $t=0,1,\ldots$. The reference price $r_t$ is the state generated by (1), starting from the fixed initial reference price $r_0$. The value $V(r_0)$ is the maximum discounted infinite-horizon profit.

\section*{Fixed inputs and admissibility assumptions}
The upper bound on feasible prices is $U$, the memory factor is $\alpha$, and the discount factor is $\gamma$. The source assumptions are
\[
\alpha\in[0,1),\qquad \gamma\in[0,1),\qquad 0^0=1,
\]
\[
b,a>0,\qquad \eta^+,\eta^-\geq 0,\qquad \eta^+>\eta^-.
\]
Here $b,a,\eta^+,\eta^-$ are fixed demand parameters. The strict inequality $\eta^+>\eta^-$ selects gain-seeking demand. The required restrictions on $U$ are
\[
D(0,U)\geq 0,\qquad\text{i.e.,}\qquad U\leq\frac{b}{a+\eta^-},
\]
\[
U\geq\frac{b}{2a}.
\]
These are restrictions on the fixed inputs, not additional price decisions. Marginal cost is assumed to be zero.

The case $\alpha=1$ is excluded: the source explains that reference prices would then remain constant over the entire planning horizon and a constant pricing strategy would be optimal irrespective of gain--loss asymmetry. The endpoint $\gamma=0$ is included, with the printed convention $0^0=1$.

\section*{Demand and one-period profit}
For price $p$ and reference price $r$ in $[0,U]$, demand is
\begin{equation}
D(r,p)=
\begin{cases}
b-ap+\eta^+(r-p),&r>p,\\
b-ap,&r=p,\\
b-ap+\eta^-(r-p),&r<p.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\end{equation}
The source also gives the concise expression
\[
D(r,p)=b-ap+\eta^+\max\{r-p,0\}+\eta^-\min\{r-p,0\}.
\]
The base demand, independent of reference prices, is
\[
D(p,p)=b-ap.
\]
The difference $r-p$ is the perceived surcharge/discount: $r<p$ is perceived as a loss, whereas $r>p$ is perceived as a gain. The corresponding demand change is $\eta^-(r-p)$ or $\eta^+(r-p)$, respectively.

With zero marginal cost, the one-period profit is
\[
\Pi(r,p)=pD(r,p).
\]
The source calls $\Pi(p,p)$ the base profit. Under the stated premises $\eta^+>\eta^-$ and $p\geq 0$, it also gives
\[
\Pi(r,p)=\max\{\Pi^+(r,p),\Pi^-(r,p)\},
\]
\[
\Pi^+(r,p)=p[b-ap+\eta^+(r-p)],\qquad
\Pi^-(r,p)=p[b-ap+\eta^-(r-p)].
\]

\section*{Linked Bellman equation and pricing strategies}
The paper explicitly identifies the following as the Bellman equation for problem (3):
\begin{equation}
V(r)=\max_{p\in[0,U]}\Pi(r,p)+\gamma V(\alpha r+(1-\alpha)p).
\tag{4}
\end{equation}
This equation applies for $r\in[0,U]$; the maximization is over the sum of current profit and discounted continuation value.

A pricing strategy is a function
\[
p(\cdot):[0,U]\longrightarrow[0,U].
\]
For a given reference price $r$, $p(r)$ specifies a feasible price in (4). Its reference-price path $\{r_t\}$ evolves according to
\[
r_{t+1}=\alpha r_t+(1-\alpha)p(r_t),\qquad t=0,1,\ldots.
\]
The optimal pricing strategy solving (4) is denoted by $p^*(r)$. No cyclic, constant-price, or myopic-policy restriction is imposed on problem (3).

\paragraph{Source scope and boundary wording.}
The operative model above is Section 2, physical PDF pages 8--10, of Hu, Chen, and Hu, \emph{Dynamic Pricing with Gain-Seeking Reference Price Effects}. The demand definition and parameter restrictions are available on the supplied original pages and are not unresolved. The later special-case restrictions $\alpha=0$ and $\eta^-=0$ are not assumptions of this selected formulation. On page 9 the paper states $U\geq b/(2a)$ so that the base profit is ``not monotone'' on $[0,U]$. At the permitted equality $U=b/(2a)$, the base profit $\Pi(p,p)=p(b-ap)$ is increasing up to its endpoint; thus that explanatory wording does not hold at equality. The printed weak inequality is retained without repair.
