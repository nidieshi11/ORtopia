\subsection*{Definitions and model scope}
A relief coordination center arranges commodity deliveries $A$ and casualty transport over given locations $C$ and usable roads $N$. Demand locations $CD$, supply locations $CS$ and hospitals or receiving locations $CH$ are explicitly supplied. Inputs give each road's distance or travel charge, commodity needs and casualties awaiting transport at demand points, hospital receiving capacities, total network supply of each commodity, commodity and casualty capacities of vehicle categories $V$, and available departures from supply or hospital locations. The center seeks the smallest sum of each vehicle category's road-use count multiplied by road distance or charge.

Commodity road flows $UZ_{aop}$, unmet commodity quantities $UDEV_{ao}$, untransported casualty quantities $LDEV_o$ and allocated supply $SUP_{ao}$ are nonnegative continuous quantities. Casualties traveling on roads $LZ_{op}$ and vehicle-category road-use counts $Y_{opv}$ are nonnegative integers. Commodities and casualties cannot use unlisted roads, and self-transport is zero. Vehicle counts exist only on declared roads, with other entries treated as zero. A road count may exceed one and is not replaced by a simple used-or-unused flag.

At each demand location and for each commodity, inflow less outflow plus unmet quantity exactly equals demand. For casualties, outflow less inflow plus untransported quantity exactly equals the number awaiting transport. At each supply location, commodity outflow less inflow cannot exceed allocated supply, and allocations across supply locations cannot exceed total network supply of that commodity. No separate source-specific stock cap is added. At every hospital, casualty inflow less outflow cannot exceed its supplied receiving capacity.

Unmet quantities of each commodity across all demand locations sum exactly to the greater of total demand less total network supply and zero. Untransported casualties across demand locations sum exactly to the greater of total casualty demand less total hospital receiving capacity and zero. These aggregate shortfalls are fixed by inputs rather than freely increased to save travel, though their allocation across demand locations is chosen with the transport arrangement.

On every usable road, total flow of all commodities cannot exceed the sum of each vehicle category's commodity capacity times its road-use count. Casualty flow cannot exceed the analogous sum using casualty capacities. These are two separate capacity checks, not a combined allowance for goods and people. At each demand location and for each vehicle category, departure count equals arrival count; this vehicle-count balance is imposed only at demand locations. Total departures of each vehicle category from the union of supply and hospital locations to all locations cannot exceed its available departure count. The result describes road-level vehicle and material movements, without an additional requirement of a single incoming and outgoing route at every location.
