MAR minimax inventory control: Problem (2.4)

inf ⁡ π∈Π sup ⁡ Q∈MAR𝔼Q [∑ t=1TC tπ] . 		(2.4)

The policy and distribution are subject to the definitions and restrictions below. The outer decision is the admissible inventory policy π; the inner choice is the joint demand probability measure Q. Here MAR = MART, with the horizon suppressed in the notation.

Fixed data and notation

The model has backlogging, a finite discrete-time horizon T ≥ 1, strictly positive rational support bound U and backorder cost b, fixed initial inventory x0, and fixed marginal mean demand μ:

U,b ∈ ℚ+,U > 0,b > 0,x 0,μ ∈ [0,U]ℚ,[0,U]ℚ ≜ [0,U] ∩ ℚ.

The per-unit holding cost is 1, as normalized in Subsection 2.1 by a scaling argument. The primitives x0,μ,U,b are rational; demand distributions have rational support and policies order rational quantities. The endpoints x0 = 0,U and μ = 0,U are included.

For a vector x, the source’s history notation is

x[s] = (x1,…,xs),x[t1,t2] = (xt1,…,xt2).

Period indices in [1,T] range over the discrete periods 1,…,T. The random variable Dt is demand in period t. The inventory level xt is measured after ordering at the start of period t, before Dt is known.

Admissible policies and inventory feasibility

The inherited admissible family Π^ consists of the T-dimensional vectors of functions

π = {xtπ,t ∈ [1,T]}

satisfying

x1π  is a constant, x 1π ≥ x 0π ≜ y 1π ≜ x 0, xtπ : [0,U] ℚt−1→ℚ is a deterministic map, t ∈ [2,T], xt+1π(d) ≥ y t+1π(d) ≜ x tπ(d [t−1]) − dt, t ∈ [1,T − 1],d = (d1,…,dt) ∈ [0,U]ℚt.

As required in Subsection 2.1, actions are measurable functions of the demand history. The inequality means that a nonnegative amount of inventory is ordered in each period. The notation x1π(d[0]), or x1π(q) for q ∈ [0,U]ℚ, denotes the same constant x1π.

The operative policy family in (2.4) is precisely

Π = {π ∈Π^ : xtπ(d [t−1]) ∈ [0,U] for all t ∈ [1,T] and d ∈ [0,U]ℚT} ,x 0 ∈ [0,U]ℚ.

Thus the bounded post-order inventory requirement belongs to Π, not to the definition of Π^; the history domains remain rational.

Period costs

The positive and negative parts are defined in the source by

z+ ≜ max ⁡ (z,0),z−≜ max ⁡ (−z,0),z ∈ ℝ.

The inherited cost relations are

Ct ≜ b[Dt − xt]+ + [xt − Dt]+, t ∈ [1,T], C(x,d) ≜ b(d − x)+ + (x − d)+, Ctπ(D [t]) = C(xtπ(D [t−1]),Dt), t ∈ [1,T].

The notation Ctπ in (2.4) suppresses its dependence on D[t].

Demand measures and martingale restrictions

A probability measure is rationally supported if it assigns probability 1 to rational vectors of its finite dimension. For such a measure Q, supp ⁡ (Q) denotes the possibly infinite set of rational vectors assigned strictly positive probability, rather than a topological closure. The random variable or vector DQ is distributed according to Q.

For μ ∈ [0,U]ℚ, the inherited one-period ambiguity set is

𝔐(μ) = {Q : Q is a rationally supported probability measure, supp ⁡ (Q) ⊆ [0,U]ℚ, }.

The set MART consists of all rationally supported probability measures of discrete-time martingale sequences (D1,…,DT) such that the marginal distribution of Dt belongs to 𝔐(μ) for every t ∈ [1,T]. Written explicitly, its support, marginal-mean, and martingale restrictions are

supp ⁡ (Q) ⊆ [0,U]ℚT, 𝔼Q[Dt] = μ, t ∈ [1,T], 𝔼Q[Dt∣D[t−1]] = Dt−1, t ∈ [2,T].

The conditional expectation equality is the martingale property under Q. In the source’s conditional-distribution statement, this also gives

the conditional distribution of Dt given D[t−1] belongs to 𝔐(Dt−1),t ∈ [2,T].

No independence or product-measure restriction is imposed.

Basis for the selected bounded-policy formulation

Lemma 1 (physical PDF page 7), whose proof appears in Technical Appendix 6.1 (pages 33–34), supplies the reduction used immediately before (2.4):

∀ ⁡π^ ∈Π^∃ ⁡π ∈Π∀ ⁡d = (d1,…,dT) ∈ [0,U]ℚT∀ ⁡t ∈ [1,T],C tπ(d [t]) ≤ Ctπ^(d [t]).

Its premises here are the bounded nonnegative demand support and x0 ∈ [0,U]ℚ, with the inherited costs and rational-policy restrictions above. This statement justifies selecting Π in (2.4); it is not an additional decision constraint. The separate dynamic-programming formulations and the independent-demand model are not part of this selected formulation.
