Fixed data, indices, states, and admissible actions

There are m resources and n product or customer classes. Throughout,

i ∈{1,…,m},j ∈{1,…,n},t ∈{1,…,τ}.

The last period in which sales can occur is τ. Unqualified sums and quantifiers over i,j,t use these ranges.

The fixed resource vector and consumption matrix are

c→ ≡ (ci),A ≡ (ai,j),

where A is an m × n matrix. For every i, ci > 0 is the integer amount of resource i available at the beginning of period 1. For every i,j, ai,j is the integer amount of resource i required by a class j customer. The jth column of A is denoted by Aj. An accepted class j customer earns the fixed revenue

fj > 0∀ ⁡j.

The selected formulation uses fj, not the optional time-dependent fare variant mentioned in the paper.

At most one customer arrives in each period. A class j customer arrives in period t with probability pt,j; no customer arrives with probability

1 −∑ jpt,j.

These are fixed arrival probabilities, not decision variables. No strict-positivity assumption on all arrival probabilities is imposed in the formulation of Section 4.1.
