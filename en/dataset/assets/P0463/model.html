Fixed data and operating assumptions

The problem is defined on an undirected weighted graph

G = (V,E),V = D ∪ S ∪ C,

where D, S, and C are the sets of depots, satellites, and customers, respectively. The edge set is partitioned into the two echelon edge sets

E1 = {(i,j)∣i ∈ D ∪ S,j ∈ S,i≠j}, E2 = {(i,j)∣i ∈ S ∪ C,j ∈ C,i≠j}.

First-echelon (FE) vehicles traverse E1 and second-echelon (SE) vehicles traverse E2. For each e ∈ E, the fixed weight ce is the transportation cost of traversing edge e.

There is an unlimited homogeneous fleet for each echelon. The respective vehicle capacities satisfy

QFE > QSE.

At satellites, goods are unloaded from FE vehicles and directly loaded into SE vehicles, with no intermediate storage. Deliveries from different FE vehicles to the same SE vehicle are not allowed. Multiple FE vehicles may visit a satellite provided that they supply different SE vehicles.

For every i ∈ C, demand ξi is a non-negative integer random variable with mean μi and variance σi2:

ξi ∈{0,1,2,…},𝔼[ξi] = μi,Var ⁡ (ξi) = σi2,i ∈ C.

The joint demand distribution is fixed data. No independence assumption is imposed: demands of different customers may be statistically dependent. The fixed chance-constraint level η specifies the required probability that the demand on each SE route fits within its capacity. The general problem definition does not print an additional numerical restriction on η.

Routes and the feasible tour-tree set

First-echelon route (Definition 1). An FE route is a sequence

f = (n0,n1,…,nm,nm+1)

with

m ≥ 1, n0 = nm+1 ∈ D, ni ∈ S for all i ∈{1,…,m}, ni ≠nj for all i,j ∈{1,…,m},i≠j.

Second-echelon route (Definition 2). A valid SE route is a sequence

r = (n0,n1,…,nm,nm+1)

with

m ≥ 1, n0 = nm+1 ∈ S, ni ∈ C for i ∈{1,…,m}, ni ≠nj for all i,j ∈{1,…,m},i≠j,

and, with C′ = {n1,…,nm},

ℙ (∑ i∈C′ξi ≤ QSE) ≥ η. 		(1)

Constraint (1) applies to every SE route in a feasible tour-tree, not to the combined stochastic demand of the whole tour-tree.

Tour-tree (Definition 3 and inherited feasibility). A tour-tree comprises a single FE route and all SE routes supplied by that FE route. Its sequence representation is

t = (n0,(n1,R1),…,(nm,Rm),nm+1),

where

m ≥ 1,n0 = nm+1 ∈ D,nk ∈ Sfor k ∈{1,…,m},

and Rk is the set of SE routes starting at satellite nk. The underlying FE route obeys Definition 1, and each route in each Rk obeys Definition 2. Customers are visited at most once within an elementary tour-tree; the paper distinguishes these elementary tour-trees from the later algorithmic relaxation allowing revisits.

Every SE route leaves its satellite with full capacity. Consequently, an FE route supplies at most

Definition of the bound in (3)

Section 3 defines Q¯ by the following property:

∑ i∈C′μi > Q¯⟹ℙ (∑ i∈C′ξi ≤ QSE) < η,for every C′⊂ C.

The referenced definition is resolved in Section 5.3 (physical PDF pages 15–17). For a customer combination, the paper defines

μ(C′) = ∑ i∈C′μi,C′⊂ C.

Its upper feasibility bound μ¯ must satisfy

μ(C′) > μ¯⟹ℙ (∑ i∈C′ξi ≤ QSE) < η,for every C′⊂ C.

The paper sets the fixed parameter used in (3) to this bound:

Q¯ = μ¯.

To obtain μ¯, the printed procedure initializes

μ∗ = ∑ i∈Cμi

and decreases μ∗ by one until it finds a customer combination C′⊂ C such that

μ(C′) = μ∗,ℙ (∑ i∈C′ξi ≤ QSE) ≥ η;

it then sets μ¯ = μ∗. Here μ∗ is a preprocessing search value, not an MP decision variable. No scenario-selection optimization problem, sampling approximation, or variance-based feasibility test replaces (1) in this formulation.

Scope of the printed bound construction. The unit decrement is printed in Algorithm 2, although the general problem statement does not require integer means. No rounding convention or exceptional-case termination rule is added here. Section 5.3 also contains a prose statement that, above μ¯, the probability of exceeding capacity exceeds η. That statement is not the defining violation condition of (1); the operative chain above retains the explicit requirement that (1) is violated, namely that the probability of demand not exceeding capacity is strictly less than η.
