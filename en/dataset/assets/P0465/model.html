An admissible policy is a sequence π = {yt,pt : t = 1,…,T} for which (yt,pt) depends only on the historical information

(yr,pr,dr : r = 1,…,t − 1),

not on future information. At the beginning of period t, the retailer observes the inventory xt, chooses the price pt and order-up-to level yt, and receives any order instantaneously. Demand is then realized and satisfied to the maximum extent using on-hand inventory. Unsatisfied demand is backlogged; leftover inventory is carried forward. The end-of-period profit is

𝔯t = − k ×1{yt > xt}− c(yt − xt) + pt(D0(pt) + βt) − h(yt − D0(pt) − βt),t = 1,…,T. 		(1)

The objective is the expected average profit for sufficiently large T, not a separate single-epoch objective. The rewards need not be independent because they depend on the inventory states.

Fixed data, unknown demand, and input assumptions

The system model is

M = (D0,μ0,k,c,h),k > 0,c > 0,h : ℝ→ℝ+.

Here k is the fixed ordering cost, c is the variable cost per unit ordered, and h is the holding cost for positive remaining inventory and backlogging cost for negative remaining inventory. The quantities k,c,h are known. The demand function D0 and noise distribution μ0 are unknown and learned online. The distribution μ0 governs the demand noise, with

𝔼μ0[β] = 0.

The following are restrictions on the model inputs, not additional choices of the policy.

D0 : [0,1]→[d̲0,d¯0],D0(0) = d¯0,D0(1) = d̲0.

D0(p) + β ∈ [d̲,d¯]almost surely for every p ∈ [0,1],d̲ > 0.

A3. Holding and backlogging cost. The function h is convex and Lipschitz continuous with constant L′≥ 1:

Algorithm 1: complete online construction

The input is k,c,h,T and the regression-oracle-specific parameter γ, together with the known features, oracle, constants, and inventory ranges specified in the assumptions. The output is (yt,pt) for each t = 1,…,T. For epochs b = 1,2,3,…, the printed construction performs the following operations:

  1. Compute 𝜃^b using O and the observations from Hb−1.
  2. Construct D¯b by (11), using Λb and Δb defined above.
  3. Construct E<b by (13), with the stated value of κ, and construct μ^b by (12).
  4. Construct H¯b by (14).
  5. For every s ∈ [s̲,s¯] and S ∈ [S̲,S¯], compute (15) with the epoch substitutions and find r¯b(s,S) by (16).

  6. Select the inventory pair and its pricing function:

    (sb,Sb) = argmax ⁡ s∈[s̲,s¯] S∈[S̲,S¯] r¯b(s,S),

    with pb the optimal pricing decisions associated with

    ϕ(sb,Sb)(⋅;D¯ b,r¯b(sb,Sb),μ^b).

  7. Let tb be the first period in Bb. Implement

    ytb = Sb,ptb = pb(Sb), yt = xt,pt = pb(xt),t ∈Bb ∖{tb}.

    The epoch terminates once xt < sb. Restocking occurs only at the first period of each epoch. Within every selling period, the demand, state transition, and profit are those in the opening system block and (1).

The estimates and policy are renewed at the beginning of every epoch. No discretization, infrequent-update rule, inventory-disposal rule, or explore-then-exploit initialization is added to this version.

Printed initialization issue. Algorithm 1 begins with b = 1, but (12) requires a nonempty E<b. With no preceding epochs, (13) gives E<1 = ∅, so (12) has a zero denominator. Section 3.2 nevertheless states that the choice of κ yields |E<b|≥ b∕2, without an initial-epoch exception in the supplied main-paper passages. The printed Algorithm 1 gives no initial empirical distribution or separate first-epoch rule. Also, no initial value or bound for x1 is supplied in its system and algorithm block to ensure that the first assignment yt1 = S1 respects yt1 ≥ x1. These expressions are retained without adding an initialization or a repair.
