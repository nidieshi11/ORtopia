Arrival-rate construction from Appendix B. Appendix B, physical PDF pages 24–25 (printed pages 22–23), specifies how EV path flows determine station arrivals. It defines l as the maximum length for which ℙ(Dn ≤ l) is smaller than α. For a path with its installed charging stations, identify the preceding charging locations from which each subsequent charging location or the destination can be reached within this range. Travellers are divided in equal proportions between these feasible preceding recharge choices. Apply the same reasoning backwards along the path, adding all the contributions that require recharging at each station. The aggregate station arrival rate is the sum of its arrival rates from the individual EV paths. Mere passage through a station does not imply recharging there, so the construction is not the sum of all path flows incident on the station.

For precision, the following unnumbered bookkeeping restates the appendix’s backward equal-proportion construction; its auxiliary symbols are editorial notation, not additional planning decisions or newly attributed equation numbers. Fix one EV path and class, with path flow fp, and list in path order the origin, the installed charging-station occurrences, and the destination. For each non-origin occurrence j, let B(j) be its feasible predecessors: earlier charging occurrences, or the full-battery origin, from which the along-path distance to j is at most the appendix’s range l. Set the required arrival mass at the destination to rd = fp, and initialize every earlier mass to zero. Visit the destination and then the charging occurrences in reverse path order. At each occurrence j, distribute its accumulated mass by

ri ←ri + rj |B(j)|for each i ∈ B(j).

The origin terminates the backward accounting and is not a charging arrival merely because it is the origin. The path’s arrival contribution to a charging occurrence j is its accumulated rj. If a node has multiple charging occurrences, their contributions are summed. Applying this construction to each EV class with its own range and flow, then summing over paths, O-D pairs and EV classes, gives λk. This last summation is the appendix’s stated aggregation, not an assumption that every EV visiting node k charges there. A feasible charging chain is required for this construction; the paper does not define an arbitrary arrival rate when a required predecessor set is empty.

The printed example has node sequence 1,2,3,4,5,6, charging stations 2,3,4, consecutive link lengths 25,30,10,10,25 km, path flow fp = 100 vehicles/hour and l = 50 km. The destination 6 can be reached after charging at either 3 or 4, in equal proportions. Station 4 can be reached after charging at either 2 or 3, again in equal proportions; station 3 requires charging at 2. Accordingly,

λ4,p = 1 2(100) = 50,λ3,p = 1 2(100) + 1 2(50) = 75,λ2,p = 75 + 1 2(50) = 100.

These are the rates printed in Figure B.11. This example illustrates the general rule; its numerical flow, link lengths and range are not fixed inputs of every instance.
