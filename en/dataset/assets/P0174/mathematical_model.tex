\subsection*{Definitions and model scope}
A warehouse assigns known orders $I$ to robots $R$ and coordinates pickers $P$ at shelves $S$ holding stock-keeping units $U$. Every order has exactly one zero-or-one robot assignment $\alpha_{i,r}$, and each robot receives at most the given order limit $q^I$. Robot and picker shelf visit records $\beta_{r,s},\gamma_{p,s}$ are zero or one. A shelf receives at most one picker, receives a picker only if at least one robot visits, and every robot visit requires a picker there. The aim is the earliest possible latest robot arrival at the route end; picker finish times have no separate charge.

For each robot, shelf, and product, nonnegative picked quantity $\#_{r,s,u}$ is continuous, not restricted to whole units. Its zero-or-one product-use record $\mu_{r,s,u}$ cannot exceed that quantity, and the quantity cannot exceed the shelf's inventory $n^S_{s,u}$ times either the product-use record or the robot visit record. Thus a selected product contributes at least one unit. Each robot shelf visit requires at least one selected product. For every robot and product, all picked quantities equal the sum of the order requirements $n^O_{i,u}$ assigned to that robot. Across robots, picks from a shelf cannot exceed its inventory. Total picked quantity weighted by unit weights $w^U_u$ and by unit volumes $v^U_u$ must stay within the robot's respective weight and volume limits $w^R,v^R$.

Each robot and picker has a route with separate virtual start and end labels, and each shelf has a sequence of robot service with its own virtual start and end labels; these labels add no physical resources. Zero-or-one robot arcs $\delta_{r,s,s^{\prime}}$, picker arcs $\varepsilon_{p,s,s^{\prime}}$, and shelf service arcs $\sigma_{s,r,r^{\prime}}$ exclude self-loops. At an actual shelf, each robot's incoming and outgoing counts equal its visit record; the same holds for each picker. At an actual robot in a shelf's service sequence, incoming and outgoing counts equal that robot's visit to the shelf. Every route and every shelf sequence has exactly one arc leaving its virtual start and exactly one entering its virtual end; unused routes or sequences go directly from start to end.

Nonnegative whole-number robot positions $\tilde\delta_{r,s}$, picker positions $\tilde\varepsilon_{p,s}$, and shelf service positions $\tilde\sigma_{s,r}$ start at zero at their virtual starts. For each admissible arc, the next position is at least the current position plus one minus the relevant given allowance multiplied by one minus that arc's selection. Robot and picker routes use the route position allowance $\dot M$; shelf sequences use the service position allowance $\ddot M$. At actual shelves, robot or picker positions cannot exceed that route's number of visited shelves, even for unvisited shelves. At actual robots, service positions cannot exceed the number of robots visiting that shelf. Virtual ends can take the actual visit count plus one. These finite unselected-arc requirements remain in force.

Nonnegative robot arrivals $\epsilon_{r,s}$, picker arrivals $\theta_{p,s}$, and picking starts $\rho_{r,s}$ determine timing. For every actual shelf and virtual end, arrival is at least the given travel time from that resource's current location multiplied by its start-to-destination arc. At an actual shelf, robot arrival and picking start cannot exceed the given shelf time allowance times its robot visit record; picker arrival cannot exceed that allowance times its picker visit record. Consequently these records are zero for unvisited actual shelves. The processing time per selected product type $h$ is multiplied by the number of selected product types at that robot and shelf, not by picked quantity.

For every robot, actual shelf, and possible following shelf or end, the following robot arrival is at least picking start plus that processing time plus given robot travel time, minus the corresponding travel-time allowance times one minus the robot arc. For every robot and every picker, the same relation bounds the following picker arrival using picker travel time and the picker arc; this requirement is applied to all robots, including those not visiting that shelf. For every robot, shelf, and picker, picking start is at least the larger of robot and picker arrivals, minus the shelf time allowance multiplied by two minus the robot visit record minus the picker visit record. For every pair of robots at a shelf, the latter picking start is at least the former picking start plus the former processing time, minus the service-time allowance times one minus their service arc. All these are lower bounds, so waiting can occur; finite allowances on unselected arrangements are retained.

When resources are homogeneous and their initial states interchangeable, the sum of shelf labels visited by an earlier-numbered picker cannot exceed that for any later-numbered picker, and the sum of order labels assigned to an earlier-numbered robot cannot exceed that for any later-numbered robot. These ordering requirements are used only under that interchangeability condition. The warehouse chooses assignments, quantities, visits, routes, and timing together, seeking the lowest possible largest robot end-arrival time.
