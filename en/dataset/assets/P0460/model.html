DFRP infinite-horizon discounted Markov decision model

π∗ = arg ⁡ max ⁡ π𝔼 { ∑ t=0∞γtC t(St,Xπ(S t))|S0} .

The optimization is over deterministic stationary policies: a policy π maps every state St to a feasible routing decision,

Xπ(S t) ∈X(St)for every state St.

Here S0 is an initial state and

0 < γ < 1.

Stationarity is with respect to the complete state, including the day of the cycle; the policy may therefore be day-differentiated when viewed only as a function of the inventory component Rt.

Feasible routing decisions

X(St) = {xt : ∑ j∈L:((i,[t]),(j,[t+1]))∈Axijtdl = R itdl∀ ⁡i,d ∈L,l ∈T, ∑ l∈T ∑ d∈Lxijtdl ≤ Q ij[t] ∀ ⁡((i,[t]),(j,[t + 1])) ∈M, xijtdl ∈ ℤ ≥0 ∀ ⁡l ∈T,i,j,d ∈L, xt ∈Y }. 		(1)

The fixed set Y is the set of routing decisions complying with the imposed 2-alt load plan. The tactical plan is exogenous and fixed for the operating season: it supplies the weekly trailer movement patterns and two next-terminal options for each location–destination pair. These next-terminal options depend on current location and ultimate destination, rather than on the shipment’s origin. The source leaves Y abstract; it does not supply an algebraic expansion of this set in the MDP formulation.

Fixed inputs, network, and demand

Time is discretized into operational days. The start of each operational day is a decision epoch. The operating horizon is infinite, and trailer movement patterns and stochastic demand patterns repeat each cycle. The cycle length is T, with days

T = {0,1,2,…,T − 1}.

The wrap-around time-space network and its nodes are

G = (N,A),n = (i,τ),i ∈L,τ ∈T,

where L is the set of terminal locations. The arc set consists of movement arcs M and inventory arcs I:

A = M ∪I.

Movement arcs have the form

((i1,τ1),(i2,τ2)),i1≠i2,

and represent scheduled trailer dispatches from i1 on day τ1 to i2 on day τ2. All movement arcs m ∈M have travel time one day. Travel times that are multiples of a base time period are accommodated in this version by adding dummy nodes to L and breaking multi-period arcs into single-period arcs. Thus freight is at terminals, not in transit on trailers, at decision epochs. Arcs leaving day T − 1 wrap around to day

(T − 1 + 1) mod T = 0.

For each movement arc

m = ((i1,τ1),(i2,τ2)) ∈M,

Qi1i2τ1 is the capacity on its scheduled trailers supplied by the tactical plan. Inventory arcs have the printed form

((i1,τ1),(i2,τ1 + 1)),i1 = i2,

with the network’s wrap-around interpretation. They have duration one day and are uncapacitated: holding a pallet at a terminal is always a feasible option.

The flat network, without the time dimension, is

Gflat = (L,Aflat),

where Aflat describes how terminals are connected.

A commodity comprises pallets with common origin o, destination d, availability day e, and due day l, with o,d terminal locations and e,l ∈T. Its random quantity is

Do,d,e,l ∈ ℤ≥0,

with finite support and given distributional information. It is the number of pallets originating at o on day e and promised delivery at d by day l. The source assumes

Do,d,e,landDo′,d′,e′,l′are independent for e≠e′.

Demand quantities are realized in each cycle. Pickup from shippers and delivery to consignees are city operations and are exogenous to this model. Due days are not hard deadlines: late delivery is permitted subject to penalties, and freight in danger of being too late is outsourced.

The total possible sojourn time of each commodity in the system is no more than T. An instance of a commodity must be delivered, either by the carrier or by a third party, before a new instance can potentially appear. Pallets leave the system only through delivery to their ultimate destination or outsourcing.

State and decision definitions

For decision epochs

t ∈ ℤ≥0,[t] := t mod T,

the state is

St = ([t],Rt),Rt = (Ritdl) l∈T,i,d∈L,i≠d.

Ritdl counts pallets at terminal i at the beginning of epoch t that must be delivered to terminal d by day l. The inventory does not distinguish origins or availability times. The day [t] specifies the available trailer capacities and the position in the repeating demand pattern.

The routing decision vector is

xt = (xijtdl) l∈T,i,j,d∈L.

For ((i,[t]),(j,[t + 1])) ∈A, xijtdl counts pallets with destination d and due day l loaded on that arc: i≠j means movement, while i = j means holding at terminal i. Routing is the only decision; outsourcing is part of the transition, not an independent decision.

Printed indexing scope. The state vector is defined only for i≠d, whereas the first row of (1) is printed for all i,d ∈L, including i = d. The printed scope is retained; no value for Rdtdl is supplied here to repair that mismatch. Likewise, the decision vector and integrality row are printed over all i,j,d ∈L, while the movement interpretation and the flow sums refer to network arcs. No additional non-arc zero constraints are imposed.

Deterministic post-decision and stochastic transitions

After a feasible decision xt ∈X(St), the system first moves deterministically from Rt to the post-decision inventory R¯t = (R¯itdl), before new freight arrives:

R¯i,tdl = { ∑ j∈L:((j,[t]),(i,[t+1]))∈Axjitdl, ∀ ⁡d ∈L,i ∈L,l ∈T,i≠d, Δout(t + 1,i,d,p) = 0, 		(2)

The fixed parameter p controls lateness tolerance. The function Δout(t + 1,i,d,p) returns 1 if the pallet will be outsourced, meaning that its earliest arrival is p or more days after due day l, based on the shortest path in G from i to d starting at time t + 1; it returns 0 otherwise. In the wrap-around network, the source says that this function first determines whether due day l is in the past or future using the sojourn-time assumption, then determines whether outsourcing is inevitable. Outsourcing is assessed as soon as this becomes clear. For a pallet sent from i to j at epoch t, the outsourcing penalty is assessed at t and the pallet is removed before epoch t + 1. Equation (2) also removes pallets delivered to their ultimate destinations.

Printed outsourcing notation and boundary. Although its definition depends on due day l, the printed argument list of Δout(t + 1,i,d,p) omits l; that list is retained. The preceding prose says a pallet can be late “by up to p days” before being too late, but the definition following (2) says “p or more days,” and the example in the transition paragraph outsources at p days. The operative reading here is the definition accompanying (2), including equality at p; no threshold repair is made.

The stochastic arrival transition is

Ri,t+1dl = R¯ i,tdl + D i,d,[t+1],l∀ ⁡d ∈L,l ∈T,i≠d. 		(3)

Here i is a terminal location as in the inventory definition; the displayed quantifier is retained as printed. The next complete state is determined by the same state definition at epoch t + 1, with cycle day [t + 1].

One-period reward

For state St and feasible routing xt, the three printed components are

Ct1(S t,xt) = β∑ d,l,i,jxijtdl1 {j=d}, Ct2(S t,xt) = −clate ∑ d,l,i,jΛ(t,l,p)xijtdl1 {j=d∧Δlate(t,l,p)=1}, Ct3(S t,xt) = −cout ∑ d,l,i,jδ(j,d)xijtdl1 {j≠d∧Δout(t+1,j,d,p)=1}, Ct(St,xt) = Ct1(S t,xt) + Ct2(S t,xt) + Ct3(S t,xt).

The sums retain the source’s shorthand indices d,l,i,j, corresponding to terminal indices d,i,j ∈L and due day l ∈T. The factors 1{⋅} are indicators of their displayed conditions.

  • β is revenue per pallet delivered by the carrier to its final destination. Outsourced deliveries do not earn this revenue.
  • clate is the per-pallet, per-day late-delivery penalty. The function Δlate(t,l,p) returns 1 if a pallet will be late when delivered to its ultimate destination d by the end of decision epoch t, and 0 otherwise. The function Λ(t,l,p) returns the number of days the pallet will be late if j = d and Δlate(t,l,p) = 1, and 0 otherwise. These functions are retained in the abstract form used in the paper.
  • cout is the per-pallet outsourcing penalty. The source describes δ(j,d) as the shortest path from terminal j to destination d in Gflat, and uses it as the remaining travel distance: the outsourcing penalty is proportional to that distance. No additional edge-length convention is imposed.

The fixed parameters β, clate, and cout are the same for all commodities. Numerical values and demand distributions used only in the computational experiments are not imposed on this formulation.

Linked optimality equations

The paper gives the following Bellman characterization and optimal decision rule for this same discounted MDP:

V ∗(S t) = max ⁡ xt∈X(St) {Ct(St,xt) + γ𝔼 [ V ∗(S t+1)|St]} ,∀ ⁡St. 		(4)

Xπ∗ (St) = arg ⁡ max ⁡ xt∈X(St) {Ct(St,xt) + γ𝔼 [ V ∗(S t+1)|St]} ,∀ ⁡St. 		(5)

V ∗ is the optimal expected total future discounted reward from a state onward. The next state is obtained through (2)–(3) under the selected action. The conditioning in (4)–(5) is retained exactly as printed.
