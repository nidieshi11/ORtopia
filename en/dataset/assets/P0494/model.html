Mobile parcel lockers with time-ordered service and depot reloads

This is the explicitly approved operational revision of the route-planning model of Route Planning for Last-Mile Deliveries Using Mobile Parcel Lockers: A Hybrid Q-Learning Network Approach. It retains the paper’s location-selection, task-construction and three cost components. The changes to its printed routing constraints are specified below. The original transcription is archived separately.

Parking locations and service tasks

Each accepting customer n supplies coordinates hnk and a time window [Enk,Lnk] at each stop k. With parking centres gi and labels bnk, the location stage (1)–(3) assigns stops to nearest centres to minimise within-cluster squared Euclidean distance; each occupied centre is the arithmetic mean of its assigned stops. The paper selects the smallest number P of sites allowing walking range dink ≤ ρn and service radius ρn ≤ rm for the associated site. Within that number of sites the squared-distance objective determines the clustering. For the released family there is one stop per customer; all points are collinear, both radii are one, and strictly separated groups have unique nearest centres. One representative from each group is more than two distance units from the next, giving a matching lower bound on P. The route stage uses these centres; it does not relocate them for lower delivery cost.

For each parking space i with permitted interval [Ei,Li], the interval construction (15)–(18) intersects parking and customer windows and subdivides the effective availability into tasks of duration SV i. On these instances every customer window contains every relevant parking interval and its width equals one service duration, so there is one task per site. The distinct task u = (i,a) has location i(u) = i, service duration su = SV i, time-window endpoints eu,lu, and a given parcel quantity qu. The proximity and total-demand parameters are inputs; the paper supplies no general formula for parcel quantities from customer coordinates alone. No parking fee or occupancy charge applies.

Differences from the printed equations and author algorithm

Physical page 16 gives (4)–(14). In (5) the indexed fixed cost is printed outside the sum. Here costs are summed for the activated lockers individually. Equation (6) is implemented by charging every actual traversal. Equation (7) sums times for all lockers without an assignment factor; here only the executed visit contributes. Equation (8) and the exactly-once intent of (10) are preserved. Equation (9) bounds a locker’s total assigned quantity across the horizon; the approved reload model bounds each depot-to-depot round. The free/summed locker indices in (11)–(12) are replaced by an actual start and final return for each active locker and paired intermediate depot visits. The all-pairs equality in (13) also constrains unused arcs and uses one depot label where distinct depot visits need different times. It is replaced by the precedence inequalities on actual traversals above. Route sequences encode the binary visit/order/deployment choices of (14).

The operating prose on physical p. 13 describes initial loading and return after a round. Pages 21–22 discuss holding and returning to reload; Appendix A on p. 49 decodes a task order. These passages motivate the revision. They do not make it identical to (4)–(14), or establish that running the paper’s heuristic proves a global optimum of the revised model. The revised clock cannot restart before the preceding depot return. Historical literal-equation results remain attached separately.
