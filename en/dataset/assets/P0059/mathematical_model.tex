\section*{P0059 Long-run matching of impatient arrivals}
\subsection*{Mathematical formulation}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal A(\nu)=\left\{a\in\mathbb Z_+^{d(d+1)/2}:\quad
2a_{ii}+\sum_{j<i}a_{ji}+\sum_{j>i}a_{ij}\le\nu_i,
\quad i=1,\ldots,d\right\}.\tag{A}
\end{equation}

\begin{align}
v_i(\nu,a)&=\nu_i-2a_{ii}-\sum_{j<i}a_{ji}-\sum_{j>i}a_{ij},\quad i\in\mathcal T,\tag{S}\\
g(a)&=\sum_{1\le i\le j\le d}r_{ij}a_{ij}.\tag{R}
\end{align}

\[
q(v)=\sum_{i=1}^d\lambda_i+\sum_{i=1}^d\mu_i v_i.
\]

\begin{align}
\tau_{k+1}-\tau_k&\sim\operatorname{Exp}(q(v^k)),\tag{T1}\\
\Pr\{\nu^{k+1}=v^k+e_i\mid v^k\}&=\frac{\lambda_i}{q(v^k)},\quad i\in\mathcal T,\tag{T2}\\
\Pr\{\nu^{k+1}=v^k-e_i\mid v^k\}&=\frac{\mu_i v_i^k}{q(v^k)},\quad i\in\mathcal T:v_i^k>0.\tag{T3}
\end{align}

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac1t\,

\sum_{k:\,0<\tau_k\le t}g(a^k)
\right]\quad}\tag{DSM-R}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{Definitions and model scope}
A matching service receives people of finitely many types. Each type has an independent Poisson arrival stream with a given strictly positive rate, and each waiting person has an independent exponentially distributed remaining patience with the given strictly positive finite departure rate for that type. Patience is independent of arrivals. Each unordered pair of types has a finite symmetric reward, which may be negative; two different people of the same type may also be paired. Missing pair rewards may be supplied as zero. There is no separate waiting charge or abandonment penalty. The service sees arrivals, types and past departures, but not future arrivals or individual patience deadlines.

Immediately after each arrival or patience departure, before choosing matches, the service observes nonnegative whole-number waiting counts waiting counts $\nu_i$. It chooses a nonnegative whole-number number of pairs pair counts $a_{ij}$ for every unordered type pair. For each type, twice its same-type pair count plus all its cross-type pair counts cannot exceed its waiting count. Choosing no pairs is allowed. All chosen pairs leave immediately. The remaining count for a type is its observed waiting count less twice its same-type pair count and less all its cross-type pair counts. The immediate reward is the sum, over unordered type pairs, of reward per pair times the number paired.

After these matches, the rate of the next external event is the sum of all type arrival rates plus, for each type, its patience departure rate multiplied by its remaining count. The time until that event is exponential with this total rate. Conditional on the event, a type's arrival probability is its arrival rate divided by the total rate, and its patience-departure probability is its departure rate times its remaining count divided by the total rate. An arrival increases the corresponding count by one; a departure decreases it by one and can occur only when that count is positive. This update occurs before the next matching decision. Matched people no longer contribute departure events. There is no artificial upper limit on the waiting population and no replacement of the state-dependent event rate by a fixed rate.

The service starts empty at time zero with no initial matches. Each later choice may depend on the observed history of arrivals, departures, waiting counts and previous choices, and may use independent randomization, but must always respect the available people at that moment. The service seeks the greatest long-run expected reward per unit of calendar time: for each positive time horizon it takes the expectation of rewards earned at external events strictly after time zero and no later than that horizon, divides by the horizon, and then uses the lower limiting value as the horizon grows without bound. This long-run measure does not equate a single finite simulation's average reward with the achieved long-run value.
