Budget-constrained online probing: data and decision variables

This is the budget-constrained, unit-weight version in Section 4 of Vera, Banerjee, and Gurvich, Online Allocation and Pricing: Constant Regret via Bellman Inequalities, arXiv:1906.06361v2. The probing-cost alternative is not imposed. In (7), sums over j and k range over [n] and [m], respectively. The parameters and decision-variable domain are

(bh,bp,z) ∈ ℕ2 × ℝ ≥0n,x ∈ ℝ ≥03n+2nm.

The symbol P[bh,bp,z] denotes the optimal value of (7); the paper also writes the same value as P[(bh,bp),z] or P[b,z].

For every j ∈ [n], xja, xjr, and xjp summarize the numbers of future type-j arrivals accepted without probing, rejected without probing, and probed. For every (j,k) ∈ [n] × [m], xjka and xjkr summarize the numbers of probed type-j arrivals revealing reward rjk that are subsequently accepted and rejected. These are continuous LP variables, not integer decisions for individual arrivals.

The horizon is T, with time-to-go

t = T,T − 1,…,1.

The arriving public type is drawn from [n] with known distribution p = (p1,…,pn). Each arrival of type j has an independent random reward, with known reward support and probabilities

Rj ∈{rjk : k ∈ [m]},qjk := ℙ[Rj = rjk](j ∈ [n],k ∈ [m]).

Section 4 assumes, without loss of generality,

rj1 < rj2 < ⋯ < rjm,rjm > 0∀ ⁡j ∈ [n].

The support has size m. The expected reward coefficient used in (7) is

r¯j := ∑ k∈[m]rjkqjk∀ ⁡j ∈ [n].

Section 2.1 calls the expected reward rj = 𝔼[Rj]; the operative coefficient in Section 4 and (7) is the barred r¯j, distinct from each realized reward rjk.

All arrivals have unit weights in the selected version. The initial resource (hiring) and probing budgets are fixed data

Bh ∈ ℕ,Bp ∈ ℕ.

Acceptance consumes one unit of the resource budget; probing consumes one unit of the probing budget. An arrival can be accepted without probing. Section 2.1 denotes the resource budget by B and states that the problem reduces to OnlineKnapsack when either Bp ≥ T or Bp = 0; these are boundary cases, not additional restrictions on (7).
