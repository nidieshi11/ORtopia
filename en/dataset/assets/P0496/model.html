The objective is the verbal objective in the unnumbered Perfect Bayesian Equilibrium definition of Garcia, Janssen, and Shopova, Dynamic Pricing with Uncertain Capacities, January 26, 2022 (physical PDF pp. 7–9). Each firm’s first-period strategy is optimal conditional on its capacity type, given the rival’s strategies (pj1c,pj1u) and the equilibrium continuation reactions. Strategies may be mixed.

Subject to the following equilibrium requirements:

  1. At the beginning of period 2, the non-selling firm i updates its belief 𝜃(pj1) about the rival being constrained using Bayes’ Rule whenever possible.
  2. For every first-period price pair (p11,p21), each firm i with unsold capacity chooses pi2 to maximize second-period profit, given the rival’s second-period price pj2 and the updated belief 𝜃(pj1).

  3. Each firm i chooses (pi1c,pi1u) to maximize expected profit over both periods, given (pj1c,pj1u) and the equilibrium reactions

    pi2(pi1,pj1),i = 1,2,j≠i.

The source’s strategy and belief objects are

(pi1c,p i1u,p i2(pi1,pj1)),i = 1,2,j≠i,𝜃(pj1).

Here c and u denote constrained and unconstrained capacity types. The belief 𝜃(pj1) is held by the non-selling firm i about firm j being constrained after observing j’s first-period price. Conditional on having capacity left, a firm’s second-period pricing does not depend on whether its original type was constrained or unconstrained (footnote 14).

The fixed baseline inputs and timing are as follows. There are two firms and two selling periods,

i ∈{1,2},t ∈{1,2}.

A constrained firm can sell one unit; an unconstrained firm can serve demand in both periods. Initial capacities are privately known. The fixed prior satisfies

Pr ⁡ (firm i is constrained) = α,Pr ⁡ (firm i is unconstrained) = 1 − α,i = 1,2,

and capacity types are independent across firms. Production cost is normalized to zero. Consumers are myopic and have unit demand; market size in each period and willingness to pay are both normalized to 1. A consumer arrives in each period, observes current prices, and buys from the lowest-priced available firm if that price is no greater than 1. Otherwise the consumer leaves and does not return. If both firms charge the same price, all demand goes to one firm, with each firm selling with probability 1∕2.

Nature draws capacities before period 1 and each firm learns its own type. Firms then choose pi1. After the first purchase, firms observe both first-period prices and know which firm sold, if either did. They update beliefs; only firms with unsold capacity choose pi2.

Printed purchasing-threshold conflict. The operative setup on physical p. 8 says that purchase occurs when the lowest price is “not larger than 1.” Figure 1 on that page instead prints

p1 < 1,p2 < 1.

The weak purchasing threshold is retained here, consistently with the paper’s subsequent sales and price atoms at 1; the figure’s strict thresholds are not imposed simultaneously. The baseline definition does not state a global price action interval. In particular, the paper explicitly considers first-period deviations p1 > 1, so equilibrium supports below are not global bounds on feasible prices.

For all prices in the common support, each type’s equilibrium profit must be constant. The linked payoff relation is

Πu(p) = Πc(p) + Q(p)𝜃(p).

Consequently, Q(p)𝜃(p) is constant and positive over the support; the posterior increases with the price, and an unconstrained firm uses only prices having positive first-period sale probability. The upper endpoint has a mass point.

Allowed endpoint. The source includes p¯ = 1 and requires 𝜃(p¯) = p¯. If its statement that both types have a mass point is read as requiring a strictly positive atom for each type, this conflicts at p¯ = 1 with Bayesian updating whenever both types have positive prior probability. The printed parameter range and atom statement are retained; no replacement atom specification or restriction p¯ < 1 is added. Likewise, formulas (1)–(2) remain restricted to 0 < 𝜃 < 1 at this endpoint.
