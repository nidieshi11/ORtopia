Operative definitions and variable roles

Network and indices. The directed transport network is

G = (N,A),A = {(i,j) : i,j ∈N,i≠j}.

Here N is the set of terminals, K the set of vehicle types, S the set of potential transport services, and H the set of competing alternatives. For each service s, Ls denotes its sequence of legs, indexed by ls. Each leg is an arc, and the service sequence starts and ends at the same node. Services may have intermediate stops.

Fixed operational data. For vehicle type k, V k is the number of available vehicles and Qk is their capacity in TEUs. The parameter Wsk is the maximum number of cycles of service s that one vehicle of type k can perform during the planning horizon. The paper describes this as maximum operating time divided by cycle time, including travel and terminal time; its explicit computation on PDF page 20 is

Wsk = Tmax ⁡ tsksail + tskport.

Here Tmax ⁡ is maximum operating time, tsksail sailing time and tskport time spent in ports. No rounding of this expression is imposed.

The fixed cost of operating service s with type k is cskFIX, in euros; the variable cost per container transported from i to j using that service and type is cijskVAR, in euros per TEU. The aggregated demand is Dij, in TEUs. The incidence parameters are defined by

δijls = { 1,if a container traveling from i to j uses leg ls, 0,otherwise, (i,j) ∈A,s ∈S,ls ∈Ls,
ϕijs = { 1,if both terminals i and j are contained in service s, 0, otherwise , (i,j) ∈A,s ∈S.

Deterministic utility definition. The selected generic utility specification is the one used to derive (19):

UijO = Ū ijO + β cpij + βffij = ŪijO + β cpij + βf ∑ s∈S ∑ k∈Kϕijsfsk. 		(17)

In particular,

fij = ∑ s∈S ∑ k∈Kϕijsfsk.

These definitions apply to OD pairs (i,j) ∈A. The quantity ŪijO is the exogenous part of the operator’s utility, not the full utility UijO. The coefficients βc and βf weight price and frequency, respectively. Competing utilities Uijh, for (i,j) ∈A and h ∈H, are fixed data. They remain abstract in this generic deterministic formulation, as does ŪijO; no random errors, sampled coefficients, or realization indices are introduced. Competition is fixed and exogenous and does not react to the operator’s decisions.

Frequency representation. The number of binary digits is defined in the prose immediately preceding (19):

Bsk = ⌈log ⁡ 2(WskV k + 1)⌉.

The sums in (19) and (20) run from b = 0 to Bsk − 1. The variables fskb are the binary digits of fsk. The auxiliary variable aijskb represents the product of the binary digit fskb and the cargo variable xijsk, using the printed linearization (21)–(23) and domain (33), rather than an additional nonlinear equality.

Decision variables. The operator chooses vsk, the number of type-k vehicles assigned to service s; fsk, its frequency with that vehicle type; and pij, the price per container from i to j. The variables xijsk are cargo volumes assigned to the operator’s services and zijh are cargo volumes assigned to competing alternatives. The original notation table gives cargo volumes in TEUs and prices in euros per TEU.

The variables λij are introduced as dual variables associated with the demand equalities (9). The binary pairs (yijI,yijII) and (yijIh,yijIIh) implement the operator and competitor complementarity disjunctions, respectively. The symbols MijI, MijII, MijIh, and MijIIh are fixed big-M coefficients; the paper supplies no formulas or numerical bounds for them in this formulation. They are retained abstractly. Although the printed maximization subscript in (19) lists only v,f,p,x,z, the frequency digits, product variables, dual variables, and disjunction variables are also variables of the final formulation.

Scope of the added cap. Equation (56) is introduced because the data used to estimate the mode-choice coefficients contain IWT frequencies no greater than 35 services per week. Its printed scope is each service–vehicle-type frequency fsk, not the aggregate OD frequency fij. Apart from this explicitly selected cap, numerical fleet, network, utility-coefficient, and operating-time values from the case study are not imposed.

Source qualifications retained without repair

  • The final block on PDF page 16 explicitly inherits (2)–(7) and (9)–(11). Its disjunctions replace the preceding nonlinear complementarity rows; neither those superseded rows nor a separate lower-level optimization problem are imposed here.

  • The source defines the scalar digit count Bsk but uses the distinct set symbol B in (21)–(23), (32), and (33) without defining that set or relating it explicitly to the service-dependent digit ranges. Both the printed sums and the printed quantifiers have been retained; no definition of B has been supplied.

  • Equation (34) contains competitor-indexed variables but prints only the quantifier ∀ ⁡(i,j) ∈A, with no h-quantifier. This omission is preserved. The symbol ℕ in (7) and (33) is also retained without adding a convention about zero.

  • Equation (33) makes the product variable integer, although the preceding prose describes a binary–continuous product and (10) imposes only nonnegativity on cargo. Equation (35) makes the demand-equality dual variable nonnegative, although an equality multiplier is not generally sign-restricted. These printed restrictions are retained, not relaxed.

  • The frequency term in (19) follows the printed same-service product in the revenue transformation on PDF page 15. In contrast, (17) uses the total OD frequency in the utility of every operator shipment. Expanding that total-frequency utility against all operator cargo would also produce cross-service products, which are absent from (19). The printed objective and utility definition are retained without adding such terms.

  • The generic formulation divides by βc but does not state an admissible range or a zero-coefficient exception. No additional sign or nonzero constraint has been inserted. The prose following (17) calls the exogenous part Ūij, omitting the superscript O that appears in the formulas; the operative notation follows the formulas.
