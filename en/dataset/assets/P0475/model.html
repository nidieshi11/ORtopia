Asymmetric decentralized supply-chain equilibrium

For every manufacturer i = 1,…,M, the manufacturer’s MPEC is

max ⁡ yi,x^,v vTy i − ciTy i, (10) s.t. ∑ k=1My k = ∑ j=1Nx j, (11) 0 ≤q^ + (B^ + Ĥ)x^ ⊥x^ ≥ 0, (12) yi ≥ 0. (13)

In manufacturer i’s maximization, the production quantities yk of all other manufacturers, k≠i, are held fixed. The manufacturer–retailer equilibrium simultaneously solves these MPECs for all M manufacturers, with the same retailer order vector x^ and wholesale price vector v. Thus the selected model is the coupled equilibrium problem with equilibrium constraints, not a maximization of aggregate manufacturer profit. Only yi is the manufacturer’s free production decision; v is implicitly determined through wholesale market clearing and the retailers’ equilibrium. There is no manufacturer differentiation: every manufacturer sells a given product at the same wholesale price.

Indices, data, and variable roles

There are M manufacturers, N retailers, and P products. Manufacturer indices run from 1 to M, retailer indices from 1 to N, and product indices from 1 to P. In particular, k in (11) is a manufacturer index, whereas k in xjk and pjk below is a product index.

The fixed unit production cost of manufacturer i is ci ∈ ℝP. Its production vector is yi ∈ ℝP. The wholesale price vector is

v = (v1,v2,…,vP) ∈ ℝP.

The retailer order vectors and their stack are

xj = (xj1,xj2,…,xjP) ∈ ℝP,x^ = (x 1,x2,…,xN) ∈ ℝNP.

Here xjk is retailer j’s order quantity of product k. Nonnegative orders are imposed by (12), and nonnegative production by (13) in every manufacturer’s block. The displayed model imposes no separate sign constraint on v.

The fixed inverse-demand intercepts and their stack are

aj = (aj1,aj2,…,ajP) ∈ ℝP,â = (a 1,a2,…,aN) ∈ ℝNP.

The vector â gives the prices consumers would pay at zero retail supply and at a realization 𝜖 = 1. The inverse-demand sensitivity matrix is B^ ∈ ℝNP×NP; its blocks Hj and Gjl are P × P matrices.

Wholesale decisions and retailer orders are made before 𝜖 is realized. Wholesale prices and manufacturer profits are therefore deterministic in this decentralized model.

Retailer j has fixed risk-aversion parameter γj. The paper assumes risk-averse retailers and also allows the risk-neutral boundary case γj = 0. Its risk adjustment is

ηj = 1 − γjσ,ηj > 0,j = 1,…,N.

The strict positivity of ηj is the paper’s maintained premise to avoid trivial equilibria. The stated boundary cases are

γj = 0orσ = 0⟹ηj = 1.

Assumption 5.1. The matrix B^ + Ĥ is positive definite.

This is an input premise, not a constraint on production or orders. Proposition B.4 states that it implies that every Hj, j = 1,…,N, is positive definite.

The vector in (12) is defined by

q^ = ( v η1 − a1, v η2 − a2,…, v ηN − aN) ∈ ℝNP.

In particular, q^ depends on the wholesale price variable; it is not independent fixed data in the manufacturer MPEC.

Defining retailer preferences and complementarity

The following rows define the retailer response represented by (12); they are not additional upper-level maximizations. Given v and the orders of the other retailers, retailer j’s printed mean–standard-deviation decision is

max ⁡ xj≥0 − xjTv + x jT(a j −B^j∙x^) − γjxjT(a j −B^j∙x^)σ. 		(1)

With the above definition of ηj, the paper rewrites this as

max ⁡ xj≥0 − xjTv + η jxjT(a j −B^j∙x^). 		(2)

and gives the following retailer best-response complementarity rows:

0 ≤ v ηj− (aj − 2Hjxj −∑ l=1 l≠j NG jlxl) ⊥ xj ≥ 0,j = 1,…,N.

Their concatenation is precisely

0 ≤q^ + (B^ + Ĥ)x^ ⊥x^ ≥ 0. 		(9)

Equation (12) is this same condition, imposed once in each manufacturer’s feasible block. Proposition 5.2 states that under Assumption 5.1, for a given wholesale price v, (9) has a unique solution, the retailer equilibrium. Its jth block defines the implicit response

xj(v) : ℝP→ℝP.

The manufacturer formulation (10)–(13) replaces the implicit-response formulation (3)–(4); the latter is not imposed as a second manufacturer problem.

As defined on page 12 and in Appendix A, for s,t ∈ ℝn,

0 ≤ s ⊥ t ≥ 0⟺sr ≥ 0,tr ≥ 0,srtr = 0,r = 1,…,n.

Thus the complementarity in (12), with n = NP, means

x^ ≥ 0,q^ + (B^ + Ĥ)x^ ≥ 0,x^T[q^ + (B^ + Ĥ)x^] = 0.

These are the meaning of (12), not an alternative algorithmic subproblem.

Source scope and printed qualifications

The operative block and coupling are from Section 5.2, physical pages 20–21; the defining demand and preferences are from pages 6–10. The complementarity definition is in Appendix A, pages 34–35, and the asymmetric retailer proof is on pages 45–46. Equation (14), on page 21, specifies a numerical example in Section 5.3.1, not a further condition of the general asymmetric equilibrium, and is not imposed here. The paper does not establish uniqueness of the coupled manufacturer–retailer equilibrium for the asymmetric chain.

Two printed qualifications are retained without repair. First, (1) identifies xjT(aj −B^j∙x^)σ as the profit standard deviation, without an absolute value or an accompanying restriction making the revenue coefficient nonnegative. Second, the asymmetric retailer proof and (9)–(12) use 2Hjxj on the diagonal, although the general demand definition and Assumption 5.1 do not state that Hj is symmetric. Positive definiteness alone does not supply that symmetry. The printed expressions and their stated scope are retained; no revenue-sign restriction, symmetry assumption, or transpose correction has been added.
