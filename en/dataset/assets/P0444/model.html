Selected extensions and source scope. The objective and policy definition are on physical PDF page 6. The following feasible sets apply the customer-index replacement on pages 6–7 together with the multiperiod-travel replacement on page 7 to the original definitions on pages 5–6. The paper prints these two replacements separately; their simultaneous use is explicitly stated in Section 6 on page 17. Accordingly, the loaded-movement term in the travel-time transition is replaced by its sum over customers. The single-period transitions and resource balances are superseded, not imposed in addition. Integer domains from (5) are retained for the customer-indexed loaded movements.

Indices, data, decisions, and state

T = {1,…,T} set of periods over which demands occur, I = set of locations, K = set of vehicle types, D = set of customers.

The customer extension defines D as the set of customers; the discussion and experiments also refer to customer types. For i,j ∈I, k ∈K, d ∈D, and t ∈T:

  • Dijtd is the random number of demands from customer d that must be carried from i to j at period t.
  • xijtke is the decision giving the number of type-k vehicles moving empty from i to j at period t.
  • xijtkdl is the decision giving the number of type-k vehicles moving loaded from i to j at period t, serving customer d.
  • cijtke is the fixed cost of the corresponding empty movement.
  • cijtkdl is the fixed net profit of the corresponding loaded movement serving customer d.

The movement costs and profits remain abstract data in this formulation; the experimental cost-generation rules are not imposed.

With the customer extension, the vector notation of page 5 becomes

Dt = [Dijtd] i,j∈I,d∈D, xte = [x ijtke] i,j∈I,k∈K,xtl = [x ijtkdl] i,j∈I,k∈K,d∈D, cte = [c ijtke] i,j∈I,k∈K,ctl = [c ijtkdl] i,j∈I,k∈K,d∈D, xt = [xte∣x tl], c t = [−cte∣c tl].

In particular, the reward in the policy objective is

ctxt = ∑ i,j∈I ∑ k∈K (−cijtkex ijtke + ∑ d∈Dcijtkdlx ijtkdl) .

Demand assumptions and service interpretation. Page 4 assumes temporal independence, stated in the original single-customer notation as: Dijt and Di′j′t′ are independent for t≠t′. Page 6 uses this assumption to justify deterministic Markov policies. The customer extension introduces Dijtd without separately restating an independence formula with customer indices; it does not impose independence between customers within a period. The demand distribution is otherwise abstract in the core model. A demand at a location can be served only by a vehicle at that same location. All customer demands must be served in the application, but demands not served by in-house vehicles are treated as lost to this model and served by chartered vehicles. Loaded-movement net profit is the incremental profit of in-house service over chartered service. Consequently the printed in-house demand condition is an inequality, not an equality.

Inbound-resource state and travel times. On page 7, Rit′tk is the number of type-k vehicles inbound to location i at time t, arriving there at time t′; the definition states i ∈I, k ∈K, t ∈T, and t′∈T. The component Rittk supplies the vehicles available for movements at time t. The fixed datum τij is the travel time from i to j. Section 5.3, page 14, explicitly gives the multiperiod state vector as

Rt = [Rit′tk] i∈I,k∈K,t≤t′<t+τmax ⁡ ,

where τmax ⁡ is the maximum travel time possible.

Printed indexing issue. The page-7 definition states t′∈T, while its transition is printed with scope t′ > t; page 14 gives the state-vector range t ≤ t′ < t + τmax ⁡ , without truncation at T. These printed scopes are retained above. The displayed transition also retains the carry-in term Rjt′tk. No zero-padding convention for components outside the displayed state-vector range, nor an additional end-of-horizon restriction on movements, is supplied here to reconcile these scopes.
