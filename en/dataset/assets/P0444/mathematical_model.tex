\section*{Full-horizon stochastic integer multicommodity resource-allocation policy model}

\begin{align*}
\max_{\pi}\quad &\mathbb{E}\left\{\left.\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}}c_tX_t^{\pi}(R_t,D_t)\,\right|\,R_1\right\}\\
\text{subject to}\quad
&x_t=X_t^{\pi}(R_t,D_t),\qquad t\in\mathcal{T},\\
&(x_t,R_{t+1})\in\mathcal{Y}(R_t,D_t),\qquad t\in\mathcal{T}.
\end{align*}
Here \(\pi\) is a deterministic Markov policy, specified by the sequence of decision functions \(\{X_t^{\pi}:t\in\mathcal{T}\}\). Each function maps the current resource state and observed demand outcome to a feasible decision. Policy feasibility applies to the states and demand outcomes at which these functions are used, not merely to one fixed demand realization. The initial resource state \(R_1\) is given; subsequent resource states are generated by the decisions and transitions below.

\paragraph{Selected extensions and source scope.}
The objective and policy definition are on physical PDF page 6. The following feasible sets apply the customer-index replacement on pages 6--7 together with the multiperiod-travel replacement on page 7 to the original definitions on pages 5--6. The paper prints these two replacements separately; their simultaneous use is explicitly stated in Section 6 on page 17. Accordingly, the loaded-movement term in the travel-time transition is replaced by its sum over customers. The single-period transitions and resource balances are superseded, not imposed in addition. Integer domains from (5) are retained for the customer-indexed loaded movements.

\section*{Feasible decisions and resource transitions}
For each \(t\in\mathcal{T}\), the selected feasible-decision set is
\[
\mathcal{X}(R_t,D_t)=
\left\{x_t:\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}}\left(x_{ijt}^{ke}+\sum_{d\in\mathcal{D}}x_{ijt}^{kdl}\right)=R_{itt}^{k}
&&i\in\mathcal{I},\ k\in\mathcal{K},\\
&\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}x_{ijt}^{kdl}\leq D_{ijt}^{d}
&&i,j\in\mathcal{I},\ d\in\mathcal{D},\\
&x_{ijt}^{ke}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
&&i,j\in\mathcal{I},\ k\in\mathcal{K},\\
&x_{ijt}^{kdl}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
&&i,j\in\mathcal{I},\ k\in\mathcal{K},\ d\in\mathcal{D}
\end{aligned}\right\}.
\]
The corresponding feasible-transition set is
\[
\mathcal{Y}(R_t,D_t)=
\left\{(x_t,R_{t+1}):\begin{aligned}
&R_{jt',t+1}^{k}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\left(
\mathbf{1}_{\tau_{ij}}(t'-t)x_{ijt}^{ke}
+\mathbf{1}_{\tau_{ij}}(t'-t)\sum_{d\in\mathcal{D}}x_{ijt}^{kdl}
\right)+R_{jt't}^{k},\\[-1mm]
&\hspace{35mm}j\in\mathcal{I},\ k\in\mathcal{K},\ t'>t,\\
&x_t\in\mathcal{X}(R_t,D_t)
\end{aligned}\right\}.
\]
Thus all available vehicles are assigned to empty or loaded movements; the loaded movements of all vehicle types share the demand limit for each origin, destination, and customer. The indicator used in the transition is defined on page 7 by
\[
\mathbf{1}_{y}(x)=
\begin{cases}
1,&x=y,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

\section*{Indices, data, decisions, and state}
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{T}&=\{1,\ldots,T\}&&\text{set of periods over which demands occur},\\
\mathcal{I}&=\text{set of locations},\\
\mathcal{K}&=\text{set of vehicle types},\\
\mathcal{D}&=\text{set of customers}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The customer extension defines \(\mathcal{D}\) as the set of customers; the discussion and experiments also refer to customer types. For \(i,j\in\mathcal{I}\), \(k\in\mathcal{K}\), \(d\in\mathcal{D}\), and \(t\in\mathcal{T}\):
\begin{itemize}
\item \(D_{ijt}^{d}\) is the random number of demands from customer \(d\) that must be carried from \(i\) to \(j\) at period \(t\).
\item \(x_{ijt}^{ke}\) is the decision giving the number of type-\(k\) vehicles moving empty from \(i\) to \(j\) at period \(t\).
\item \(x_{ijt}^{kdl}\) is the decision giving the number of type-\(k\) vehicles moving loaded from \(i\) to \(j\) at period \(t\), serving customer \(d\).
\item \(c_{ijt}^{ke}\) is the fixed cost of the corresponding empty movement.
\item \(c_{ijt}^{kdl}\) is the fixed net profit of the corresponding loaded movement serving customer \(d\).
\end{itemize}
The movement costs and profits remain abstract data in this formulation; the experimental cost-generation rules are not imposed.

With the customer extension, the vector notation of page 5 becomes
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_t&=[D_{ijt}^{d}]_{i,j\in\mathcal{I},\ d\in\mathcal{D}},\\
x_t^e&=[x_{ijt}^{ke}]_{i,j\in\mathcal{I},\ k\in\mathcal{K}},&
x_t^l&=[x_{ijt}^{kdl}]_{i,j\in\mathcal{I},\ k\in\mathcal{K},\ d\in\mathcal{D}},\\
c_t^e&=[c_{ijt}^{ke}]_{i,j\in\mathcal{I},\ k\in\mathcal{K}},&
c_t^l&=[c_{ijt}^{kdl}]_{i,j\in\mathcal{I},\ k\in\mathcal{K},\ d\in\mathcal{D}},\\
x_t&=[x_t^e\mid x_t^l],&c_t&=[-c_t^e\mid c_t^l].
\end{aligned}
\]
In particular, the reward in the policy objective is
\[
c_tx_t=\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{I}}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}
\left(-c_{ijt}^{ke}x_{ijt}^{ke}+\sum_{d\in\mathcal{D}}c_{ijt}^{kdl}x_{ijt}^{kdl}\right).
\]

\paragraph{Demand assumptions and service interpretation.}
Page 4 assumes temporal independence, stated in the original single-customer notation as: \(D_{ijt}\) and \(D_{i'j't'}\) are independent for \(t\ne t'\). Page 6 uses this assumption to justify deterministic Markov policies. The customer extension introduces \(D_{ijt}^{d}\) without separately restating an independence formula with customer indices; it does not impose independence between customers within a period. The demand distribution is otherwise abstract in the core model. A demand at a location can be served only by a vehicle at that same location. All customer demands must be served in the application, but demands not served by in-house vehicles are treated as lost to this model and served by chartered vehicles. Loaded-movement net profit is the incremental profit of in-house service over chartered service. Consequently the printed in-house demand condition is an inequality, not an equality.

\paragraph{Inbound-resource state and travel times.}
On page 7, \(R_{it't}^{k}\) is the number of type-\(k\) vehicles inbound to location \(i\) at time \(t\), arriving there at time \(t'\); the definition states \(i\in\mathcal{I}\), \(k\in\mathcal{K}\), \(t\in\mathcal{T}\), and \(t'\in\mathcal{T}\). The component \(R_{itt}^{k}\) supplies the vehicles available for movements at time \(t\). The fixed datum \(\tau_{ij}\) is the travel time from \(i\) to \(j\). Section 5.3, page 14, explicitly gives the multiperiod state vector as
\[
R_t=[R_{it't}^{k}]_{i\in\mathcal{I},\ k\in\mathcal{K},\ t\leq t'<t+\tau_{\max}},
\]
where \(\tau_{\max}\) is the maximum travel time possible.

\paragraph{Printed indexing issue.}
The page-7 definition states \(t'\in\mathcal{T}\), while its transition is printed with scope \(t'>t\); page 14 gives the state-vector range \(t\leq t'<t+\tau_{\max}\), without truncation at \(T\). These printed scopes are retained above. The displayed transition also retains the carry-in term \(R_{jt't}^{k}\). No zero-padding convention for components outside the displayed state-vector range, nor an additional end-of-horizon restriction on movements, is supplied here to reconcile these scopes.

\section*{Linked exact value-function equations}
The exact recursion on page 6 uses the same feasible-transition set, with the selected replacements above:
\begin{equation}
V_t(R_t)=\mathbb{E}\left\{\left.
\max_{(x_t,R_{t+1})\in\mathcal{Y}(R_t,D_t)}
\bigl\{c_tx_t+V_{t+1}(R_{t+1})\bigr\}
\,\right|\,R_t\right\}.
\tag{6}
\end{equation}
The defining relation preceding (7) on page 8 is
\[
V_t(R_t)=\mathbb{E}\{V_t(R_t,D_t)\mid R_t\},
\]
where
\begin{equation}
V_t(R_t,D_t)=
\max_{(x_t,R_{t+1})\in\mathcal{Y}(R_t,D_t)}
\bigl\{c_tx_t+V_{t+1}(R_{t+1})\bigr\}.
\tag{7}
\end{equation}
The page image uses the same plain symbol \(V_t\) for the one-argument expected value function and the two-argument demand-conditioned function; their arguments distinguish their roles. The printed recursion does not supply a terminal-value equation for \(V_{T+1}\); none is added. The Monte Carlo approximation (8), value-function approximations, posterior deterministic programs, and updating algorithms are not part of this policy formulation.
