Algorithm 1: privacy-aware dynamic personalized pricing

Revenue objective and price-selection rule

The retailer seeks to maximize

𝔼 [∑ t=1Tp tyt] ,pt ∈ [0,1],t = 1,…,T.

The selected policy is Algorithm 1, not a claim that its prices exactly maximize this cumulative objective. Its price-selection row is

pn = arg ⁡ max ⁡ p∈[0,1] min ⁡ {1,pf (ϕn⊤ ⁡𝜃^p) + γϕ n⊤ ⁡(Λp)−1ϕn} ,ϕn = ϕ(xn,pn),n = T0 + 1,… ⁡ ,T.

The states and releases in this row are defined below. The setting is a single product with unlimited inventories (pp. 2–3).

Printed price notation. Algorithm 1, line 10, defines ϕn using pn, not the optimization variable p. This printed dependence is retained; it is not replaced by ϕ(xn,p).

Demand, information, and fixed model assumptions

We retain the abstract exponential-family model of Section 3 (pp. 9–10), without specializing to either of its illustrative likelihood examples. At periods t = 1,…,T, xt is the arriving customer’s personal information, pt is the offered price, and yt ∈ ℝ is realized demand. The known feature map and unknown fixed model satisfy

ϕt = ϕ(xt,pt) ∈ ℝd,𝜃∗∈ ℝd.

No particular dimension or coordinate domain for xt is specified in this abstract formulation. The distribution-family components ζ,m(⋅),h(⋅) remain abstract. The printed demand model is

Pr ⁡ [yt = y∣pt,xt,𝜃∗] = exp ⁡ {ζ (yϕ t⊤ ⁡𝜃∗− m(ϕ t⊤ ⁡𝜃∗)) + h(y)}. 		(1)

Its conditional mean and noise representation are

f(ϕt⊤ ⁡𝜃∗) := m′(ϕ t⊤ ⁡𝜃∗) = 𝔼[y t∣pt,xt,𝜃∗],

yt = f(ϕt⊤ ⁡𝜃∗) + ξ t. 		(2)

The paragraph following (2) states that ϕt = ϕ(xt,pt) and ξt are independent random variables satisfying

𝔼[ξt∣pt,xt] = 0.

The history and subsequent conditional centering are

Ft = {(xτ,yτ,pτ)}τ=1t,𝔼[ξ t∣xt,pt,Ft−1] = 0.

In the general setting used here, x1,…,xT are arbitrarily chosen before pricing starts (the oblivious-adversary setting). The policy chooses pt conditioned on Ft−1 and xt, after which demand is realized according to (2).

The source imposes the following model assumptions throughout the paper. They concern fixed inputs and the demand process, not additional optimization constraints on the estimator:

BY < ∞,|yt|≤ BY for every period t in every database D; ∥ϕ(x,p)∥2 ≤ 1,∥𝜃∗∥ 2 ≤ 1 for all x,p; 𝔼[ξt∣xt,pt,Ft−1] = 0, there exists s < ∞ such that 𝔼[eλξt ∣xt,pt,Ft−1] ≤ eλ2s2∕2 for all λ ∈ ℝ; f = m′ : ℝ→[0,1],f continuously differentiable and strictly increasing; 1 ≤ K < ∞,K−1 ≤ f′(z) ≤ K for all |z|≤ 2; 1 ≤ G < ∞,G−1 ≤ ζ ≤ G.

Here BY ,s,K,G are model parameters. In the likelihood notation used by Algorithm 3, the family in (1), evaluated at the candidate parameter 𝜃, is

p(yt∣ϕt,𝜃) = exp ⁡ {ζ (ytϕt⊤ ⁡𝜃 − m(ϕ t⊤ ⁡𝜃)) + h(y t)}.

The likelihood symbol p( ⋅∣ ⋅) is distinct from the scalar price p. This uses the Section 3 family, rather than the differently written recap on p. 25.

Printed release-time conflict. Section 6.1 on p. 21 states that PrivateCov(n,𝜀,δ) is the Σnp matrix released at the end of iteration n − 1. The tree retains its printed iteration indices and uses every historical row once. In particular, Algorithm 2 does not specify an iteration-zero release for the permitted T0 = 0 case.

Privacy definition attached to the policy

For the privacy definition (pp. 12–14), write

D = {ot}t=1T,o t = (xt,yt),D′ = {o t′} t=1T.

Neighboring databases differing at time t satisfy

ot≠ot′,o τ = oτ′for all τ≠t.

For 𝜀,δ > 0, anticipating (𝜀,δ)-differential privacy requires, for every such pair and every measurable set P>t,

Pr ⁡ [pt+1,… ⁡ ,pT ∈P>t∣π,D] ≤ e𝜀 Pr ⁡ [p t+1,… ⁡ ,pT ∈P>t∣π,D′] + δ. 		(3)

The private releasers use historical data only, and the price optimizer uses their outputs and the current xt, not historical sensitive data directly (pp. 17–18). The claimed composite privacy levels are

𝜀 = 𝜀1 + 𝜀2,δ = δ1 + δ2.

This privacy definition is model-free; the demand assumptions above are used for utility analysis, not to define privacy. Corollary 4 on p. 23 calls the resulting price sequence “differentially private” without the word “anticipating,” although its cited Proposition 2 concludes anticipating differential privacy. The definition retained here is (3), not the stronger full-price-sequence requirement.

Source scope. The operative formulation is the parameterized Algorithm 1 and its Algorithms 2–3 dependencies, with the Section 3 demand family. Gaussian and logistic examples, theorem-specific parameter choices, regret-analysis inequalities, and simulation settings are not additional operative constraints. The paper refers proofs to an online supplement; the published electronic companion, EC.2, uses the history of summaries through period t − 1 in the price at t. EC.3.1 uses a single horizon-based tree depth. The supplied page images for pp. 3, 20, 21, and 24 support the objective and algorithm rows reproduced above.

Selection on the archived benchmark inputs. For the ten supplied scalar inputs, T0 ≥ 2 and a release exists before every adaptive decision including the last. The implementation chooses the largest maximizer in the printed price selector. Each case has price-independent ϕ(x,p) = (1 + x)∕2 and the logistic mean. At initialization the scalar accepted matrix is positive; the strict doubling gate preserves positivity. Consequently the capped optimistic score is nondecreasing in the offered price on each realized history, and price one is a maximizing choice. Uniform exploratory offers remain mandatory. These instance facts do not specify price-dependent or multidimensional cases. Only future public offers enter the anticipating-privacy statement. The archived seeded diagnostic paths, noisy releases, purchases and estimates support reproduction and are not a public release with an asserted privacy guarantee.
