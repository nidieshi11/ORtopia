\section*{Algorithm 1: privacy-aware dynamic personalized pricing}

\subsection*{Revenue objective and price-selection rule}
The retailer seeks to maximize
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}p_ty_t\right],
\qquad p_t\in[0,1],\quad t=1,\ldots,T.
\]
The selected policy is Algorithm 1, not a claim that its prices exactly maximize this cumulative objective. Its price-selection row is
\[
p_n=\arg\max_{p\in[0,1]}\min\left\{1,
 p f\!\left(\phi_n^{\top}\widehat\theta^{p}\right)
 +\gamma\sqrt{\phi_n^{\top}(\Lambda^{p})^{-1}\phi_n}\right\},
\qquad \phi_n=\phi(x_n,p_n),
\quad n=T_0+1,\ldots,T.
\]
The states and releases in this row are defined below. The setting is a single product with unlimited inventories (pp.~2--3).

\paragraph{Printed price notation.}
Algorithm 1, line 10, defines $\phi_n$ using $p_n$, not the optimization variable $p$. This printed dependence is retained; it is not replaced by $\phi(x_n,p)$.

\subsection*{Demand, information, and fixed model assumptions}
We retain the abstract exponential-family model of Section 3 (pp.~9--10), without specializing to either of its illustrative likelihood examples. At periods $t=1,\ldots,T$, $x_t$ is the arriving customer's personal information, $p_t$ is the offered price, and $y_t\in\mathbb R$ is realized demand. The known feature map and unknown fixed model satisfy
\[
\phi_t=\phi(x_t,p_t)\in\mathbb R^d,
\qquad \theta^*\in\mathbb R^d.
\]
No particular dimension or coordinate domain for $x_t$ is specified in this abstract formulation. The distribution-family components $\zeta,m(\cdot),h(\cdot)$ remain abstract. The printed demand model is
\begin{equation}
\Pr[y_t=y\mid p_t,x_t,\theta^*]
=\exp\!\left\{\zeta\left(y\phi_t^{\top}\theta^*
-m(\phi_t^{\top}\theta^*)\right)+h(y)\right\}.
\tag{1}
\end{equation}
Its conditional mean and noise representation are
\[
f(\phi_t^{\top}\theta^*):=m'(\phi_t^{\top}\theta^*)
=\mathbb E[y_t\mid p_t,x_t,\theta^*],
\]
\begin{equation}
y_t=f(\phi_t^{\top}\theta^*)+\xi_t.
\tag{2}
\end{equation}
The paragraph following (2) states that $\phi_t=\phi(x_t,p_t)$ and $\xi_t$ are independent random variables satisfying
\[
\mathbb E[\xi_t\mid p_t,x_t]=0.
\]
The history and subsequent conditional centering are
\[
\mathcal F_t=\{(x_\tau,y_\tau,p_\tau)\}_{\tau=1}^{t},
\qquad
\mathbb E[\xi_t\mid x_t,p_t,\mathcal F_{t-1}]=0.
\]
In the general setting used here, $x_1,\ldots,x_T$ are arbitrarily chosen before pricing starts (the oblivious-adversary setting). The policy chooses $p_t$ conditioned on $\mathcal F_{t-1}$ and $x_t$, after which demand is realized according to (2).

The source imposes the following model assumptions throughout the paper. They concern fixed inputs and the demand process, not additional optimization constraints on the estimator:
\begin{align*}
& B_Y<\infty,\qquad |y_t|\le B_Y
&&\text{for every period }t\text{ in every database }D;\\
&\|\phi(x,p)\|_2\le1,\qquad \|\theta^*\|_2\le1
&&\text{for all }x,p;\\
&\mathbb E[\xi_t\mid x_t,p_t,\mathcal F_{t-1}]=0,\\
&\text{there exists }s<\infty\text{ such that }
\mathbb E[e^{\lambda\xi_t}\mid x_t,p_t,\mathcal F_{t-1}]
\le e^{\lambda^2s^2/2}
&&\text{for all }\lambda\in\mathbb R;\\
&f=m':\mathbb R\longrightarrow[0,1],
\qquad f\text{ continuously differentiable and strictly increasing};\\
&1\le K<\infty,\qquad K^{-1}\le f'(z)\le K
&&\text{for all }|z|\le2;\\
&1\le G<\infty,\qquad G^{-1}\le\zeta\le G.
\end{align*}
Here $B_Y,s,K,G$ are model parameters. In the likelihood notation used by Algorithm 3, the family in (1), evaluated at the candidate parameter $\theta$, is
\[
p(y_t\mid\phi_t,\theta)
=\exp\!\left\{\zeta\left(y_t\phi_t^{\top}\theta
-m(\phi_t^{\top}\theta)\right)+h(y_t)\right\}.
\]
The likelihood symbol $p(\,\cdot\mid\cdot\,)$ is distinct from the scalar price $p$. This uses the Section 3 family, rather than the differently written recap on p.~25.

\subsection*{Algorithm 1 inputs, initialization, and sequential updates}
The fixed algorithmic inputs on p.~20 are
\[
\varepsilon_1,\delta_1,\varepsilon_2,\delta_2>0,
\qquad \rho\ge1,\qquad \gamma>0,
\]
together with the number of pure-exploration periods $T_0$ and the maximum number of \textsc{PrivateMLE} calls $D_\infty$. The output is $p_1,p_2,\ldots,p_T$. The quantities $\widehat\theta^p$, $\Lambda^p$, and $D_{\mathrm{MLE}}$ are maintained algorithmic states, not fixed inputs; the superscript $p$ marks privatized quantities.

Initialize, exactly as in line 3,
\[
\delta'_2=\frac{\delta_2}{2D_\infty},
\qquad
\varepsilon'_2\leftarrow
\frac{\varepsilon_2}{2\sqrt{2D_\infty\ln(1/\delta'_2)}},
\qquad
\Lambda^p=\rho I_d,
\qquad \widehat\theta^p=0,
\qquad D_{\mathrm{MLE}}=0.
\]
For the first $T_0$ periods, offer prices uniformly at random:
\[
p_t\sim U[0,1],\qquad t=1,\ldots,T_0.
\]
For each $n=T_0+1,\ldots,T$, first obtain
\[
\Sigma^p_{\mathrm{used},n}\leftarrow R_{n-1},\quad R_j:=\text{the output of iteration }j\text{ of the single horizon-}T\text{ covariance tree},
\qquad \Lambda_n^p=\Sigma^p_{\mathrm{used},n}+\rho I_d.
\]
If
\[
\det(\Lambda_n^p)>2\det(\Lambda^p)
\quad\text{and}\quad D_{\mathrm{MLE}}<D_\infty,
\]
perform the assignments
\[
\widehat\theta^p\leftarrow
\textsc{PrivateMLE}(n,\rho,\varepsilon'_2,\delta'_2),
\qquad \Lambda^p\leftarrow\Lambda_n^p,
\qquad D_{\mathrm{MLE}}\leftarrow D_{\mathrm{MLE}}+1.
\]
Otherwise retain these states. Then offer the price given by the price-selection row above. Here $I_d$ is the $d$-dimensional identity matrix, and the covariance-related matrices have dimension $d\times d$.

Remark 4 (pp.~20--21) permits omitting forced exploration, with $T_0=0$. No theorem-specific tuning or stochastic-context assumption is imposed on the parameterized Algorithm 1 reproduced here.

\subsection*{Algorithm 2: \textsc{PrivateCov}}
Algorithm 2 (p.~21) has the signature
\[
\textsc{PrivateCov}(T,\varepsilon,\delta)
\quad\text{returns}\quad\Sigma_1^p,\ldots,\Sigma_{T-1}^p.
\]
Its local parameters and initial states are
\begin{align*}
\delta'&\leftarrow\frac{\delta}{2\lceil\log_2T\rceil},
&\varepsilon'&\leftarrow
\frac{\varepsilon}{2\lceil\log_2T\rceil\ln(1/\delta')},\\
\sigma_{\varepsilon',\delta'}^2
&=\frac{2\ln(1.25/\delta')}{(\varepsilon')^2},
&m&=\lceil\log_2T\rceil,\\
\Sigma(\ell)&=\widehat\Sigma(\ell)=0,
&&\ell=0,\ldots,m-1.
\end{align*}
Here the local integer $m$ is the binary-tree depth, distinct from the distribution-family function $m(\cdot)$.

For $n=1,2,\ldots,T-1$, express $n$ in binary and identify its least significant nonzero bit:
\[
n=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}b_n(\ell)2^\ell,
\qquad b_n(\ell)\in\{0,1\},
\qquad \ell_n\leftarrow\min\{\ell:b_n(\ell)=1\}.
\]
Update and reset in the printed order:
\[
\Sigma(\ell_n)\leftarrow
\phi_n\phi_n^{\top}+\sum_{\ell<\ell_n}\Sigma(\ell),
\qquad
\Sigma(\ell)\leftarrow\widehat\Sigma(\ell)\leftarrow0
\quad\text{for all }\ell<\ell_n.
\]
Calibrate noise and release:
\[
\widehat\Sigma(\ell_n)\leftarrow\Sigma(\ell_n)+W^n,
\qquad
W^n_{ij}=W^n_{ji}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}
\mathcal N(0,\sigma_{\varepsilon',\delta'}^2),
\]
\[
\Sigma_n^p=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}b_n(\ell)\widehat\Sigma(\ell).
\]
The source describes $W^n$ as a symmetric random Gaussian matrix; its displayed symmetry and i.i.d. notation are retained without adding a sampling convention. Section 6.1 (p.~22) defines the corresponding unperturbed sample covariance by
\[
\Sigma_n=\sum_{t=1}^{n}\phi_t\phi_t^{\top},
\qquad n=1,\ldots,T-1.
\]

\paragraph{Printed release-time conflict.}
Section 6.1 on p.~21 states that
$\textsc{PrivateCov}(n,\varepsilon,\delta)$ is the $\Sigma_n^p$ matrix released at the end of iteration $n-1$. Algorithm 2 instead labels its release at iteration $n$ by $\Sigma_n^p$ and incorporates $\phi_n\phi_n^{\top}$ in that iteration. Algorithm 1 calls $\textsc{PrivateCov}(n,\varepsilon_1,\delta_1)$ before selecting $p_n$, whereas Section 5.1 requires releasers used at time $n$ to access only earlier observations. The printed conflict is preserved here as provenance. The selected version uses the Section 6.1 sentence: the covariance available for decision $n$ is the one released at the end of iteration $n-1$. The tree retains its printed iteration indices and uses every historical row once. In particular, Algorithm 2 does not specify an iteration-zero release for the permitted $T_0=0$ case.

\subsection*{Algorithm 3: \textsc{PrivateMLE}}
At a requested call,
\[
\textsc{PrivateMLE}(n,\rho,\varepsilon,\delta)
\quad\text{returns}\quad\widehat\theta_n^p.
\]
Algorithm 1 supplies $(\varepsilon,\delta)=(\varepsilon'_2,\delta'_2)$. Algorithm 3 (p.~24) first makes the assignments
\[
B_1\leftarrow(B_Y+1)G,
\qquad B_2\leftarrow KG,
\qquad \rho\leftarrow\max\{\rho,2B_2/\varepsilon\},
\]
\[
\nu_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{2}\leftarrow
\frac{B_1^2\bigl(8\ln(2/\delta)+4\varepsilon\bigr)}{\varepsilon^2},
\qquad
w\sim\mathcal N(0,\nu_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{2}I_d).
\]
It then returns
\[
\widehat\theta_n^p
=\arg\min_{\|\theta\|_2\le2}
\left\{\left(\sum_{t<n}-\ln p(y_t\mid\phi_t,\theta)\right)
+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\theta\|_2^2+w^{\top}\theta\right\}.
\]
Here $\theta\in\mathbb R^d$ is the estimation variable, $w\in\mathbb R^d$ is the sampled perturbation, and $t<n$ ranges over the observed periods preceding the call. The radius-two estimation constraint is distinct from the fixed-model assumption $\|\theta^*\|_2\le1$. The reassignment of $\rho$ is reproduced within Algorithm 3; no additional reassignment of Algorithm 1's covariance states is inserted.

\paragraph{Printed privacy-allocation discrepancy.}
Algorithm 1's allocation above retains the factor $2$ outside the square root in the denominator of $\varepsilon'_2$. Corollary 3 on p.~23 omits that factor in its stated allocation. Its alternate allocation is not substituted into Algorithm 1.

\subsection*{Privacy definition attached to the policy}
For the privacy definition (pp.~12--14), write
\[
D=\{o_t\}_{t=1}^{T},\qquad o_t=(x_t,y_t),
\qquad D'=\{o'_t\}_{t=1}^{T}.
\]
Neighboring databases differing at time $t$ satisfy
\[
o_t\ne o'_t,\qquad o_\tau=o'_\tau\quad\text{for all }\tau\ne t.
\]
For $\varepsilon,\delta>0$, anticipating $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-differential privacy requires, for every such pair and every measurable set $\mathcal P_{>t}$,
\begin{equation}
\Pr[p_{t+1},\ldots,p_T\in\mathcal P_{>t}\mid\pi,D]
\le e^{\varepsilon}
\Pr[p_{t+1},\ldots,p_T\in\mathcal P_{>t}\mid\pi,D']+\delta.
\tag{3}
\end{equation}
The private releasers use historical data only, and the price optimizer uses their outputs and the current $x_t$, not historical sensitive data directly (pp.~17--18). The claimed composite privacy levels are
\[
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2,
\qquad \delta=\delta_1+\delta_2.
\]
This privacy definition is model-free; the demand assumptions above are used for utility analysis, not to define privacy. Corollary 4 on p.~23 calls the resulting price sequence ``differentially private'' without the word ``anticipating,'' although its cited Proposition 2 concludes anticipating differential privacy. The definition retained here is (3), not the stronger full-price-sequence requirement.

\paragraph{Source scope.}
The operative formulation is the parameterized Algorithm 1 and its Algorithms 2--3 dependencies, with the Section 3 demand family. Gaussian and logistic examples, theorem-specific parameter choices, regret-analysis inequalities, and simulation settings are not additional operative constraints. The paper refers proofs to an online supplement; the published electronic companion, EC.2, uses the history of summaries through period $t-1$ in the price at $t$. EC.3.1 uses a single horizon-based tree depth. The supplied page images for pp.~3, 20, 21, and 24 support the objective and algorithm rows reproduced above.


\paragraph{Selection on the archived benchmark inputs.}
For the ten supplied scalar inputs, \(T_0\geq2\) and a release exists before every
adaptive decision including the last. The implementation chooses the largest
maximizer in the printed price selector. Each case has price-independent
\(\phi(x,p)=(1+x)/2\) and the logistic mean. At initialization the scalar
accepted matrix is positive; the strict doubling gate preserves positivity.
Consequently the capped optimistic score is nondecreasing in the offered
price on each realized history, and price one is a maximizing choice.
Uniform exploratory offers remain mandatory. These instance facts do not
specify price-dependent or multidimensional cases. Only future public offers
enter the anticipating-privacy statement. The archived seeded diagnostic
paths, noisy releases, purchases and estimates support reproduction and are
not a public release with an asserted privacy guarantee.
