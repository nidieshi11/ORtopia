The decision variables are the affine coefficients 𝜃t and V t,i for t = 1,…,τ and i = 1,…,m. No sign restrictions on these decision variables are imposed in the printed (D1) block. The states x and offer sets S index its inequalities; they are not additional decision variables of this linear program.

Fixed inputs and indexed feasible sets

There are m flight legs and n products. A product is a flight-itinerary and fare-class combination. The initial capacity vector and product set are

c = (c1,…,cm),N = {1,…,n},

where ci is the capacity of leg i. The fare for product j is fj. The consumption matrix is the m × n matrix

A ≡ (aij).

Its entry aij is the integer amount of resource i required by a class-j customer. Its ith row Ai is the incidence vector for leg i, and its jth column Aj is the incidence vector for product j. Throughout, i indexes legs, j indexes products, and t indexes time; every sum over i in (5)–(7) runs over i = 1,…,m.

There are τ discrete periods, counted forward, with τ the last period. In each period there is one customer arrival with probability λ, and no customer arrival with probability 1 − λ. Conditional on an arrival and an offer set S ⊆ N, the customer chooses product j ∈ S with probability Pj(S) and makes no purchase with probability

P0(S) = 1 −∑ j∈SPj(S),S ⊆ N.

The choice probabilities are fixed inputs; no particular choice model is imposed in this formulation. The set S = ∅ means that no product is offered and is included among the possible offer sets.

At the beginning of a period, x is the m-vector of unsold seats. The state space and feasible-product set are

X = {0,…,c1}×⋯ ×{0,…,cm},N(x) = {j ∈ N : x ≥ Aj},x ∈ X.

Thus N(x) consists of products that can be offered at state x; the vector inequality requires sufficient capacity on each leg. The initial state is c. Every subset S ⊆ N(x), including the empty set, indexes a constraint in (7).

Affine value-function construction

Let vt(x) be the maximum total expected revenue over periods t,…,τ when the state at the beginning of period t is x. The selected approximation is

vt(x) ≈ 𝜃t + ∑ iV t,ixi,t = 1,…,τ,x ∈ X. 		(5)

Here V t,i estimates the marginal value of a seat on flight i in period t, and 𝜃t is a constant offset.

The inherited value-function definition is the Bellman equation

vt(x) = max ⁡ S⊆N(x) {∑ j∈SλPj(S)(fj + vt+1(x − Aj)) + (λP 0(S) + 1 − λ)vt+1(x)} = max ⁡ S⊆N(x) {∑ j∈SλPj(S)[fj −(vt+1(x) − vt+1(x − Aj))]} + v t+1(x), ∀ ⁡t = 1,…,τ,x ∈ X, 		(1)

with boundary conditions

vτ+1(x) = 0∀ ⁡x ∈ X.

Equation (1) defines the exact value function being approximated, not additional constraints on the affine coefficients. The paper obtains (D1) by substituting (5) into the Bellman inequalities of (D0); (7) is the resulting complete inequality family, and the fixed terminal coefficients above implement the terminal value.
