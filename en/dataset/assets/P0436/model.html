Index sets, fixed data, and decision roles

The distribution network is the graph (N,E): N is the set of buses, indexed by i, and E is the set of lines, indexed by ij for the line between buses i and j. The distribution network is operated radially. The generator set is G, indexed by g, with Gi ⊆G the generators at bus i and ig ∈N the location of generator g. The battery set is B, indexed by b, with Bi ⊆B the batteries at bus i and ib ∈N the location of battery b. The time-period set is T = {1,…,T}; Ω is the disruption-scenario set; and Lb is the set of pieces of battery b’s efficiency curve, indexed by ℓ in the constraints.

The fixed parameters are:

  • ditp,ditq: deterministic active and reactive demand throughout the horizon (MW, MVAr).

  • Rij,Xij: line resistance and reactance (kΩ); Wij: apparent-power thermal limit (MVA).

  • V̲i,V ¯i: lower and upper bounds on squared voltage ((kV)2).

  • s̲gp,s¯gp,s̲gq,s¯gq: lower and upper active and reactive generation bounds (MW, MVAr); RgU,RgD: up and down ramp rates (MW/h).

  • Īb: maximum battery energy-storage capacity (MWh); ūb: upper bound on apparent-power output capacity (MVA); αbℓ,βbℓ: slope and intercept of efficiency piece ℓ; Δt: time step (h).

  • cbB: battery apparent-power capacity cost ($/MVA); cl: load-mismatch penalty ($/MW, $/MVAr); cg,1,cg,2: linear and quadratic active-generation cost coefficients ($/MW, $/(MW)2).

  • τω: recovery time in time steps for scenario ω; tD: observed disruption time; Bgtω,Bijtω: fixed disruption indicators; ptω: finite-support disruption probability mass; M: a large number, left abstract by the source.

The source describes Īb as estimated from implied bounds using worst-case values attained by the upper bound on apparent-power output capacity, but supplies no general formula for that estimate.

The continuous operating decisions are sgtp,sgtq (active/reactive generation), V it (squared voltage), Pijt,Qijt (active/reactive line flow), wbt (battery state of charge), zbtp,zbtq (actual battery active/reactive output), ybt (active output before losses), and Litp+,Litq+ and Litp−,Litq− (load shedding and load compensation, respectively). The investment decision ub is apparent-power output capacity, not energy capacity. Its cost is incurred before the first time period, and (2t) fixes it at its inherited value in every continuation problem. The hats in (2r)–(2t) denote fixed entering-state data for that conditional problem. The variable bounds and nonnegativity restrictions are exactly those displayed above; no additional sign restrictions are imposed on line flows, y, or battery outputs.

A symbol without a subscript denotes a vector. A dot in a subscript collects the corresponding component indices, for example

s⋅,t−1p = {s g,t−1p∣g ∈G}.

The nominal plan in (1) is implemented only until the first disruption. A plan in (2) is implemented only until the next disruption. The linking state comprises active generation, battery state of charge, and installed battery apparent-power capacity.

A disruption is the failure of a single line or generator. Recovery lasts the scenario-dependent time τω, during which there is no additional disruption. The indicator parameters equal one for a failed component and zero otherwise. In particular, the printed line-failure rule is

Bijt′ω = 1,t′ = t D,…,tD + τω,

when line ij is the disrupted component; all other line and generator indicators are zero during those periods. The source similarly uses Bgtω = 1 to enforce zero active/reactive generation and relax ramping for a failed generator. The recovery interval includes both printed endpoints. The next disruption can occur only after tD + τω.

Given the earliest possible disruption time t′, ptω is the probability of observing scenario ω at time t′ + t, with waiting-time support t = 1,…,T. The waiting time is conditionally independent of the history given t′. In (1), the first disruption is at 1 + t. The distribution is kept abstract; the exponential waiting-time distribution, uniform component probabilities, common recovery times, and numerical battery data used in the experiments are not imposed here.

Printed omissions and official implementation distinctions

The printed (1j) uses wb0, but (1m) bounds only t ∈T and the paper does not supply an initial-storage equality in model (1) or its defining prose. The official implementation instead bounds storage at time zero and fixes it to input battery-initial-state data. Neither that equality nor an instance-specific initial value is imported here.

The printed balance rows retain ∑ ⁡ ij∈E for each bus i. The paper does not explicitly specify a reverse-arc convention or opposite-direction flow equalities. The official implementation constructs directed reverse arcs and imposes opposite-flow equalities; those additional implementation rows are not added to the printed formulation.

The official implementation uses a functional-component status parameter opposite to the paper’s failure-indicator convention. It conditionally imposes functional-line voltage equations and functional-generator ramp rows instead of the printed big-M rows. Here (2d)–(2j) retain the paper’s indicators and coefficients. No numerical choice of M is introduced.

The implementation’s conditional objective builder takes a supplied recovery duration and omits continuation terms whose times exceed T. This differs from the repeated-ω scope printed in (2a) and avoids evaluating states beyond the horizon. These implementation choices are not used to repair the printed recursion or to strengthen the conditional terminal statement A.3.
