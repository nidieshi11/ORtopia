Fixed inputs, information, and decision roles

Demand D ≥ 0 and selling price Ps ≥ 0 are exogenous random quantities. Both parties have the same prescribed first and second moments. The supplier’s fixed unit cost satisfies f ≥ 0. The retailer chooses the profit share in stage 1, the supplier chooses the unit wholesale price w ≥ 0 in stage 2, and the retailer chooses capacity Q ≥ 0 in stage 3. The wholesale payment is linear:

c(Q) = wQ.

Unused capacity has zero salvage value. Before taking the worst case over distributions, the decentralized profit and the parties’ shares are

Π = EPs,D [Ps min ⁡ (Q,D)] − wQ. 		(2)
ΠR := (1 − γ)Π,ΠS := γΠ + (w − f)Q.

Here γ is the supplier’s share and 1 − γ is the retailer’s share; α in (21) is the transformed wholesale-price variable, not a profit share.

The given moments matrix and its distributional ambiguity set are

Σ = ( E(Ps2) E(PsD) E(Ps) E(PsD) E(D2) E(D) ),
Φ(Σ0) = {(Ps,D);Ps,D ≥ 0,Ps,D have moments matrix Σ = Σ0} .

The matrix Σ0 is fixed data. All five moments in these formulas take their values from its entries. As stated on page 10, a moments matrix of nonnegative Ps,D is positive semidefinite and has nonnegative entries:

Σ ≽ 0,Σ ≥ 0.

The latter inequality is entrywise. The notation σ(D) denotes the standard deviation of demand.

Supplier response retained in the final formulation

This is the supplier response used by the selected version, replacing the earlier unrestricted response over w ≥ 0. For w > wUB, Section 5 prints ΠS = 0.

The final response analysis treats γ ∈ [0,1]. In particular,

w(1) = f,w(γ) > ffor all γ ∈ [0,1).

At γ = 1, the retailer is indifferent because its profit share is zero. Endnote 3 specifies the Strong Stackelberg Equilibrium convention: among multiple optimal retailer decisions, the retailer chooses Q to maximize the supplier’s payoff.
