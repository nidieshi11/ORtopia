\section*{P2: Menu of linear contracts with endogenous information acquisition}

\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\Pi(\alpha(\cdot),\beta(\cdot))
={}&E_{\mu_{ps}}[\mu_{ps}+\alpha(\mu_{ps})]
-E_{\mu_{ps}}[\alpha^2(\mu_{ps})/2]
-\Gamma(\sigma)\\
&-\rho\sqrt{\sigma_\theta^2\sigma^2+\sigma_\varepsilon^2}
-\widehat{\pi}(\sigma).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\text{(P2).}\qquad
&\max_{\alpha(\cdot)\geq 0,\,\beta(\cdot)}
\quad \Pi(\alpha(\cdot),\beta(\cdot))\\
&\text{s.t.}\quad
\pi(\mu_{ps})\geq\pi(\mu_{ps},\widehat{\mu}_{ps}),
\quad\forall\mu_{ps},\widehat{\mu}_{ps}
&&\text{(IC1)}\\
&\qquad\sigma=\arg\max_{\sigma'\in(0,1]}\widehat{\pi}(\sigma')
&&\text{(IC2)}\\
&\qquad\widehat{\pi}(\sigma)\geq 0.
&&\text{(IR)}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

The producer chooses the commission function $\alpha(\cdot)$ and fixed-transfer function $\beta(\cdot)$. The seller chooses the information precision $\sigma$, the report $\widehat{\mu}_{ps}$, and subsequently the sales effort $a$. Thus $\sigma$ in the producer's objective is induced by (IC2), rather than an additional maximization variable of (P2). Condition (IC1) is global reporting incentive compatibility; (IC2) is the global information-acquisition choice; (IR) is ex ante participation with reservation expected net income normalized to zero. The equality to $\arg\max$ in (IC2) is retained as printed; no tie-breaking rule is added.

\subsection*{Contract and seller-profit definitions}

For a reported posterior mean, the compensation schedule is
\begin{equation*}
w(x,\mu_{ps})=\alpha(\mu_{ps})x+\beta(\mu_{ps}),
\qquad \alpha(\mu_{ps})\geq 0.
\end{equation*}
The menu is $\{\alpha(\cdot),\beta(\cdot)\}$. The fixed transfer $\beta(\mu_{ps})$ has no sign restriction in (P2). The report $\widehat{\mu}_{ps}$ and actual type $\mu_{ps}$ range over posterior-mean values, with no truncation of the normal type space stated in the model. The subscript $ps$ denotes the posterior, not a component index.

Given precision $\sigma$, type $\mu_{ps}$, and effort $a$, demand has the conditional distribution
\begin{equation*}
x\mid\mu_{ps}\sim N\!\left(\mu_{ps}+a,
\sigma_\theta^2\sigma^2+\sigma_\varepsilon^2\right).
\end{equation*}
If the seller reports $\widehat{\mu}_{ps}$, his expected compensation is
\begin{equation*}
\alpha(\widehat{\mu}_{ps})(\mu_{ps}+a)+\beta(\widehat{\mu}_{ps}).
\end{equation*}
His sales-effort cost and optimized profit, excluding the sunk information-acquisition cost, are
\begin{gather*}
V(a)=a^2/2,\qquad a\geq 0,\\
\pi(\mu_{ps},\widehat{\mu}_{ps})
=\max_{a\geq 0}
\left[\alpha(\widehat{\mu}_{ps})(\mu_{ps}+a)
+\beta(\widehat{\mu}_{ps})-a^2/2\right].
\end{gather*}
Truthful profit and truthful sales effort are defined by
\begin{gather*}
\pi(\mu_{ps})\equiv\pi(\mu_{ps},\mu_{ps}),\\
a(\mu_{ps})=\alpha(\mu_{ps}).
\end{gather*}
The same truthful-profit definition is written explicitly in the appendix as
\begin{equation*}
\pi(\mu_{ps})
=\alpha(\mu_{ps})\left(\mu_{ps}+\frac{\alpha(\mu_{ps})}{2}\right)
+\beta(\mu_{ps}).
\end{equation*}
The seller's expected profit before information acquisition is
\begin{equation*}
\widehat{\pi}(\sigma)=E_{\mu_{ps}}[\pi(\mu_{ps})]-\Gamma(\sigma),
\qquad
\mu_{ps}\sim N\!\left(\mu_\theta,\sigma_\theta^2(1-\sigma^2)\right).
\end{equation*}
In evaluating $\widehat{\pi}(\sigma')$ in (IC2), the distribution of $\mu_{ps}$ is correspondingly the distribution with precision $\sigma'$. The menu remains fixed during this seller choice.

\subsection*{Demand, signal, and information-acquisition primitives}

The producer and seller are risk neutral. The fixed demand primitives are the prior mean $\mu_\theta$, prior variance $\sigma_\theta^2$, and demand-noise variance $\sigma_\varepsilon^2$. Demand and the private signal are
\begin{gather*}
x=\theta+a+\varepsilon,\\
\theta\sim N(\mu_\theta,\sigma_\theta^2),
\qquad \varepsilon\sim N(0,\sigma_\varepsilon^2),\\
s=\theta+\eta,
\qquad \eta\sim N(0,\sigma_\eta^2),\\
\sigma^2\equiv\frac{\sigma_\eta^2}{\sigma_\eta^2+\sigma_\theta^2}.
\end{gather*}
The random variables $\theta$ and $\varepsilon$ are independent; $\eta$ is independent of both $\theta$ and $\varepsilon$. The seller can select $\sigma_\eta$, or equivalently $\sigma$, through information-acquisition effort. A larger $\sigma_\eta$ means less informative market information.

The original posterior definitions are
\begin{equation}
\theta\mid s\sim N(\mu_{ps},\sigma_{ps}^2),
\tag{1}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
(\mu_{ps},\sigma_{ps}^2)
\equiv\left(\sigma^2\mu_\theta+(1-\sigma^2)s,
\sigma_\theta^2\sigma^2\right).
\tag{2}
\end{equation}
The fixed information-acquisition cost function is left abstract, subject to the printed premises
\begin{gather*}
\sigma\in(0,1],\qquad \Gamma(1)=0,\\
\Gamma'(\sigma)<0,\qquad\Gamma''(\sigma)>0,
\qquad\sigma\in(0,1].
\end{gather*}
The paper describes $\sigma=0$ as revealing the exact market condition, but excludes that value from the decision domain. It describes the posterior as approaching the prior as $\sigma\to1$, so that the signal contains no useful information, while including $1$ in the decision domain. These printed boundary statements and the ratio defining $\sigma^2$ are retained without an additional endpoint construction for the signal.

\subsection*{Operations costs and the eliminated production decision}

The fixed unit production cost is $c$, the emergency-production unit cost is $c'$, and the unit salvage value is $v$. The sales price is fixed at $1+c$, normalizing the profit margin to one. The input restrictions are
\begin{equation*}
v<c<c'<1+c.
\end{equation*}
If demand exceeds the initial production quantity $q$, emergency production satisfies the excess demand; otherwise leftover inventory is salvaged. The mismatch-loss function and producer profit before seller compensation are
\begin{gather*}
L(z)=(c-v)\max\{0,z\}+(c'-c)\max\{0,-z\},\\
x-L(q-x).
\end{gather*}
The operations-cost coefficient used in (P2) is
\begin{equation*}
\rho=(c'-v)\phi\!\left(\Phi^{-1}\!\left(\frac{c'-c}{c'-v}\right)\right),
\end{equation*}
where $\phi(\cdot)$ is the standard normal density and $\Phi^{-1}(\cdot)$ is the inverse standard normal distribution function.

The conditional production calculation referenced by (P2) is printed in terms of a signal-contingent effort $a(s)$:
\begin{gather*}
x\mid s\sim N\!\left(\mu_{ps}+a(s),
\sigma_\theta^2\sigma^2+\sigma_\varepsilon^2\right),\\
q(s)=\mu_{ps}+a(s)
+\sqrt{\sigma_\theta^2\sigma^2+\sigma_\varepsilon^2}
\,\Phi^{-1}\!\left(\frac{c'-c}{c'-v}\right),\\
E[L(q(s)-x)\mid s]
=\rho\sqrt{\sigma_\theta^2\sigma^2+\sigma_\varepsilon^2}.
\end{gather*}
Under the truthful menu, the producer's information at the production decision is
\begin{equation*}
I=\{\mu_{ps},\sigma\},
\end{equation*}
where precision is inferred from the seller's optimization and effort is given by $a(\mu_{ps})=\alpha(\mu_{ps})$. The paper therefore uses the same minimum expected mismatch cost
\begin{equation*}
\rho\sqrt{\sigma_\theta^2\sigma^2+\sigma_\varepsilon^2}
\end{equation*}
in (P2). Production has already been optimized out; the displayed production calculation is not an additional optimization problem or an additional constraint on the menu.

\subsection*{Contract-stage assumptions and scope}

The producer offers the menu before information acquisition. Rejection ends the game. Following acceptance, the seller chooses information precision, obtains the private signal, and chooses a contract by reporting his posterior mean. The producer then chooses initial production. The seller subsequently exerts sales effort, demand is realized, emergency production or salvage occurs, and compensation is paid. Both efforts and the private signal are unobservable to the producer. In this selected model the seller cannot renege after acceptance, regardless of the realized signal; no interim participation constraint is imposed.
