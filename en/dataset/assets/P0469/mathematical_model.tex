\section*{Robust dynamic assortment optimization with a known contamination upper bound}

\subsection*{Objective and admissibility}
The selected performance objective is to minimize the Typically-Optimal-Typically-Evaluated (TOTE) regret by an admissible sequential assortment policy:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Regret}^{\mathrm{TOTE}}(T)
:=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T} R(S^*;\{v\})-R(S_t;\{v\})\right].
\tag{2}
\end{equation}
The subject-to conditions on the retailer's decisions are
\[
S_t\subseteq[N],\qquad |S_t|\le K,\qquad
S_t=\mathfrak{P}_t\bigl(\{S_\tau,i_\tau\}_{\tau\le t-1}\bigr),
\qquad t=1,2,\ldots,T,
\]
where the $T$ random functions $\mathfrak{P}_1,\ldots,\mathfrak{P}_T$ constitute an admissible policy as specified below. The benchmark is
\[
S^*=\arg\max_{S\subseteq[N],\,|S|\le K}R(S;\{v\}).
\]
TOTE evaluates both assortments using the typical-customer model even at times when the arriving customer is an outlier. This is the performance criterion selected by the paper for its upper-bound analysis, rather than the alternative best-in-hindsight criterion.

\subsection*{Inputs, choice model, and information protocol}
There are $N$ items and $T$ customer-arrival periods. The capacity parameter is $K$. The item parameters satisfy
\[
r_i\in[0,1]\quad\text{(known revenue)},\qquad
v_i\in[0,1]\quad\text{(unknown utility)},\qquad i\in[N].
\]
The true contamination parameter and the known upper bound have distinct roles:
\[
\varepsilon\in[0,1),\qquad \overline{\varepsilon}\ge\varepsilon,
\]
with $\overline{\varepsilon}$ supplied to Algorithm 1. The selected utility normalization is $v_i\in[0,1]$, not the optional larger-upper-bound variant discussed on page 7.

At time $t$, the observed purchase satisfies
\[
i_t\in S_t\cup\{0\},\qquad t=1,\ldots,T.
\]
A purchase of item $i_t\in S_t$ produces revenue $r_{i_t}$; $i_t=0$ denotes no purchase and produces no revenue. For a typical customer,
\begin{equation}
\Pr[i_t=i\mid S_t]
=\frac{v_i}{v_0+\sum_{j\in S_t}v_j},\qquad v_0=1,
\tag{1}
\end{equation}
for purchase outcomes $i\in S_t\cup\{0\}$. Thus no purchase is a most probable outcome for a typical customer. An outlier instead purchases according to an arbitrary unknown distribution $Q_t$, marginalized on $S_t\cup\{0\}$; $Q_t$ may change with $t$.

The source's assumptions (A1)--(A2) and Definition 1 specify the adversary and the information available to the retailer. Let
\[
\phi_t\in\{0,1\},\qquad t=1,2,\ldots,T,
\]
where $\phi_t=1$ means an outlier and $\phi_t=0$ means a typical customer. Bounded contamination means
\[
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\phi_t\le\varepsilon T. \tag{A1}
\]
The adaptive adversary consists of $T$ arbitrary measurable functions $\mathfrak{A}_1,\ldots,\mathfrak{A}_T$ with
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}&=\{\phi_\tau,Q_\tau,S_\tau,i_\tau\}_{\tau\le t-1},\\
\mathfrak{A}_t &: \{\phi_\tau,Q_\tau,S_\tau,i_\tau\}_{\tau\le t-1}
\longmapsto(\phi_t,Q_t),\qquad t=1,\ldots,T.
\end{aligned}
\tag{A2 / Definition 1}
\]
Thus outlier arrivals and choice models can depend on previous customers, offered assortments, and past purchasing activity. The retailer's policy has the smaller information history
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{G}_{t-1}&=\{S_\tau,i_\tau\}_{\tau\le t-1},\\
\mathfrak{P}_t &: \{S_\tau,i_\tau\}_{\tau\le t-1}\longmapsto S_t,
\qquad S_t\subseteq[N],\quad |S_t|\le K,
\qquad t=1,\ldots,T.
\end{aligned}
\]
If $\phi_t=0$, $i_t$ is realized according to (1), conditioned on $S_t$; if $\phi_t=1$, it is realized according to $Q_t$. The retailer is not given the outlier indicators or the outlier distributions.

The plain symbol $P_t$ denotes the customer's choice model, not the policy function $\mathfrak{P}_t$:
\[
P_t=\begin{cases}
\{v\},&\phi_t=0,\\
Q_t,&\phi_t=1.
\end{cases}
\]
The function $R(S;P)$ denotes the expected revenue from offering $S$ under choice model $P$. For the typical model and for estimated utilities, respectively,
\[
R(S;\{v\})=R(S;v)=\frac{\sum_{i\in S}r_iv_i}{1+\sum_{i\in S}v_i},
\qquad
R(S;\widehat v)=\frac{\sum_{i\in S}r_i\widehat v_i}{1+\sum_{i\in S}\widehat v_i}.
\]
The second expression is the defining remark in Algorithm 1; the true-utility expression also appears in the supplementary proof of Lemma 2 (physical page 31).

\subsection*{Known-upper-bound construction: Algorithm 1}
The inputs are $T$, $\overline\varepsilon$, $\{r_i\}$, and $K$; the output is the sequence $\{S_t\}_{t=1}^{T}$. Initialize
\[
\widehat v^{(0)}\equiv1,\qquad
\widehat\Delta_{\overline\varepsilon}(0)=1,\qquad
\mathcal A^{(0)}=[N],\qquad
T_0=128(K+1)^2N\ln T.
\]
For epochs $\tau=0,1,2,\ldots$, perform the following rows in their stated order:
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_\tau^{(i)}
&=\arg\max_{S\subseteq\mathcal A^{(\tau)},\,|S|\le K,\,i\in S}
 R(S;\widehat v^{(\tau)}),
&&i\in\mathcal A^{(\tau)},\\
\gamma^{(\tau)}
&=\max_{i\in\mathcal A^{(\tau)}}R(S_\tau^{(i)};\widehat v^{(\tau)}),\\
\mathcal A^{(\tau+1)}
&=\left\{i\in\mathcal A^{(\tau)}:
R(S_\tau^{(i)};\widehat v^{(\tau)})
+2\widehat\Delta_{\overline\varepsilon}(\tau)\ge\gamma^{(\tau)}\right\},\\
n_i&=0,\qquad n_0(i)=0,
&&i\in\mathcal A^{(\tau+1)},\\
T_\tau&=2^\tau T_0.
\end{aligned}
\]
For the next $T_\tau$ time periods, sample $i\in\mathcal A^{(\tau+1)}$ uniformly at random, provide $S_\tau^{(i)}$ to the arriving customer, observe $i_t$, and update the counters for that sampled item:
\[
S_t=S_\tau^{(i)},\qquad
n_i\leftarrow n_i+\mathbf1\{i_t=i\},\qquad
n_0(i)\leftarrow n_0(i)+\mathbf1\{i_t=0\}.
\]
Purchases of another item $\ell\in S_\tau^{(i)}$, $\ell\ne i$, do not contribute to these counters. At the end of the epoch the printed estimate update is
\[
\widehat v_i^{(\tau+1)}
=\max\{1,n_i/n_0(i)\},\qquad i\in\mathcal A^{(\tau+1)}.
\]
Define the epoch contamination upper bound and active-set cardinality by
\[
\overline\varepsilon_\tau
=\min\{1,\overline\varepsilon T/T_\tau\},\qquad
N_\tau=|\mathcal A^{(\tau+1)}|.
\]
The error upper bound used by Algorithm 1 is
\[
\widehat\Delta_{\overline\varepsilon}(\tau+1)
=\begin{cases}
1,&T_\tau<\dfrac{\overline\varepsilon T}{4(K+1)},\\[6pt]
16K(K+1)\left(
\dfrac{\overline\varepsilon_\tau}{2}
+\sqrt{\dfrac{\overline\varepsilon_\tau N_\tau\ln T}{T_\tau}}
+\dfrac{2N_\tau\ln T}{3T_\tau}
\right)
+16\sqrt{\dfrac{KN_\tau\ln T}{T_\tau}},&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The accompanying notation describes $\mathcal A^{(\tau+1)}\subseteq[N]$ as the active items explored in epoch $\tau$, $S_\tau^{(i)}\subseteq[N]$ as an assortment containing $i$, and the counters as $n_i,n_0(i)\in\mathbb N$. It also states
\[
\widehat v^{(\tau)}\in[0,1]^N,\qquad
\gamma^{(\tau)}\in[0,1],\qquad
\widehat\Delta_{\overline\varepsilon}(\tau+1)\in[0,1].
\]
These are the source's descriptions, with the inconsistencies below retained rather than repaired.

\paragraph{Printed inconsistencies in this construction.}
Algorithm 1, line 14, prints $\max\{1,n_i/n_0(i)\}$. This conflicts with the stated range $\widehat v^{(\tau)}\in[0,1]^N$ and with the supplementary proof of Lemma 1 (physical page 30), which treats $\widehat v_i=n_i/n_0$ when that ratio is at most one and sets $\widehat v_i=1$ when it exceeds one. The operative update above retains Algorithm 1's printed maximum, not the supplementary reading. No zero-denominator convention is supplied. Likewise, the error-bound expression is not capped at one, despite its stated $[0,1]$ range. The explanatory bullets on pages 9--10 say that $\gamma^{(\tau)}$ and $S_\tau^{(i)}$ are computed using $\mathcal A^{(\tau+1)}$; the actual lines 5--6, retained above, use $\mathcal A^{(\tau)}$. The pseudocode gives geometric epoch lengths and an unbounded epoch loop, without a rounding rule for $T_\tau$ or an explicit terminal partial-epoch rule; none is added here.

\subsection*{Linked assortment computation: Section 4.1 and Algorithm 2}
This is the computation invoked by Algorithm 1, not a replacement objective for the sequential problem. At an invocation, the candidate items are the current $\mathcal A^{(\tau)}$, the utilities are the current $\widehat v^{(\tau)}$, and the required item is $i\in\mathcal A^{(\tau)}$. Section 4.1 writes the generic computation for a specific $i\in[N]$ as
\begin{equation}
\max_{|S|\le K,\,i\in S}R(S;\widehat v)
=\max_{|S|\le K,\,i\in S}
\frac{\sum_{j\in S}r_j\widehat v_j}{1+\sum_{j\in S}\widehat v_j}.
\tag{4}
\end{equation}
For any $\alpha\in(0,1]$, the feasibility check asks whether there exists $S\subseteq[N]$, $|S|\le K$, $i\in S$ such that
\[
R(S;\widehat v)\ge\alpha
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sum_{j\in S}r_j\widehat v_j
\ge\alpha+\alpha\sum_{j\in S}\widehat v_j
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sum_{j\in S}(r_j-\alpha)\widehat v_j\ge\alpha.
\]
Because $i$ must be included, the check is equivalently whether there exists
\[
S'\subseteq[N]\setminus\{i\},\qquad |S'|\le K-1
\]
satisfying
\begin{equation}
(r_i-\alpha)\widehat v_i
+\sum_{j\in S'}(r_j-\alpha)\widehat v_j\ge\alpha.
\tag{5}
\end{equation}
The nearby prose instructs including in $S'$ the items with the largest $(K-1)$ positive values of $(r_j-\alpha)\widehat v_j$, and checking (5). A successful check means that revenue level $\alpha$ is attainable; otherwise it is not. These are threshold tests within the computation, not constraints imposed on every offered assortment for every $\alpha$.

Algorithm 2 supplies the following binary-search rows. Its inputs are $\{r_j\}_{j=1}^{n}$, $\{\widehat v_j\}_{j=1}^{n}$, a must-have item $i$, $K$, and stopping accuracy $\delta$. Its stated output is an assortment $\widehat S$ with
\[
|\widehat S|\le K,\qquad i\in\widehat S,
\]
that maximizes $R(\widehat S;\widehat v)$. Initialize
\[
\alpha_\ell=0,\qquad \alpha_u=1,\qquad \widehat S=\varnothing.
\]
While $\alpha_u-\alpha_\ell\ge\delta$, set
\[
\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}}\leftarrow(\alpha_\ell+\alpha_u)/2.
\]
For each $j\ne i$, sort
\[
\psi_j:=(r_j-\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}})\widehat v_j
\]
in descending order and form
\[
\Psi:=\{j\ne i:\psi_j\ge0\}.
\]
The remaining printed rows are
\[
\begin{aligned}
t&:=\psi_i+\text{the $(K-1)$ $\psi_j$ in $\Psi$ with the largest values};\\
\text{if }t\ge\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}}:\quad
\widehat S&=\{i\}\cup
\{\text{the $(K-1)$ items in $\Psi$ with the largest $\psi_j$ values}\},
\qquad \alpha_\ell\leftarrow\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}};\\
\text{else}:\quad \alpha_u&\leftarrow\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $t$ is the scalar used inside Algorithm 2, rather than a customer-arrival index. The loop then repeats its width test.

\paragraph{Printed omissions in Algorithm 2.}
Line 6 defines $\psi_j$ only for $j\ne i$, while line 7 uses $\psi_i$; the missing definition is not inserted above. Lines 7--8 request $(K-1)$ items from $\Psi$ without specifying what happens when fewer are available. The prose uses positive values, whereas the pseudocode explicitly uses $\psi_j\ge0$; the pseudocode's nonnegative test is retained. The only printed accuracy specification is the stopping test $\alpha_u-\alpha_\ell\ge\delta$: no value of $\delta$ for the Algorithm 1 invocation, or exact-optimality guarantee at that finite stopping accuracy, is supplied. The output is nevertheless described as a maximizer, and its initialization is the empty set. No tie-breaking, shortage, or initialization repair is added.
