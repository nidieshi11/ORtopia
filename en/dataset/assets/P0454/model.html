Here pvj and pvw are the proportions of segment v’s impressions allocated to guaranteed campaign j and non-guaranteed campaign w, respectively. The auxiliary variables in this linear program are x, hw, mvw, hjd, mvjd, hjr, and mvjr, with the campaign and segment indices appearing in (11)–(23). The printed formulation imposes the sign and interval restrictions in (21)–(23); it does not impose additional sign restrictions on hw or mvjr, or a separate sign restriction on x. No such restrictions are added here. In particular, the auxiliary variables are not fixed inputs, and no integrality restriction is imposed on the decisions.

Fixed inputs, indexing, and assumptions

The planning horizon is a single period. The sets and data below are those of Table 1 and Sections 3.1–3.3 (physical PDF pages 9–13).

V

Set of viewer segments. Each viewer belongs to a unique, finest viewer segment.

J, W

Sets of guaranteed and non-guaranteed ad campaigns, respectively.

V j, V w

Sets of viewer segments targeted by guaranteed campaign j and non-guaranteed campaign w, respectively.

Jv, Wv

Sets of guaranteed and non-guaranteed campaigns targeting viewer segment v, respectively.

Dj, Rj

Impression demand and minimum expected reach of guaranteed campaign j ∈J. A targeted reach is specified for each guaranteed contract.

Bw

Budget of non-guaranteed campaign w ∈W.

Nv

Population size of viewer segment v ∈V .

φ

Number of available ad slots on a web page, with the printed restriction φ ≥ 1.

evw

Average revenue per impression from non-guaranteed campaign w on segment v, for w ∈W, v ∈V w. It is the bid price when the bid is unchanged during the period; otherwise it may be estimated from the campaign’s effective cost per mille or that of similar campaigns.

Mean supply and half-width of the supply interval for segment v.

Exogenously given revenue, delivery, and reach risk levels. Experimental risk choices in Section 6.3 are not fixed inputs of the general formulation.

Transformation scope and qualifications

Sections 4.1–4.2 obtain (10)–(14) by linearizing and relaxing the revenue construction and then using its dual. Proposition 4 states that this yields a lower bound on the robust revenue objective, rather than an exact transformation in general. Proposition 5 gives an exact delivery transformation, resulting in (15)–(16) and their nonnegativity conditions. Theorem 3 and Proposition 6 give the exact transformation of the robust piecewise reach construction, resulting in (17)–(19) and hjr ≥ 0. The paper states this reach exactness for general uncertainty budgets, not only integer budgets. The underlying reach function has already been lower-bounded as above. Remark 2 identifies the preceding chance-constraint approximation as using deterministic lower bounds. Superseded robust or chance constraints are not imposed in addition to (11)–(23).
