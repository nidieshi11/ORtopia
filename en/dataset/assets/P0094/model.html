Core model: multi-trip VRPTW with an unloading queue

Source: Section 3.1, PDF pp.3–4, and Section 3.4, PDF p.7, SPM (44)–(48). This is the full integer trip-set formulation, over the complete feasible column set.

Physical data. N = {1,…,n} are collection customers and 0 is the depot. Customer i has demand qi > 0, service duration si and service-start window [ei,li]. Vehicle capacity is Q, fleet size is m, and the operating horizon is [0,T]. Let cij be distance and tij travel time including service at the origin; hence tij = si + driving timeij and s0 = 0. Depot unloading slots are u = 1,…,P of length L, starting at Su = (u − 1)L; SP+1 = T. C is the number of simultaneous unloading positions, available for simultaneous unloading. Unloading q units takes δq time and reserves complete slots.

Complete trip domain. R is the set of all feasible single trips. A trip r specifies an ordered sequence (0,i1,…,ik,0) of distinct customers, a departure time ar ≥ 0, service-start times br,ij, a depot return time dr, and consecutive unloading slots ur,…,vr. It is in R precisely when

k ≥ 1,∑ j=1kq ij ≤ Q,br,i1 ≥ ar + t0i1, br,ij+1 ≥ br,ij + tijij+1(j < k),eij ≤ br,ij ≤ lij(j = 1,…,k), dr = br,ik + tik0,dr ≤ Sur,1 ≤ ur ≤ vr ≤ P, δ∑ j=1kq ij ≤ Svr+1 − Sur,fr := Svr+1 ≤ T.

The unloading reservation consists of consecutive full slots; normally vr − ur + 1 = ⌈δ∑ ⁡ jqij∕L⌉. A longer reservation is dominated, and may be retained without improving the minimum-distance objective. If the last slot is truncated at T, the displayed duration inequality ensures enough unloading time across the occupied slots. The trip occupies a vehicle continuously on [ar,fr), including travel, queue waiting and the full unloading reservation. All these quantities are attributes of a column. Trip size is governed by the capacity and timing constraints.

Thus cost is travel distance. Boundary times use half-open occupancy so a vehicle can start a new trip exactly when its previous unloading reservation ends.
