\section*{Stochastic pickup and delivery with predictive route insertion}
\paragraph{Conditional source interpretation.}
The model below selects the route-state dimensions in (4)--(6), the ordinary
leg descriptions and departure/load definitions of the 2015 paper (printed
pp.\ 241--242), and the physical current-position anchor in its Fig.\ 1.
It uses the pickup indicator as the waiting factor. The 2015 article says
that its formulation summarizes Cort\'es et al.\ (2009); that paper's
printed p.\ 33 explicitly identifies the waiting factor with the pickup
indicator of the route being costed. The earlier 2008 paper uses the
literal name $z$ for this indicator. The travel and waiting coefficients here
follow the two terms of 2015 (8). The selected expressions and their
differences from the printed matrices are specified below.

\paragraph{Information, histories and decisions.}
Consider an observed current request and a tree of $N-1$ future requests
with historical transition probabilities. There are $F$ vehicles.
At history $h$ of depth $d\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$ let $e_h$ be the time
of the known or forecast request and let $\pi_h$ be the probability of
reaching $h$. The root has probability one. All histories with the same
observations have the same decisions up to their branching point.
The initial position, load, preceding route and unserved passengers of each
vehicle are given. To produce the state at a child history, follow the
parent's assigned route until the child request time. Remove the completed
prefix, including a service ending at the request time, retain all
passengers still aboard and interpolate the vehicle's position along
its current travel segment. An idle vehicle stays at its last position.
In the ten input cases, travel has given constant speed and each recorded
service and transfer time is zero.

At each history choose a vehicle $j$, a pickup rank $a$ and a delivery
rank $b$ for its incoming passenger. Given $q$ pending stops of that vehicle
immediately before insertion, put $w=q+2$. The ordinal domain is
$1\leq a\leq w-1$, $2\leq b\leq w$ and $a<b$. Delete the new pickup and
delivery from the new list to recover exactly the prior ordered list.
Other vehicles retain their pending routes. Enforce pickup before delivery,
passenger-vehicle consistency, nonnegative onboard loads and vehicle
capacity on every prefix. Initial onboard passengers have their pending
deliveries and disembark by the end of their vehicle's route. Execute the
root decision in the current receding-horizon update.

\paragraph{States of an issued route.}
For vehicle $j$ and issued route at $h$, ordinary stops are
$i=1,\ldots,w_{jh}$, and row zero is its vehicle anchor at issue. Write
$r_{jhi}=1$ for pickup and $r_{jhi}=0$ for delivery. The stop also records
its passenger identity and leg time $\Gamma_{jhi}$ from the preceding
point, including the input's transfer delay. On the deterministic leg data
at the specified history the conditional state prediction of (6) is
\[
 L_{jh0}=L^{\rm anchor}_{jh},\quad T_{jh0}=e_h,\qquad
 L_{jhi}=L_{jh,i-1}+2r_{jhi}-1,\qquad
 T_{jhi}=T_{jh,i-1}+\Gamma_{jhi}.
\]
Here $L_{jhi}$ is the load after leaving stop $i$; the previous stop's load
travels on leg $i$. The vector width is $w_{jh}+1$. Given the input travel
and service times and the fixed route, the conditional expectations equal
these values. Observations at a later request produce the state from which
its own route is issued.

\paragraph{Costs.}
Use the pickup factor on the same route whose ranks and state are being
priced. The fleet cost of the route set $S_h$ at issue is
\[
 D_h=\sum_{j=1}^{F}\sum_{i=1}^{w_{jh}}
 \left[L_{jh,i-1}\left(T_{jhi}-T_{jh,i-1}\right)
       +r_{jhi}\left(T_{jhi}-T_{jh0}\right)\right].
\]
Price the preceding routes at the given initial vehicle anchors by the
same rule to obtain $D_{-1}$. Define $p(h)$ as the parent of $h$,
with $D_{p(h)}=D_{-1}$ at the root. The finite decision tree minimizes
the expected increments of (7),
\[
 \min_{\text{feasible nonanticipative insertions}}
       \sum_{d=0}^{N-1}\sum_{h:\,\mathrm{depth}(h)=d}\pi_h\big(D_h-D_{p(h)}\big).
\]
The probabilities of children sum to the parent's probability, so the same
objective equals the expected cost at the last decision histories less
the original baseline. Bellman minimization over every vehicle and legal
rank pair certifies the finite input optimum.

\paragraph{Printed alternatives.}
In 2015 (4)--(6) the predicted state $x(k+t)$ has the width of the issued
$S(k+t-1)$, whereas (8) writes $w_j(k+t)$ and $z_j^i(k+t)$, including at
$t=N$ where the control vector ends at $k+N-1$. The selection above
matches the ranked state and the issued list and uses $k$ through $k+N-1$
as the decision epochs. The cited 2009 (11) uses the preceding issued
list and its pickup factors with the predicted snapshot. Its driver charge
and additional waiting weight are absent from 2015 (8). The 2015
Appendix A displays a strictly lower triangular load matrix and
$B_T^2=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,\ldots,1)$ for departure times.
For signed stop changes $d_i=2r_i-1$, its load at rank $i\geq1$ is
$L_0+d_0+\sum_{s<i}d_s$. The after-stop definition above gives
$L_0+\sum_{s\leq i}d_s$ instead. The diagonal clock gives $T_0+\Gamma_i$,
whereas the cumulative clock gives $T_0+\sum_{s\leq i}\Gamma_s$.
The original literal transcription is preserved separately for comparison.
The legacy input also contains passenger-specific price coefficients;
under the selected indicator interpretation these have no operational
effect. Every input retains its original history tree, dimensions, demand,
probabilities and vehicle data.
