Known-demand stochastic dynamic pricing with financial milestone penalties

𝔼 [∫ 0Tp(t)dS(t) −∑ i=1m [β 1,i(ξi − S(τi))+ + β 2,i(ζi − R(τi))+]] , 		(3)

subject to

S(T) ≤ Calmost surely. 		(4)

For every t ∈ [0,T], nonanticipation means that p(t) is allowed to depend only on past demand observations and price realizations. The decision is the pricing policy; sales and revenues are the resulting stochastic processes. The paper defines J∗ as the supremum of (3) over all nonanticipating pricing policies satisfying (4).

Demand and cumulative processes

The seller starts with C units of a homogeneous good to sell over a horizon T. Potential customers arrive according to a homogeneous Poisson process with instantaneous rate Λ. Each customer has willingness to pay v for one unit, independently drawn from the cumulative distribution function F(⋅). In this selected version, Λ and F(⋅) are known to the decision-maker. A customer arriving at time t purchases one unit if v ≥ p(t) and otherwise leaves without purchasing. The resulting instantaneous arrival rate is

λ(p(t)) := Λℙ(v ≥ p(t)) = ΛF¯(p(t)), 		(1)

where

F¯(⋅) := 1 − F(⋅).

For every t ∈ [0,T], S(t) is the number of units sold in [0,t], and R(t) is the revenue accumulated up to time t. All time processes are right continuous with left limits. Revenue used in milestone comparisons is actual sales revenue, excluding penalties: an incurred penalty does not reduce the revenue used for future revenue targets.

Milestone data and penalties

Before combining the milestone grids, the sales targets are specified by

0 < t1 < t2 < ⋯ < tk ≤ T,ξ1 ≤ ξ2 ≤⋯ ≤ ξk ≤ C,

and the revenue targets by

0 < t1′ < t 2′ < ⋯ < t k′′≤ T,ζ 1 ≤ ζ2 ≤⋯ ≤ ζk′.

Here ξi is the minimum number of units targeted for sale by ti, and ζi is the cumulative revenue targeted by ti′.

The paper places both types of targets on the common grid

τ0 = 0 < τ1 < τ2 < ⋯ < τm = T,ξ0 = 0,ζ0 = 0.

On this common grid, the corresponding target is set to zero whenever a sales or revenue constraint is absent at τi. Thus the preceding monotonicity statements describe the original target sequences, not additional monotonicity constraints on the zero-filled common-grid arrays. Targets at τm = T are optional; the paper does not require either complete sellout or a positive terminal revenue target.

The fixed penalty parameters satisfy

β1,i > 0,β2,i > 0,i = 1,…,m.

The sales and revenue milestone conditions referred to in the penalty definition are, respectively,

S(τi) ≥ ξi,R(τi) ≥ ζi,i = 1,…,m.

These are targets that may be violated, not additional hard feasibility constraints in (3)–(4). Their respective shortfall penalties are

β1,i(ξi − S(τi))+andβ 2,i(ζi − R(τi))+,i = 1,…,m. 		(2)

The penalty is proportional to the amount by which the target is missed.

Input assumptions and their defining quantities

The paper introduces the following assumptions as holding throughout the paper. They are restrictions on the demand model and fixed input data, rather than additional sample-path milestone constraints.

Assumption 1. The willingness-to-pay distribution has support in [0,p¯], where

0 < p¯ < ∞,

and F¯(⋅) is continuously differentiable on [0,p¯].

Let ψ(⋅) denote the inverse of F¯(⋅) over [0,1]. Define the revenue function by

r(λ) := λψ(λ∕Λ),λ ∈ [0,Λ].

Assumption 2. (i) r(λ) is strictly concave for λ ∈ [0,Λ].

(ii) Define the revenue-maximizing price and its corresponding sales rate by

p∗ = argmax ⁡ p∈[0,p¯]pF¯(p),λ∗ = λ(p∗).

Source scope and limitations

The formulation and definitions above are from Besbes and Maglaras, Dynamic Pricing with Financial Milestones: Feedback-Form Policies, Management Science 58(9), 2012, Section 2, pp. 1717–1719 (physical PDF pages 3–5).

The printed policy statement in (3)–(4) does not separately specify a pointwise admissible-price interval for p(t). The interval [0,p¯] is explicitly stated for willingness-to-pay support, differentiability, and the maximization defining p∗. No extra policy bound is inserted here. Likewise, the inverse in (5) is retained with its printed codomain without adding an unprinted convention for an empty level set.

The hard-target deterministic formulation, its feasibility criterion, and the later sufficiently-large-penalty condition are not imposed on this stochastic penalized formulation. The online companion cited for the later feedback-policy proofs was not supplied and remains unverified; no formulas from it are used here.
