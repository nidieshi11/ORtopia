max ⁡ {sFP∗(r P) : bFP(rP) ≤ b}.

The sponsor chooses the per-unit purchase subsidy rP; b is her fixed available budget. The firm subsequently chooses its distribution quantity and price to maximize expected profit, before demand is realized. The subscripts F and P designate the for-profit firm and purchase subsidy, respectively. The sponsor’s objective and budget constraint above are the unnumbered formulation in Section 5.1 (physical PDF page 20).

Firm response and sponsor accounting

The purchase-subsidy replacements for the newsvendor quantity and price responses in Section 3.1 are

pFP∗(r P) = argmax ⁡ p∈[p̲,p¯] {p∫ c−rP p 1F¯ Dinv(𝜃,p)d𝜃}, qFP∗(r P) = F¯Dinv ( c − rP pFP∗(rP),pFP∗(r P)), sFP∗(r P) = ŝ (qFP∗(r P),pFP∗(r P)), bFP(rP) = rPqFP∗(r P).

These are the operative responses and accounting definitions on physical PDF page 19, rather than the superseded no-subsidy responses (4)–(6). As specified in footnote 5 on physical PDF page 12, throughout the paper, if multiple optimal solutions exist, the smallest one is chosen.

The fixed-price quantity response used in this construction is stated in Section 4.1 (physical PDF page 14):

qFP(rP,p) = F¯Dinv (c − rP p ,p).

Here F¯Dinv(⋅,p) is the inverse of F¯D(⋅,p), as defined immediately after (4). The paper leaves this inverse notation abstract.

Demand, consumption, profit, and variable roles

The fixed demand family consists of nonnegative random variables D(p) with distribution FD(⋅,p) and mean μD(p), indexed by the firm’s admissible price:

p ∈ [p̲,p¯],μD(p) = 𝔼[D(p)], F¯D(⋅,p) = 1 − FD(⋅,p), d¯(p) = sup ⁡ {D(p)} < ∞,p ∈ [p̲,p¯].

Thus p̲ and p¯ are fixed price endpoints, whereas d¯(p) is the upper endpoint of demand support, not a printed upper bound on the firm’s quantity decision. The marginal cost c and sponsor budget b are fixed data. The firm’s distribution quantity is q, its price is p, and d denotes realized demand. The subsidy and distribution quantity are nonnegative quantities; no integrality restriction is stated:

rP ≥ 0,q ≥ 0,p ∈ [p̲,p¯].

The purchase subsidy pays rP dollars for each unit brought to the market. The realized profit is

πP(q,p,d,rP) = p(q ∧ d) − cq + rPq, 		(1)

where

x ∧ y = min ⁡ {x,y}.

The for-profit firm’s objective is to maximize

𝔼[πP(q,p,D(p),rP)].

There are no not-for-profit break-even or loss-probability constraints in this formulation.

The expected-sales function defined in Section 4 (physical PDF page 13) is

ŝ(q,p) = ∫ 0qF¯ D(x,p)dx = 𝔼[q ∧ D(p)].

The proof of Proposition 3 (physical PDF page 27) writes the corresponding expected-profit function as

ϕ(p,q,rP) = p∫ 0qF¯ D(x,p)dx − (c − rP)q.

This is the firm’s profit objective underlying the response formulas, not the sponsor’s consumption objective.

Scope of the monotonicity premise

The sponsor formulation is stated without a hazard-order constraint. Proposition 3 provides a conditional monotonicity result for the same responses. Its printed premise is that

{D(p),p̲ ≤ p ≤p¯}is decreasing in the hazard rate order,

with the printed parenthetical explanation

−dlog ⁡ F¯D(x,p) dx is decreasing in p for any x.

Under that premise, the proposition states, for

0 ≤ rP ≤ c,

that qFP∗(rP), sFP∗(rP), and bFP(rP) are increasing in rP, while pFP∗(rP) is decreasing in rP. This range is the printed scope of Proposition 3; the sponsor’s displayed maximization does not itself print an upper bound rP ≤ c.

Printed hazard-order inconsistency. The parenthetical hazard-rate direction above is retained as printed. In the proof of Proposition 3 the paper instead uses the implication

F¯D(x,p) F¯D(q,p)is increasing in p for any x < q.

This implication has the opposite hazard-rate direction from the printed parenthetical. Neither reading is silently substituted for the other, and neither is added as an unconditional feasibility restriction on the sponsor’s problem.

Full-subsidy boundary and the printed extension

Section 5.3 and the proof of Proposition 6 identify the full-cost purchase subsidy rP = c. With

pμ ≡ argmax ⁡ {pμD(p) : p̲ ≤ p ≤p¯},

they state the boundary consumption and the minimum budget needed to induce it as

sFP∗(c) = μ D(pμ),bFP(c) = cd¯(pμ).

Printed extension beyond full cost. The proof of Proposition 6 (physical PDF page 29) states that, if rP ≥ c, the firm’s objective is increasing in q and then prints

qFP∗(r P) = d¯(p),pFP∗(r P) = pμ,

asserting that the decisions become independent of rP once rP ≥ c. The p in the first expression is retained as printed. For rP > c, this assertion conflicts with (1) when no quantity cap is imposed: after demand is fully covered, profit still increases with q. The paper does not state a decision constraint q ≤d¯(p) in its model definition. Accordingly, no such cap, clipping of the inverse argument, or repaired response for rP > c is introduced here. The inverse responses are retained as printed; the paper also supplies no explicit extension of F¯Dinv outside its probability argument range or additional price restriction to resolve such cases.
