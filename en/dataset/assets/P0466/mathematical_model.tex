\section*{Online dynamic pricing: recursive variational reformulation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\sup_{\Pi\in\mathcal G}\int_0^t L_q^\star(\lambda;\Pi_s,\pi_s,s)\,ds\\
&\text{subject to}\quad \Pi_t=0.
\end{aligned}\tag{A115}
\end{equation}
For each $q\in\mathbb N_+$ and $t\geq0$, Theorem A1 identifies the optimal value of this problem with $J^\star(\lambda;q,t)$. The arrival rate $\lambda>0$ is fixed. The decision is the function $\Pi$; $\pi$ is its density, not an independent decision. The lower-inventory optimal value in the integrand is inherited recursively from the same online problem, not optimized independently at inventory level $q$.

\subsection*{Admissible functions and recursive integrand}
The class $\mathcal G$ consists of functions $\Pi$ absolutely continuous on $\mathbb R_+$ with a density $\pi\in[0,1]$. Thus its defining requirements are
\[
\Pi\in\operatorname{AC}(\mathbb R_+),\qquad
\frac{d\Pi_s}{ds}=\pi_s\quad\text{for a.e. }s\in\mathbb R_+,
\qquad \pi\text{ takes values in }[0,1].
\]
The left endpoint is variable and the right endpoint is fixed. The Lagrangian has the printed domain
\[
L_q^\star:(\mathbb R_+;\mathbb R,[0,1],\mathbb R_+)\longmapsto\mathbb R
\]
and is
\begin{equation}
L_q^\star(\lambda;\Pi,\pi,s)
=\lambda\exp(\lambda\Pi)\pi\bigl(F^{-1}(1-\pi)+J^\star(q-1,s)\bigr),
\qquad q\in\mathbb N_+.
\tag{A116}
\end{equation}
The recursive boundary values inherited from Section 3 are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
J^\star(\lambda;q,0)&=0,&&q\in\mathbb N_+,\\
J^\star(\lambda;0,t)&=0,&&t\geq0.
\end{aligned}\tag{4, boundary values}
\end{equation}
The correspondence of optimal solutions in Theorem A1 is
\[
\pi^\star(\lambda;0,\cdot)=0,\qquad
\pi^\star(\lambda;q,s)
=\frac{\partial\Pi^\star(\lambda;q,s)}{\partial s},
\quad q\in\mathbb N_+,\quad\text{for a.e. }s\in[0,t],
\]
and holds in both directions between the optimal families in (A115) and the online policy problem.

\subsection*{Fixed inputs and distribution assumptions}
Section 3 specifies a single item, a finite selling horizon of length $t>0$, and initial inventory $q$ with
\[
\mathbb N=\{0,1,2,\ldots\},\qquad
\mathbb N_+=\{1,2,\ldots\},\qquad
q\in\mathbb N,\qquad \lambda>0.
\]
The variational statement also includes the boundary case $t=0$, and applies at each $q\in\mathbb N_+$. Customers arrive according to a Poisson process with known rate $\lambda$. Their nonnegative valuations $X_n$, $n\in\mathbb N_+$, are independent and identically distributed, with common distribution function $F$ and finite mean. The firm knows $F$ and the i.i.d. assumption, but not the specific valuations. The support has lower limit $x_L$ and upper limit $x_U$, where $x_U$ may be $\infty$. The distribution has a positive density $f$ on the entirety of its support. All random variables and processes are defined on the common probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal F,P)$.

The firm is risk-neutral, all items have zero marginal cost, and each customer purchases at most one unit. A customer purchases when the valuation is at least the posted price. The printed price--probability relation is
\[
P(\text{purchase at price }p)=1-F(p).
\]
Section 3 defines the inverse, stated there to be uniquely defined on $[0,1]$, by
\[
F^{-1}(\pi)=\{x\in[x_L,x_U]:F(x)=\pi\},\qquad \pi\in[0,1].
\]
For a desired purchasing probability and its corresponding price,
\[
\pi_s\in[0,1],\qquad
p_s=F^{-1}(1-\pi_s),\qquad 0\leq s\leq t.
\]
The optimal-policy correspondence is
\[
\pi^\star=1-F(p^\star),\qquad
p^\star=F^{-1}(1-\pi^\star).
\]
The null price is assumed to exist and satisfies
\[
1-F(p_\infty)=0.
\]
No Gumbel-domain, von-Mises, unimodality, or Theorem A2 uniqueness assumption is a premise of Theorem A1.

\subsection*{Purchasing-probability policies and inherited value functions}
The following rows define the policy values used in the recursive construction; they are not additional independent optimization problems imposed on (A115). A purchasing-probability policy
\[
\pi=\{\pi_s,0\leq s\leq t\}
\]
on $(\Omega,\mathcal F,P)$ belongs to $\mathcal W$ precisely when it is $[0,1]$-valued and satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
\pi_s&=\pi(\lambda;Q_s,t-s),&&0\leq s\leq t,\\
\pi(\lambda;q,\cdot)&\text{ is measurable},&&q\in\mathbb N,\\
\pi(\lambda;0,\cdot)&=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $Q_s$ is the inventory process, and $N$ is a standard rate-one Poisson process:
\begin{equation}
Q_s=Q_0-N\left(\lambda\int_0^s\pi(\lambda;Q_u,t-u)\,du\right),
\qquad 0\leq s\leq t.
\tag{A108}
\end{equation}
For initial inventory $Q_0=q$, the associated expected revenue is
\begin{equation}
J_\pi(\lambda;q,t)
=E\left[\left.
\lambda\int_0^t\pi(\lambda;Q_s,t-s)
F^{-1}\bigl(1-\pi(\lambda;Q_s,t-s)\bigr)\,ds
\,\right|\,Q_0=q\right].
\tag{A107}
\end{equation}
The inherited optimal-value definition is
\begin{equation}
J^\star(\lambda;q,t)=\sup_{\pi\in\mathcal W}J_\pi(\lambda;q,t).
\tag{A109}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{First-sale decomposition and Proposition A8}
The first-sale time is printed as
\[
\tau=\inf\{s\geq0:Q_s<Q_0\}.
\]
For each $\pi\in\mathcal W$ and $q\in\mathbb N$, the survival probability is
\begin{equation}
P(\tau>s\mid Q_0=q)
=\exp\left(-\lambda\int_0^s\pi(q,t-u)\,du\right),
\qquad 0\leq s<t.
\tag{A110}
\end{equation}
The printed first-sale revenue identity is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&E\left[\left.\int_0^\tau
\pi(Q_s,t-s)F^{-1}\bigl(1-\pi(Q_s,t-s)\bigr)\,ds
\,\right|\,Q_0=q\right]\\
&\quad=\int_0^t
\exp\left(-\lambda\int_0^s\pi(q,t-u)\,du\right)
\pi(q,t-s)F^{-1}\bigl(1-\pi(q,t-s)\bigr)\,ds.
\end{aligned}\tag{A111}
\end{equation}
The intervening unnumbered conditional identity is
\[
E\left[\left.\int_\tau^t
\pi(Q_s,t-s)F^{-1}\bigl(1-\pi(Q_s,t-s)\bigr)\,ds
\,\right|\,\tau,Q_0=q\right]
=J_\pi(q-1,t-\tau)/\lambda.
\]
Consequently the continuation identity is printed as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&E\left[\left.\int_\tau^t
\pi(Q_s,t-s)F^{-1}\bigl(1-\pi(Q_s,t-s)\bigr)\,ds
\,\right|\,Q_0=q\right]\\
&\quad=\int_0^t
\exp\left(-\lambda\int_0^s\pi(q,t-u)\,du\right)
\pi(q,t-s)J_\pi(q-1,t-s)\,ds.
\end{aligned}\tag{A112}
\end{equation}
For each $\pi\in\mathcal W$, Proposition A8 states that the family
$\{J_\pi(\lambda;q,\cdot),q\in\mathbb N\}$ is the unique solution to
\begin{equation}
J_\pi(\lambda;q,t)
=\int_0^t L_q\bigl(\Pi(\lambda;q,s,t),\pi(q,s),s\bigr)\,ds,
\qquad t\geq0,\quad q\in\mathbb N_+,
\tag{A113}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\Pi(q,s,t)=-\int_s^t\pi(q,u)\,du,
\qquad s\geq0,
\tag{A114}
\end{equation}
and
\[
\begin{gathered}
L_q:(\mathbb R_+;\mathbb R,[0,1],\mathbb R_+)\longmapsto\mathbb R,\\
L_q(\lambda;\Pi,\pi,s)
=\lambda\exp(\lambda\Pi)\pi
\bigl(F^{-1}(1-\pi)+J_\pi(q-1,s)\bigr),
\qquad q\in\mathbb N_+,\\
J_\pi(0,\cdot)=0.
\end{gathered}
\]
For each $\pi\in\mathcal W$, $q\in\mathbb N_+$, and $t\geq0$, the source states that $\Pi(q,\cdot,t)$ is absolutely continuous on $\mathbb R_+$ with density $\pi(q,\cdot)$. This is the construction leading to $\mathcal G$ and (A115). In (A116), the continuation term is the optimal value $J^\star$, rather than the fixed-policy value $J_\pi$ appearing in Proposition A8.

\paragraph{Printed notation and scope.}
Equations (A110)--(A116) retain the source's mixed display and suppression of $\lambda$. In particular, (A113) prints $L_q$ with three arguments although the immediately following definition has the four arguments $(\lambda;\Pi,\pi,s)$; (A114) suppresses $\lambda$ in $\Pi$ and $\pi$; and (A116) prints $J^\star(q-1,s)$ without $\lambda$. The recursive dependence is the fixed-arrival-rate continuation value inherited from (3), as stated in Theorem A1, not an instruction to reset $\lambda$ to one. Section 4 separately uses two-argument value notation for inventory and market size; that separate notation does not supply a missing argument in the printed Appendix D rows.

The prose introducing $\tau$ prints ``$\pi\in\mathcal V$'', although $\mathcal V$ is the pricing-policy class and the subsequent (A110) and Proposition A8 explicitly use $\pi\in\mathcal W$. Also, (A111) prints the upper limit $\tau$, not a truncation at $t$; the following conditional identity and (A112) print the lower limit $\tau$ without a restriction to $\tau\leq t$. The definition of $\tau$ does not guarantee a sale before the finite horizon, and the displayed continuation uses $q-1$ without a new positive-inventory qualifier in that intervening prose. These expressions and their printed scope are retained; no truncation, indicator, or extension to negative remaining times or negative inventories is added. Proposition A8 itself explicitly restricts its recursion to $q\in\mathbb N_+$.

\subsection*{Original definition of the continuation optimum}
Theorem A1 explicitly invokes (3). The following original pricing-policy rows specify its meaning, rather than adding a second pricing optimization to (A115). The class $\mathcal V$ consists of $\mathbb R_+\cup p_\infty$-valued policies
\[
p=\{p_s,0\leq s\leq t\}
\]
on $(\Omega,\mathcal F,P)$ satisfying
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_s&=p(\lambda;Q_s,t-s),&&0\leq s\leq t,\\
p(\lambda;q,\cdot)&\text{ is measurable},&&q\in\mathbb N,\\
p(\lambda;0,\cdot)&=p_\infty.
\end{aligned}
\]
Their state and revenue definitions are
\begin{equation}
Q_s=Q_0-N\left(\lambda\int_0^s
\bigl(1-F(p(\lambda;Q_u,t-u))\bigr)\,du\right),
\qquad 0\leq s\leq t,
\tag{2}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
J_p(\lambda;q,t)
=E\left[\left.\lambda\int_0^t
p(\lambda;Q_s,t-s)
\bigl(1-F(p(\lambda;Q_s,t-s))\bigr)\,ds
\,\right|\,Q_0=q\right],
\tag{1}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
J^\star(\lambda;q,t)=\sup_{p\in\mathcal V}J_p(\lambda;q,t).
\tag{3}
\end{equation}
The correspondence $\pi=1-F(p)$ gives (A107)--(A109). Thus (1)--(3) and (A107)--(A114) are the defining chain for the continuation value in the selected variational model, not additional controls or constraints imposed alongside it.

\paragraph{Endpoint expressions.}
The source includes $\pi=0$ in $\mathcal G$ and defines $F^{-1}$ on $[0,1]$, while permitting $x_U=\infty$. Accordingly (A107), Proposition A8, and (A116) retain their printed factors $\pi F^{-1}(1-\pi)$. The cited passages supply no explicit product convention at $\pi=0$ when the upper endpoint is infinite; none is introduced here.
