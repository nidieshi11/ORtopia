Online dynamic pricing: recursive variational reformulation

sup ⁡ Π∈G ∫ 0tL q⋆(λ;Π s,πs,s)ds subject toΠt = 0. 		(A115)

For each q ∈ ℕ+ and t ≥ 0, Theorem A1 identifies the optimal value of this problem with J⋆(λ;q,t). The arrival rate λ > 0 is fixed. The decision is the function Π; π is its density, not an independent decision. The lower-inventory optimal value in the integrand is inherited recursively from the same online problem, not optimized independently at inventory level q.

Admissible functions and recursive integrand

The class G consists of functions Π absolutely continuous on ℝ+ with a density π ∈ [0,1]. Thus its defining requirements are

Π ∈ AC ⁡ (ℝ+),dΠs ds = πsfor a.e. s ∈ ℝ+,π takes values in [0,1].

The left endpoint is variable and the right endpoint is fixed. The Lagrangian has the printed domain

Lq⋆ : (ℝ +; ℝ,[0,1], ℝ+)⟼ℝ

and is

Lq⋆(λ;Π,π,s) = λexp ⁡ (λΠ)π(F−1(1 − π) + J⋆(q − 1,s)),q ∈ ℕ +. 		(A116)

The recursive boundary values inherited from Section 3 are

J⋆(λ;q,0) = 0,q ∈ ℕ +, J⋆(λ;0,t) = 0,t ≥ 0. 		(4, boundary values)

The correspondence of optimal solutions in Theorem A1 is

π⋆(λ;0,⋅) = 0,π⋆(λ;q,s) = ∂Π⋆(λ;q,s) ∂s ,q ∈ ℕ+,for a.e. s ∈ [0,t],

and holds in both directions between the optimal families in (A115) and the online policy problem.

Fixed inputs and distribution assumptions

Section 3 specifies a single item, a finite selling horizon of length t > 0, and initial inventory q with

ℕ = {0,1,2,…},ℕ+ = {1,2,…},q ∈ ℕ,λ > 0.

The variational statement also includes the boundary case t = 0, and applies at each q ∈ ℕ+. Customers arrive according to a Poisson process with known rate λ. Their nonnegative valuations Xn, n ∈ ℕ+, are independent and identically distributed, with common distribution function F and finite mean. The firm knows F and the i.i.d. assumption, but not the specific valuations. The support has lower limit xL and upper limit xU, where xU may be ∞. The distribution has a positive density f on the entirety of its support. All random variables and processes are defined on the common probability space (Ω,F,P).

The firm is risk-neutral, all items have zero marginal cost, and each customer purchases at most one unit. A customer purchases when the valuation is at least the posted price. The printed price–probability relation is

P(purchase at price p) = 1 − F(p).

Section 3 defines the inverse, stated there to be uniquely defined on [0,1], by

F−1(π) = {x ∈ [x L,xU] : F(x) = π},π ∈ [0,1].

For a desired purchasing probability and its corresponding price,

πs ∈ [0,1],ps = F−1(1 − π s),0 ≤ s ≤ t.

The optimal-policy correspondence is

π⋆ = 1 − F(p⋆),p⋆ = F−1(1 − π⋆).

The null price is assumed to exist and satisfies

1 − F(p∞) = 0.

No Gumbel-domain, von-Mises, unimodality, or Theorem A2 uniqueness assumption is a premise of Theorem A1.

Purchasing-probability policies and inherited value functions

The following rows define the policy values used in the recursive construction; they are not additional independent optimization problems imposed on (A115). A purchasing-probability policy

π = {πs,0 ≤ s ≤ t}

on (Ω,F,P) belongs to W precisely when it is [0,1]-valued and satisfies

πs = π(λ;Qs,t − s),0 ≤ s ≤ t, π(λ;q,⋅) is measurable, q ∈ ℕ, π(λ;0,⋅) = 0.

Here Qs is the inventory process, and N is a standard rate-one Poisson process:

Qs = Q0 − N (λ∫ 0sπ(λ;Q u,t − u)du),0 ≤ s ≤ t. 		(A108)

For initial inventory Q0 = q, the associated expected revenue is

Jπ(λ;q,t) = E [ λ∫ 0tπ(λ;Q s,t − s)F−1(1 − π(λ;Q s,t − s))ds|Q0 = q]. 		(A107)

The inherited optimal-value definition is

J⋆(λ;q,t) = sup ⁡ π∈WJπ(λ;q,t). 		(A109)

First-sale decomposition and Proposition A8

The first-sale time is printed as

τ = inf ⁡ {s ≥ 0 : Qs < Q0}.

For each π ∈W and q ∈ ℕ, the survival probability is

P(τ > s∣Q0 = q) = exp ⁡ (−λ∫ 0sπ(q,t − u)du),0 ≤ s < t. 		(A110)

The printed first-sale revenue identity is

E [ ∫ 0τπ(Q s,t − s)F−1(1 − π(Q s,t − s))ds|Q0 = q] = ∫ 0t exp ⁡ (−λ∫ 0sπ(q,t − u)du)π(q,t − s)F−1(1 − π(q,t − s))ds. 		(A111)

The intervening unnumbered conditional identity is

E [ ∫ τtπ(Q s,t − s)F−1(1 − π(Q s,t − s))ds|τ,Q0 = q] = Jπ(q − 1,t − τ)∕λ.

Consequently the continuation identity is printed as

E [ ∫ τtπ(Q s,t − s)F−1(1 − π(Q s,t − s))ds|Q0 = q] = ∫ 0t exp ⁡ (−λ∫ 0sπ(q,t − u)du)π(q,t − s)J π(q − 1,t − s)ds. 		(A112)

For each π ∈W, Proposition A8 states that the family {Jπ(λ;q,⋅),q ∈ ℕ} is the unique solution to

Jπ(λ;q,t) = ∫ 0tL q(Π(λ;q,s,t),π(q,s),s)ds,t ≥ 0,q ∈ ℕ+, 		(A113)

where

Π(q,s,t) = −∫ stπ(q,u)du,s ≥ 0, 		(A114)

and

Lq : (ℝ+; ℝ,[0,1], ℝ+)⟼ℝ, Lq(λ;Π,π,s) = λexp ⁡ (λΠ)π(F−1(1 − π) + J π(q − 1,s)),q ∈ ℕ+, Jπ(0,⋅) = 0.

For each π ∈W, q ∈ ℕ+, and t ≥ 0, the source states that Π(q,⋅,t) is absolutely continuous on ℝ+ with density π(q,⋅). This is the construction leading to G and (A115). In (A116), the continuation term is the optimal value J⋆, rather than the fixed-policy value Jπ appearing in Proposition A8.

Printed notation and scope. Equations (A110)–(A116) retain the source’s mixed display and suppression of λ. In particular, (A113) prints Lq with three arguments although the immediately following definition has the four arguments (λ;Π,π,s); (A114) suppresses λ in Π and π; and (A116) prints J⋆(q − 1,s) without λ. The recursive dependence is the fixed-arrival-rate continuation value inherited from (3), as stated in Theorem A1, not an instruction to reset λ to one. Section 4 separately uses two-argument value notation for inventory and market size; that separate notation does not supply a missing argument in the printed Appendix D rows.

The prose introducing τ prints “π ∈V ”, although V is the pricing-policy class and the subsequent (A110) and Proposition A8 explicitly use π ∈W. Also, (A111) prints the upper limit τ, not a truncation at t; the following conditional identity and (A112) print the lower limit τ without a restriction to τ ≤ t. The definition of τ does not guarantee a sale before the finite horizon, and the displayed continuation uses q − 1 without a new positive-inventory qualifier in that intervening prose. These expressions and their printed scope are retained; no truncation, indicator, or extension to negative remaining times or negative inventories is added. Proposition A8 itself explicitly restricts its recursion to q ∈ ℕ+.

Original definition of the continuation optimum

Theorem A1 explicitly invokes (3). The following original pricing-policy rows specify its meaning, rather than adding a second pricing optimization to (A115). The class V consists of ℝ+ ∪ p∞-valued policies

p = {ps,0 ≤ s ≤ t}

on (Ω,F,P) satisfying

ps = p(λ;Qs,t − s),0 ≤ s ≤ t, p(λ;q,⋅) is measurable, q ∈ ℕ, p(λ;0,⋅) = p∞.

Their state and revenue definitions are

Qs = Q0 − N (λ∫ 0s(1 − F(p(λ;Q u,t − u)))du),0 ≤ s ≤ t, 		(2)

Jp(λ;q,t) = E [ λ∫ 0tp(λ;Q s,t − s)(1 − F(p(λ;Qs,t − s)))ds|Q0 = q], 		(1)

and

J⋆(λ;q,t) = sup ⁡ p∈V Jp(λ;q,t). 		(3)

The correspondence π = 1 − F(p) gives (A107)–(A109). Thus (1)–(3) and (A107)–(A114) are the defining chain for the continuation value in the selected variational model, not additional controls or constraints imposed alongside it.

Endpoint expressions. The source includes π = 0 in G and defines F−1 on [0,1], while permitting xU = ∞. Accordingly (A107), Proposition A8, and (A116) retain their printed factors πF−1(1 − π). The cited passages supply no explicit product convention at π = 0 when the upper endpoint is infinite; none is introduced here.
