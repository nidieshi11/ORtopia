Sets, fixed data, and decision roles

The definitions are from Section 3 and Table 1 (PDF pages 6–8) and Section 5 (page 13).

T = {0,…,u},T+ = {1,…,u,u + 1}, Okt′ ⊆O,O kt″ ⊆O, Nkt = Okt′∪O kt″ ∪P ∪D, Akt = {(i,j) : ∀ ⁡i,j ∈Nkt,i≠j},Gkt(Nkt,Akt),k ∈K,t ∈T.

K is a fixed heterogeneous fleet; O contains all origin and destination depots, and Okt′ and Okt″ are the origin and destination choices for vehicle k in period t. The set D contains sufficiently many replications of each supply point to allow repeated visits by a vehicle within a period. The distance matrix is asymmetric. πij is the length of arc (i,j) ∈Akt; τijkt is its vehicle- and period-specific travel time.

For k ∈K, φk is the per-period deployment cost, βk the unit-distance running cost, 𝜃k the unit-time running cost, and Ωk the capacity. For i ∈O ∪P ∪D, [λi,μi] is the start-of-service time window and δi the service duration. The following fixed-data conditions are stated:

δi = 0(i ∈O),Okt″ ∩O k(t+1)′≠∅.

A vehicle arriving before λi waits; service may not start after μi. Hkt is its maximum tour duration. If Hkt = 0, the vehicle is unavailable, and the depot sets must permit idling:

Hkt = 0⟹∃ ⁡o′∈O kt′,o″ ∈O kt″such that π o′o″ = 0.

The depot compatibility and idling conditions are stated for the vehicle-period depot sets in Section 3; no terminal-period convention is added here.

For i ∈P, ωi is inventory capacity. Ci contains allowable visit-period combinations, possibly with different frequencies. The fixed binary αrt is one if period t belongs to combination r ∈Ci, and zero otherwise. The separate fixed binary αikt is one if point i ∈P ∪D is accessible to vehicle k in period t, and zero otherwise; it also expresses continuity of service. Demand points are served at most once per period. The general notation introduces a minimum visit count νi, but the selected WCIRP block contains (27)–(28), not an additional minimum-count inequality.

The selected inventory policy is the discretized maximum-level policy. Li is the given set of allowable discrete inventory levels after regular delivery. The source does not prescribe a numerical discretization. The order-up-to policy is the special case Li = {ωi} for every i ∈P; this special case is not imposed here. Emergency deliveries always use the order-up-to policy.

The fixed monetary parameters χi and ζi are, respectively, the stock-out and emergency-delivery costs; for collection they price overflow and emergency collection. The route-failure cost multiplier satisfies

ψ ∈ [0,1].

CS is the average routing cost of going from the demand points of trip S to their nearest supply point and back. It remains the source-defined abstract cost; the paper gives no further algebraic formula for it.

The binary decision roles are: xijkt = 1 for traversal of arc (i,j) by vehicle k in period t; yikt = 1 for a visit to point i; zkt = 1 for use of vehicle k; cir = 1 for assignment of visit combination r to point i; and ℓirt = 1 for selection of discrete level r ∈Li at point i in period t. Each is zero otherwise.

The continuous variables are qikt, the expected delivery quantity to demand point i; Qikt, the expected cumulative quantity delivered by vehicle k arriving at point i; Iit, the expected inventory at the start of period t, before delivery; and Sikt, the start-of-service time. The variables b̲kt,b¯kt bound tour duration, while B̲,B¯ bound each vehicle’s total workload over the horizon. The selected objective does not price workload balancing, but (42)–(47) remain inherited. The known initial inventory Ii0 is fixed data, not a freely chosen initial state.

Supply points delimiting a trip can be visited in different periods. These sets are distinct from the start-of-service variable Sikt.

In a stock-out period, (15) charges χi when a regular visit occurs and χi + ζi otherwise. Initial stock-outs are charged even though the initial inventory is known. The scope T ∪T+ includes the first period after the horizon because its stock-out probability is determined by horizon decisions.

Cost-model qualification (Assumption 4, PDF pages 14–15). All objective terms except ESOEDC ignore the cost effect of demand points stocking out earlier than expected. A reaction policy means changes later in the horizon in response to recourse, ranging from doing nothing to re-optimization; it is not an additional decision block here. In the absence of inventory holding cost, Proposition 2 states that this objective overestimates real cost. In particular, emergency delivery can reduce a later trip’s delivery quantity and route-failure probability, an effect omitted by (8) and (16).

Demands are the only stochastic inputs; all other parameters are deterministic. They may be non-stationary. For i ∈P and t ∈T,

Here K is a covariance structure, not the fleet K. Definition 1 requires the forecasting model to provide 𝔼(ρit) for every t ∈T,i ∈P and the joint error distribution Φ. The latter may be theoretical or empirical but must be simulatable. No normality restriction is imposed in this formulation.

Initial state and timing (Definitions 2–3). For every i ∈P, Ii0 is observed and known with certainty and may be positive, zero, or negative. Consequently its initial stock-out probability is either zero or one. In each period t ∈T, deliveries occur before demand realizations. A regular delivery in period t means yikt = 1 for some k ∈K. The random state flag σit is one for stock-out and zero otherwise; it is not a routing decision. An emergency delivery occurs when

σit = 1andyikt = 0for all k ∈K,

and restores inventory to ωi. The limited-backorder policy requires delivery in the same period as a stock-out.

Let Λig denote inventory after a regular delivery to point i in period g. Its discrete choice is represented by ℓirg:

Λig ∈Li.

When no delivery to point i occurs during the planning horizon, the source specifies Λi0 = Ii0. In (7), period zero is also returned when no earlier regular delivery exists.

Assumption 2: restriction for route-failure calculation only. The errors used to calculate route-failure probabilities are independent and identically distributed, drawn from any marginal distribution Φ′. The paper stipulates

Brief notes on the retained printed scope

  • Equation (18) equates depot departures and arrivals but does not set either sum to one, despite the routing prose and Algorithm 1 presupposing a tour origin and destination. No missing equality is inserted.
  • Equations (21) and (39) quantify over all t ∈T, hence include t = u and refer to period u + 1. Equation (39) also prints o′∈Okt′, not Ok(t+1)′. These scopes are retained. Equation (15) likewise uses yik(u+1), whereas (46) gives visit-variable domains only on T. No terminal values are stipulated in the supplied formulation.
  • Definition 2 allows negative fixed Ii0, while (47) imposes Iit ≥ 0 for all t ∈T, including zero. Both statements are retained; no initial-inventory exception is inserted into (47).
  • The arc set excludes i = j, whereas several sums and (40), (46) range over all i,j ∈Nkt. The printed domains (46)–(47) also extend demand-point quantities or sets to all nodes. Equation (38) uses qjkt at depots, although its verbal definition is a demand-point delivery quantity. No diagonal-arc convention, depot-delivery value, or narrowed quantifier is added.
  • The prose defines stock-out as inventory becoming negative, but (3)–(6) and Appendix A use nonpositive empty-space inventory, including equality. Those printed probability boundaries are retained. Equation (2) and (B.1) index error-vector coordinates from one through |T| although T starts at zero; no reindexing rule is introduced.
  • Algorithm 1’s printed input restricts i,j to P, although its operations use depot and supply-point arcs. Its successor notation sums over the index it seeks to select, and it does not explicitly append the remaining trip to 𝔖k after the outer loop. These are retained rather than repaired.
  • The statement that (55) forces a disposal visit is stronger than its algebra in zero-load cases: the equality itself does not prohibit a zero-load predecessor outside D. The operative model retains (55), not an added disposal-predecessor arc constraint.
