\section*{P0007 Indivisible Freight Routing with Fixed Link Activation Costs}
\subsection*{Mathematical formulation}
\begin{align}
\min\quad &\sum_{k\in K}\sum_{p\in P(k)}d_k\Bigl(\sum_{(i,j)\in A(p)}c_{ij}\Bigr)x_p^k+\sum_{(i,j)\in A}f_{ij}y_{ij},\label{obj}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad &\sum_{p\in P(k)}x_p^k=1 &&k\in K,\tag{7}\\
&\sum_{k\in K}\sum_{p\in P(k):(i,j)\in A(p)}d_kx_p^k\le u_{ij}y_{ij} &&(i,j)\in A,\tag{8}\\
&y_{ij}\in\{0,1\} &&(i,j)\in A,\tag{9}\\
&\sum_{p\in P(k):(i,j)\in A(p)}x_p^k\le y_{ij} &&k\in K,(i,j)\in A,\tag{10}\\
&x_p^k\in\{0,1\} &&k\in K,p\in P(k).\tag{D}
\end{align}

\subsection*{Definitions and model scope}
A transport operator must move multiple commodities $K$ through a directed transport network $D$ while deciding which transport arcs to activate. The network comprises nodes $N$ and directed arcs $A$. Each commodity has a specified origin $s(k)$, destination $t(k)$, and strictly positive demand $d_k$. Its entire demand must travel together along one origin-to-destination path rather than being divided across paths. Different commodities may share transport arcs.

The operator knows each arc's transport cost per unit $c_{ij}$, fixed activation cost $f_{ij}$, and available capacity $u_{ij}$, all of which are nonnegative. Each commodity can choose from its complete collection of feasible origin-to-destination paths $P(k)$, and the arcs used by a path form its arc collection $A(p)$. With nonnegative costs, removing a cycle cannot increase combined transport and activation expense, so the complete range of simple paths can be considered. If only a restricted collection of paths $\bar P(k)$ is allowed, the resulting arrangement is compared only within that collection and cannot thereby be declared best over all feasible paths in the original network.

Exactly one path must be selected for each commodity. A path-selection status $x_p^k$ is one for a selected path and zero otherwise. An arc's activation status $y_{ij}$ likewise has only the two possibilities one and zero, meaning active and inactive. For each commodity and each arc, the number of that commodity's selected paths containing the arc cannot exceed the arc's activation status. Thus any arc used by a commodity must be active, although an active arc may remain unused.

The load on an arc is the sum of the full demands of all commodities whose selected paths contain that arc. This total must not exceed the arc's capacity multiplied by its activation status: an active arc offers its stated capacity, whereas an inactive arc carries nothing. Commodity demand cannot be reduced to overcome insufficient capacity. If any commodity has no available path, or all commodities cannot be transported together within these capacities, no arrangement satisfies the requirements.

The operator wants the sum of transport expense and fixed activation expense to be as low as possible. Each commodity's transport expense is its entire demand multiplied by the sum of unit transport costs on its selected path. The expenses for all commodities are added, together with one fixed charge for each active arc. That charge is not repeated when several commodities use the same arc, and an active but unused arc is still charged. The operator chooses commodity paths and arc activation states, jointly meeting the stated indivisibility, activation, and capacity requirements.
