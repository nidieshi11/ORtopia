\section*{High-level scenario-based chance-constrained TIO-MPC}
\begin{align}
\min_{\tilde t(k)}\quad &\mathrm E_{\tilde{\mathcal H}}\bigl[f_{\mathrm{opt}}(x(k),\tilde t(k),\tilde h)\bigr] \tag{17}\\
\text{subject to}\quad &\Pr_{\tilde{\mathcal H}}\bigl[\tilde g_{\mathrm{TIO}}(x(k),\tilde t(k),\tilde h)\leq0\bigr]\geq\eta, \tag{18}\\
&g_{\tilde t}(\tilde t(k))\leq0. \tag{19}
\end{align}
Here $\eta\in(0,1)$. The probability in (18) is a joint constraint-satisfaction probability, not a separate probability for each component. The decisions in (17) are continuous intervention time instants. The current state $x(k)$ is input data at this MPC step. The middle- and low-level objectives below are subsequent, separate optimizations, not additional terms in (17).

\subsection*{State, actions, uncertainties, and deterioration}
The infrastructure has $n$ components with independent condition-deterioration dynamics. For component $j$,
\[
x_j(k)=\begin{bmatrix}x_j^{\mathrm{con}}(k)\\x_j^{\mathrm{aux}}(k)\end{bmatrix}\in\mathcal X_j,
\qquad j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.
\]
The condition is $x_j^{\mathrm{con}}$; the vector $x_j^{\mathrm{aux}}$ contains the auxiliary state needed to describe the history-dependent inefficiency of corrective maintenance. The sets $\mathcal X_j$ and the functions below remain abstract in the generic model. A larger condition value means worse condition. Natural deterioration is monotonically increasing; the exponential trajectory in Fig.~3 is an illustration, not a prescribed generic dynamics function.
\[
\mathcal U=\{a_0,\ldots,a_N\},\qquad u_j(k)\in\mathcal U,
\qquad u(k)=[u_1(k)^{\mathrm T}\ \cdots\ u_n(k)^{\mathrm T}]^{\mathrm T}\in\mathcal U^n.
\]
The same action set is used for all components. There are $N$ interventions: $a_0$ is no maintenance and $a_N$ is full renewal.
\[
\theta_j(k)\in\Theta_j,\qquad
\theta(k)=[\theta_1^{\mathrm T}(k)\ \cdots\ \theta_n^{\mathrm T}(k)]^{\mathrm T}\in\Theta.
\]
These are uncertainty realizations, not decisions.
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
x_j(k+1)&=f_j(x_j(k),u_j(k),\theta_j(k))\\
&=\begin{cases}
f_j^0(x_j(k),\theta_j(k)),&u_j(k)=a_0\quad\text{(no maintenance)},\\
f_j^q(x_j(k),\theta_j(k)),&u_j(k)=a_q,\quad\forall q\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\},\\
f_j^N(\theta_j(k)),&u_j(k)=a_N\quad\text{(full renewal)},
\end{cases}\\[-2pt]
&\hspace{1cm}\forall j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.
\end{aligned}\tag{1}
\end{equation}
Natural degradation $f_j^0$ depends only on current condition and uncertainties. Renewal is stochastic but independent of current condition and maintenance history, restoring an ``as good as new'' condition. Other interventions depend on current condition and maintenance history.
\begin{align}
x(k+1)&=f(x(k),u(k),\theta(k)), \tag{2}\\
g(x(k),u(k),\theta(k))&\leq0, \tag{3}
\end{align}
where $f=[f_1^{\mathrm T}\ \cdots\ f_n^{\mathrm T}]^{\mathrm T}$. The abstract vector $g$ collects local constraints, including keeping each component's condition below its maintenance limit, and system-wide resource constraints, such as maintenance budget and working-time limits. No numerical limit or additional explicit form of $g$ is fixed for this generic formulation.

\subsection*{Prediction construction and time-instant decisions}
$N_{\mathrm P}$ and $N_{\mathrm C}$ are the prediction and control horizon lengths. The estimate $\hat x(k+l\mid k)$ is based on information available at time $k$.
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tilde x(k)&=[\hat x^{\mathrm T}(k+1\mid k)\ \cdots\ \hat x^{\mathrm T}(k+N_{\mathrm P}\mid k)]^{\mathrm T},\\
\tilde u(k)&=[u^{\mathrm T}(k)\ \cdots\ u^{\mathrm T}(k+N_{\mathrm P}-1)]^{\mathrm T},\\
\tilde\theta(k)&=[\theta^{\mathrm T}(k)\ \cdots\ \theta^{\mathrm T}(k+N_{\mathrm P}-1)]^{\mathrm T}.
\end{aligned}
\]
\begin{align}
\tilde x(k)&=\tilde f(x(k),\tilde u(k),\tilde\theta(k)), \tag{4}\\
\tilde g(x(k),\tilde u(k),\tilde\theta(k))&\leq0. \tag{5}
\end{align}
The function $\tilde f$ is obtained by recursive substitution of (2) over the prediction horizon; $\tilde g$ is obtained similarly from (3). Equations (3) and (5) describe the underlying constraint family, whose scenario-based satisfaction is imposed through (18), not as an additional robust constraint for every scenario.

The fixed quantity $\nu_{j,q}$ is the maximum number of occurrences of intervention $q$ on component $j$ within the prediction period. The continuous vector $t_{j,q,k}$ has length $\nu_{j,q}$ and contains relative intervention time instants.
\begin{equation}
\tilde t(k)=\left[
\underbrace{t_{1,1,k}^{\mathrm T}\ \cdots\ t_{1,N,k}^{\mathrm T}}_{t_1^{\mathrm T}(k)}\ \cdots\
\underbrace{t_{n,1,k}^{\mathrm T}\ \cdots\ t_{n,N,k}^{\mathrm T}}_{t_n^{\mathrm T}(k)}
\right]^{\mathrm T}.\tag{6}
\end{equation}
The TIO conversion rounds time instants to the nearest discrete time steps to obtain $\tilde u(k)$. Instants outside the control period $N_{\mathrm C}T_s$ are neglected. Here $T_s$ is the sampling time. For $N_{\mathrm C}<N_{\mathrm P}$,
\[
u_j(k+l)=a_0\quad
\forall l\in\{N_{\mathrm C},\ldots,N_{\mathrm P}-1\},\quad
\forall j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.
\]
In the preceding display the action symbol is $u_j$, i.e. explicitly:
\[
u_j(k+l)\equiv u_j(k+l).
\]
The complete deterministic timing block represented by (19) is
\begin{align}
(t_{j,q,k})_1&\geq t_{j,q}^{\min}, \tag{7}\\
(t_{j,q,k})_{\nu_{j,q}}&\leq t_{j,q}^{\max}, \tag{8}\\
(t_{j,q,k})_{i+1}-(t_{j,q,k})_i&\geq\Delta t_{j,q}^{\min},
\quad\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,\nu_{j,q}-1\}, \tag{9}\\
t_{j,q}^{\max}&=N_{\mathrm C}T_s+\nu_{j,q}\Delta t_{j,q}^{\min}. \tag{10}
\end{align}
Every row applies for all $j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$ and $q\in\{1,\ldots,N\}$. The lower bound $t_{j,q}^{\min}$ accommodates advance planning, and $\Delta t_{j,q}^{\min}$ is the minimum interval between consecutive interventions of the same type. Equation (10) is the fixed upper-bound definition allowing no intervention within the control period; it is not replaced by a bound at the control-horizon endpoint.

\subsection*{Scenarios and objective definitions}
\[
\tilde\Theta=\Theta^{N_{\mathrm P}},\qquad
\tilde{\mathcal H}\subset\tilde\Theta.
\]
The set $\tilde{\mathcal H}$ contains the selected representative uncertainty scenarios over the whole prediction period. For any $\tilde h\in\tilde{\mathcal H}$, the TIO prediction and its constraint expression are
\begin{align}
\tilde x(k)&=\tilde f_{\mathrm{TIO}}(x(k),\tilde t(k),\tilde h), \tag{11}\\
\tilde g_{\mathrm{TIO}}(x(k),\tilde t(k),\tilde h)&\leq0. \tag{12}
\end{align}
The inequality (12) defines the event in (18); it is not independently imposed for every representative scenario.
\begin{align}
J(k)&=J_{\mathrm{Deg}}(k)+\lambda\mu J_{\mathrm{Maint}}(k), \tag{13}\\
J_{\mathrm{Deg}}(k)&=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{N_{\mathrm P}}
\left|x_j^{\mathrm{con}}(k+l)-\hat x_j^{\mathrm{con}}\right|_q, \tag{14}\\
J_{\mathrm{Maint}}(k)&=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{N_{\mathrm P}}\sum_{q=1}^{p}
\gamma_{j,q}I_{u_j(k+l-1)=a_q}, \tag{15}\\
J(k)&=f_{\mathrm{opt}}(x(k),\tilde t(k),\tilde h). \tag{16}
\end{align}
Select the source-supported $1$-norm version of (14), namely $q=1$ in that row. The $q$ in (15) is instead an intervention summation index. The factor $\mu$ scales the maintenance cost and $\lambda$ expresses the condition--cost trade-off. The coefficient $\gamma_{j,q}$ is the cost of intervention $a_q$ on component $j$.
\[
I_X=\begin{cases}1,&X\text{ is true},\\0,&X\text{ is false}.\end{cases}
\]
The symbol $\hat x_j^{\mathrm{con}}$ and the upper limit $p$ are retained as printed in (14)--(15). The supplied generic passages do not specify their values or identify $p$ with $N$; no such identification is introduced here. In particular, the time-independent hatted quantity in (14) is not replaced by $\hat x_j^{\mathrm{con}}(k+l\mid k)$.
\begin{equation}
\mathrm E_{\tilde{\mathcal H}}\bigl[f_{\mathrm{opt}}(x(k),\tilde t(k),\tilde h)\bigr]
=\sum_{\tilde h\in\tilde{\mathcal H}}p(\tilde h)
 f_{\mathrm{opt}}(x(k),\tilde t(k),\tilde h).\tag{20}
\end{equation}
Here $p(\tilde h)$ is the probability of scenario $\tilde h$, distinct from the upper summation limit $p$ in (15). The equivalent finite-scenario expression of (18) is
\begin{equation}
\sum_{\tilde h\in\tilde{\mathcal H}}p(\tilde h)
 I_{\tilde g_{\mathrm{TIO}}(x(k),\tilde t(k),\tilde h)\leq0}\geq\eta.\tag{21}
\end{equation}


\section*{Middle-level time-slot allocation}
At the current planning step there are at most \(N_{\rm sl}\) available slots.
The intervals \([\tau_j,\tau_{j+1}]\), \(j=1,\ldots,N_\tau\), have disruption rates \(c_j\).
Let \(a_i=t_i^{\rm start},\,b_i=t_i^{\rm end}\). Each active slot has setup time
\(T_{\rm set}\) and cost \(c_{\rm sl}\). The required productive time is \(\widehat T_{\rm Maint}\).
\begin{align}
\min_{a,b}\quad&
 \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm sl}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_\tau}c_j
 [\max(\min(\tau_{j+1},b_i),\tau_j)-\min(\max(\tau_j,a_i),\tau_{j+1})]
 +\lambda_{\rm sl}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm sl}}c_{\rm sl}{\bf1}_{b_i\le\tau_{N_\tau+1}}\tag{22}\\
a_1&\ge\tau_1\tag{23}\\
b_{N_{\rm sl}}&\le\tau_{\max}\tag{24}\\
b_i-a_i&\ge\Delta\tau_{\min} &&(1\le i\le N_{\rm sl})\tag{25}\\
b_i+\epsilon&\le a_{i+1} &&(1\le i\le N_\tau-1)\tag{26}\\
\tau_{\max}&=\tau_{N_\tau+1}+2N_{\rm sl}\Delta\tau_{\min}\tag{27}\\
a_i\le\tau_{N_\tau+1}&\implies b_i\le\tau_{N_\tau+1} &&(1\le i\le N_{\rm sl})\tag{28}\\
\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm sl}}{\bf1}_{b_i\le\tau_{N_\tau+1}}
 (b_i-a_i-T_{\rm set})&\ge\widehat T_{\rm Maint}.\tag{29}
\end{align}
The printed index in (26) is retained. The instances use \(N_\tau=N_{\rm sl}=2\).
\section*{Low-level clustering}
At each active slot \(s\) of duration \(T_s^{\rm sl}\), consider only units in components
selected for the intervention. Initially \(\mathcal I_1=\{1,\ldots,N^{\rm bu}\}\);
subsequently \(\mathcal I_s\) contains the indices still untreated. Unit \(j\) has
location \(\xi_j\) and severity \(w_j\). Set
\(l_i=\phi_{i,s}^{\rm start},\,r_i=\phi_{i,s}^{\rm end}\).
\begin{align}
\max_{l,r}\quad&
\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm cl}}\sum_{j\in\mathcal I_s}w_j{\bf1}_{l_i\le\xi_j\le r_i}
 +\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm cl}}{\bf1}_{r_i>\bar\xi}\tag{30}\\
l_1&\ge\underline\xi\tag{31}\\
r_{N_{\rm cl}}&\le\xi_{\max}\tag{32}\\
\Delta\phi_{\min}\le r_i-l_i&\le\Delta\phi_{\max} &&(1\le i\le N_{\rm cl})\tag{33}\\
l_{i+1}-r_i&\ge\epsilon &&(1\le i<N_{\rm cl})\tag{34}\\
\xi_{\max}&=\bar\xi+2N_{\rm cl}(\Delta\phi_{\min}+\epsilon)\tag{35}\\
l_i\le\bar\xi&\iff r_i\le\bar\xi &&(1\le i\le N_{\rm cl})\tag{36}\\
\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm cl}}{\bf1}_{r_i\le\bar\xi}
 \left((r_i-l_i)/v_{\rm on}+T_{\rm on}+T_{\rm off}\right)
 +\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm cl}-1}{\bf1}_{r_i\le\bar\xi}
 (l_{i+1}-r_i)/v_{\rm off}+T_{\rm set}&\le T_s^{\rm sl}.\tag{37}
\end{align}
The drive sum in (37) includes the move to the following cluster when the current one is active,
including the printed move from an active cluster to its next inactive successor.
\section*{Sequential coordination, execution, and feedback}
The three objectives are linked by the procedure in Section~4, Fig.~4, and Algorithm~1. At the high level, the input consists of basic-unit positions $\xi$ and initial conditions $w(0)$. The algorithm initializes
\[
k\leftarrow1,\qquad w(k)\leftarrow w(0),\qquad
x(k)\leftarrow\operatorname{Aggregate}(w(k),\xi).
\]
For $k\leq k_{\mathrm{end}}$, the high-level solve and conversion produce
\[
u(k)\leftarrow\operatorname{MPC\_Optimize}(x(k)).
\]
Here the left side is the action vector $u(k)$. Only the current-step intervention decision is implemented, following the receding-horizon principle. For each intervention $a_l$, if any $u_j(k)=a_l$, the subsequent calls are
\[
\begin{aligned}
t_{\mathrm{sl}}(k)&\leftarrow\operatorname{MiddleLevel}(u(k),a_l,\widehat T_{\mathrm{Maint}}),\\
\phi(k)&\leftarrow\operatorname{LowLevel}(w(k),\xi,t_{\mathrm{sl}}(k),a_l).
\end{aligned}
\]
The first call solves the selected middle-level problem; the second solves the selected clustering problem sequentially for the active slots using the sets $\mathcal I_s$ above. The generic input $\widehat T_{\mathrm{Maint}}$ is the estimated time to perform the suggested intervention. The particular grinding estimator (47) and numerical case-study settings are not imposed on this generic formulation.

Let $\mathcal I_{\mathrm{rem}}$ be the set of indices of basic units not covered by any cluster in any active slot. The feedback quantities are
\begin{align}
v_{\mathrm{rem}}&=
\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal I_{\mathrm{rem}}}w_j}
{\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N^{\mathrm{bu}}}w_j}, \tag{38}\\
\Delta\widehat T&=
\frac{|\mathcal I_{\mathrm{rem}}|}{N^{\mathrm{bu}}}
\cdot\frac{\bar\xi-\underline\xi}{v_{\mathrm{on}}}. \tag{39}
\end{align}
The first is the remaining fraction of accumulated severity; the second estimates the additional maintenance time. A factor $\mu$ converts additional time into a cost. While basic units remain outside the returned clusters, the rule is
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
v_{\mathrm{rem}}-\mu\Delta\widehat T>0:
&\widehat T_{\mathrm{Maint}}\leftarrow\widehat T_{\mathrm{Maint}}+\Delta\widehat T,
\quad\text{repeat the middle- and low-level calls},\\[2pt]
v_{\mathrm{rem}}-\mu\Delta\widehat T\leq0:
&\text{terminate the iteration and leave those units uncovered}.
\end{array}
\]
Algorithm~1 writes $\nu_{\mathrm{rem}}$ in its ``Compute'' instruction but $v_{\mathrm{rem}}$ in its test; (38) and the Section~4 prose use $v_{\mathrm{rem}}$. The printed symbol change is noted without adding a second feedback definition. No zero-denominator convention is supplied for (38)--(39).

After the iteration the algorithm applies
\[
\operatorname{Intervention}(\phi(k),a_l).
\]
Only basic units inside the returned clusters are treated. If no intervention is suggested, the procedure proceeds to the condition update. New measurements are used when available; otherwise the algorithm uses the abstract simulation operation:
\[
\begin{aligned}
w(k+1)&\leftarrow\text{new measurements},&&\text{if available},\\
w(k+1)&\leftarrow\operatorname{Simulate}(w(k),\xi,\phi(k)),&&\text{otherwise},\\
x(k+1)&\leftarrow\operatorname{Aggregate}(w(k+1),\xi),\\
k&\leftarrow k+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
The operations Aggregate, Simulate, and Intervention remain abstract for the generic infrastructure; no case-study aggregation or simulation law is imported. The repeated symbols $\lambda$ and $\mu$ are retained in their printed high-level, low-level, and feedback roles rather than assigned new numerical relationships.
